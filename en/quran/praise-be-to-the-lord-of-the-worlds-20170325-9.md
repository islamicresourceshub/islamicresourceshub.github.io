> "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds" (Quran 1:2)

## Expressing Gratitude to Others

One of the most prominent etiquettes taught to human beings is to express gratitude to others for the favors they have done. We often highlight the indicators of gratitude and appreciation found across the diverse created worlds of the universe to emphasize this.

Should not man, who is more enriched by divine blessings than any other creature in the universe, offer his gratitude and indebtedness before God?

## The Quran Begins by Establishing This Fact

It is by establishing this fact that the **Quran** begins its first chapter. Man is one who must constantly utter words of praise before Allah, the Creator, Controller, and Sustainer of the world. Allah declares that such a person is His best servant. When the Quran demands that praises be offered to Allah, it does not stop without logically explaining the reasons and justifications for it. The Quran repeatedly highlights the breadth and vastness of the blessings Allah has bestowed upon humanity.

- Creating this universe from nothingness without any prior model,
- Sustaining the sky like a canopy without visible pillars,
- Making water and air, the foundations for the survival of all creatures including humans, readily available here,
- Carrying out the succession of generations through the process of reproduction,
- The abundance of agricultural produce,
- The modern inventions that man is making for his survival using his unique intellect,
- Enabling survival in this world by placing love and mercy within the great truth that is the heart of every created being...

These are but a few divine signs that come to our minds within a brief moment of reflection. The Quran awakens us to bring these into our thoughts and contemplation, and to become grateful through our deeds, words, and lives before the Great Power that has worked and continues to work behind them.

## 'Al-Hamd' - The Most Noble Word of Gratitude

We can understand that true believers, from their respective stations, will prepare themselves to know Allah and show gratitude to Him in the ways that are possible and prescribed for them. The most noble word of gratitude is the term '**Al-Hamd**' mentioned in this verse. The phrase that a believer must constantly use to express gratitude to Allah is '**Alhamdulillah**'.

> Ibn Abbas (RA) says: 'The phrase Alhamdulillah is the word of the grateful' (Tabari).

> Ibn Abbas (RA) says: "Once Umar al-Faruq (RA) said: 'We have deeply understood the dhikrs like Subhanallah and La ilaha illallah. But what is the significance of Alhamdulillah?' Then Ali (RA) said: 'That phrase is one that Allah loves dearly and has chosen for Himself.'"

> Ibn Abi Hatim reports: Ibn Abbas (RA) says: 'Alhamdulillah is a word of gratitude. When a servant of Allah says Alhamdulillah, Allah responds: My servant has thanked Me.'

## What the Prophet (PBUH) Said

> The Prophet (PBUH) said: "When you say 'Alhamdulillah Rabbil Alamin', you have praised Allah. Then He will increase His blessings upon you" (Tabari).

> Narrated by Jabir (RA): The Messenger (PBUH) said: "The most excellent dhikr is La ilaha illallah. The most excellent supplication is Alhamdulillah" (Tirmidhi).

May Allah, the Boundlessly Merciful, the Owner of unimaginable compassion, and the Forgiver of sins, include us among those servants who show gratitude to Him until the very end of their lives.
