Reclaiming a Strong Nation

പത്രാധിപർ · 2018-04-28 · Islamic History

The Land of the Indus Valley Civilization

India is the land of the Indus Valley Civilization. Historically, many significant trade routes passed through this region. Throughout its history, the Indian subcontinent has been renowned for its commercial and cultural wealth. Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism were born here. Furthermore, the arrival of Zoroastrianism, Judaism, Christianity, and Islam added depth to the country's cultural diversity.

From Portuguese to British Colonialism

From the sixteenth century onwards, the country faced Portuguese, Dutch, French, and British occupations. Although the Europeans initially aimed for trade relations with India, they exploited the infighting among local princely states to establish colonies across the country. From the beginning of the eighteenth century, the British East India Company gradually took over India as a British colony. The 1857 uprising against the English East India Company was the primary attempt at resistance by Indians against European occupation. However, this rebellion, known as the First War of Indian Independence, was suppressed by the British military. Most of India came under the British Empire. At the beginning of the twentieth century, the Indian independence movement, based on non-violence and led by Mahatma Gandhi, gained momentum. After years of patient struggle, India became independent from British rule on August 15, 1947. Yet, the history of independent India began with the sight of a part of the country being partitioned into a separate nation called Pakistan.

The History of Independent India

In 1950, India became an independent, sovereign republic. Governments elected by the people have governed India in succession. India stood tall before the world as a nation founded on democracy and secularism, upholding the beautiful motto of unity in diversity. However, since 1992, when the domes of the Babri Masjid were demolished by extremist traitors and the subsequent massacres took place, India's reputation before the world began to decline. The reality is that since then, our country has not only failed to return to its former glory, but its image continues to fade day by day.

The Strength of a Nation

If the strength of a nation were measured solely by the number of soldiers in its army, navy, and air force, or the vastness of its arsenal, our nation would not be doing too poorly. However, a nation is made truly strong by foresight in the economic sector, impartiality in the judiciary, and all-around development that benefits the people. There are soldiers to stop those who cross the borders. But the border security forces cannot prevent the leaking of the country's top-secret information to other nations. The military cannot stop the personal information contained in the public's Aadhaar cards from being handed over to those who misuse it. We realize with shock that all of this is happening today. It is the judges of the Supreme Court who are calling out that the government is unfairly interfering in the judicial system, which is a path to destruction. The fact that the number of people who commit multi-crore scams and leave the country without any hindrance is increasing does not seem to bother the rulers at all. Planned communal riots and the hunting of minorities and Dalits continue unabated. If public life is this miserable and insecure, how can we take pride in calling this a strong nation?
