Islamophobia: Archetypes of Ancient History

മുഹമ്മദ് അജ്മല്‍ സി · 2017-02-25 · Islamic History

The present is often a repetition of history. That is why the saying that there are lessons to be learned from history remains eternally relevant. For the Muslim community, this matter holds great importance, because they are those who should strive to embody the life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, who lived fourteen centuries ago, and learn from his responses during times of crisis.

"Indeed, in the Messenger of Allah you have an excellent example..." (Quran 33:21).

Critical Junctures of History

At critical junctures throughout history, the Muslim community has passed through numerous periods of crisis. Therefore, the challenges this community faces in the modern era, whatever they may be, can also be observed in past times. The methods adopted by the Prophet and his companions to overcome them are also very clear! The infiltration of extremist ideologies, debates based on scripture versus mere logic, and communities expelled from their homelands are not problems unique to today. Last month, as humanitarians of all nations and faiths raise their voices and unleash protests against the order signed by American President Donald Trump, banning people from seven Muslim-majority countries and refugees, a look back at history becomes timely.

The Boycott in Makkah

The wheel of time must be turned back centuries. A meeting of the polytheists of Makkah is taking place in Wadi Muhassab. They are gripped by an unprecedented fear! Umar (RA) and Hamza (RA) have embraced Islam. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ made it unequivocally clear that he would not retreat from the path of preaching monotheism. What frightened them even more was the decision taken by believers and non-believers from the Banu Hashim and Banu Muttalib families to protect the Prophet ﷺ. From the Prophet's family, only Abu Lahab did not agree to that pact. Matters had reached a point where a direct attack against the Prophet ﷺ was impossible!

In today's language, 'Islamophobia' had become very strong in Makkah. They decided that no relationship whatsoever – be it social or economic – should be maintained with Muhammad and those who supported him. Do not trade with them, do not speak to them... a complete boycott! The polytheists of Makkah decided to continue this boycott 'until Muhammad was handed over to them to be killed.' The Prophet ﷺ and his companions, along with the Banu Hashim and Banu Muttalib families who supported them, were forced to retreat into the valley of Shi'b Abi Talib in the eastern outskirts of Makkah. Social ties were completely severed.

The cries of hungry infants echoed through the valley. The only source of sustenance was the food supplies provided by some Makkans whose springs of compassion had not yet dried up. Even if they wanted to go outside Makkah to buy food, they lacked the financial means.

Voices Against Injustice

A very difficult situation. Imagine a situation where people were satisfying their hunger by eating animal hides and leaves! This dire situation continued for three years. However, eventually, a group from among the disbelievers of Makkah themselves rose up against this injustice. Many who had blood ties with the Banu Hashim family could not accept the persecution their relatives were enduring.

  • Hisham ibn Amr
  • Zuhayr ibn Abi Umayya
  • Mut'im ibn Adiyy
  • Abul Bukhtari
  • Zam'ah bin Al-Aswad

These five individuals publicly set out to tear down the boycott agreement that had been hung inside the Kaaba. Abu Jahl tried to stop them, but failed!

Meanwhile, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ informed his uncle Abu Talib of the divine revelation he had received: that all parts of the agreement, except for the phrase 'In the name of Allah', had been eaten by termites. When Abu Talib told this to those who had come to break the agreement and to Abu Jahl, the polytheists had to concede that if this was true, they would be willing to withdraw the boycott. The sight that Mut'im ibn Adiyy saw when he went to inspect the parchment on which the agreement was written was astonishing!

All the phrases of injustice and persecution, except for the part 'Bismikallahumma', had been destroyed by termites. With this, the disbelievers were compelled to withdraw the three-year-long boycott.

Lessons from History

As history repeats itself, the Muslim community must learn lessons from the Prophet's history. If Abu Talib and his family, who were not Muslims, and those five young men fought against the boycott of Muslims, then today, the reaction of people of other faiths in America and elsewhere against this injustice is indeed a repetition of history. The protests rising around us energize the Muslim community to uphold justice with people of other faiths, to do good to them, and to act with goodwill, as Allah and the Prophet commanded. It also serves as a reminder that if injustice or aggression occurs against other communities tomorrow, no matter who commits it, we are committed to standing with truth and justice!

History will continue to repeat itself. When we reflect on the boycott experienced by the Prophet and his followers in the vast desert of Makkah, all of this seems so small!

"Or do you think that you will enter Paradise while such [trial] has not yet come to you as came to those who passed on before you? They were touched by poverty and hardship and were shaken until [even their] messenger and those who believed with him said, 'When is the help of Allah?' Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near." (2:214).
