# Islamic History: An Overview

**Muhammad Sadiq Madani**

*The Al-Aqsa Mosque premises*

Yesterday serves as a lesson and a guide for today. Today is the history of tomorrow. The social and cultural records of our predecessors provide us with much to reflect upon. Through the study of history, we can emulate their virtues, distance ourselves from the causes of their downfall, and strive to reach heights even greater than theirs.

Studying history is a subject of immense importance. There is no doubt that historical accounts mixed with falsehoods can lead the new generation astray. The American historian C.L. Rowse, in his 1962 book *The Use of History*, stated: "If America intends to rule the nations of the world at any time, I say to the rulers of America: you must teach history with great importance in American schools, colleges, and universities, select the most brilliant students for it, and choose only those who have mastered history for high-level positions in political, military, and social institutions."

Fascist extremist organizations follow this principle by distorting history to implement their hidden agendas. We must realize that many things celebrated today as historical facts are actually unreal. There have been historians who inserted lies into historical truths for religious or political goals. Some have written histories by mixing truth with falsehood, failing to provide genuine guidance. Others have not hesitated to add exaggerations to capture attention.

Although the Quran is not a history book, it informs us of many pages of history starting from Adam (peace be upon him). The creation of man, the flood in the time of Nuh (peace be upon him), the destruction of the communities of Ad, Thamud, Iram, and the People of the Elephant, the various trials in the lives of the prophets, the People of the Cave, Dhul-Qarnayn, the Children of Israel, and certain events in the life of Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him)... these are just a few examples.

'Hadith' is the written record of the words, actions, and approvals of the Prophet during his twenty-three-year mission. Prophetic history is not merely history; it is also a source of religious legal instructions. Just as one must not attribute to the Prophet (peace be upon him) hadiths that were not narrated from him, one must not add false matters to the Prophetic history. Therefore, historical books written by hadith scholars are more authentic and reliable than those written by individuals unconnected to the field of hadith. Books like Imam Bukhari's *At-Tarikh al-Kabir*, Imam Dhahabi's *Tarikh al-Islam* and *Siyar A'lam an-Nubala*, and Suyuti's *Tarikh al-Khulafa* are superior to historical works like Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani's *Al-Aghani* or Yaqut al-Hamawi's *Mu'jam al-Udaba*.

Many historical works written in the latter half of the last century are toxic. This is because many historians of this period were orientalists hostile to Islam or those with socialist leanings. They moved their pens according to their own ideologies.

One who studies Islamic history can understand it in three main stages:

One: The history of some prophets who lived before Muhammad (peace be upon him) and their communities. The reason for including the history of past communities in Islamic history is that the Quran and Hadith contain accounts regarding past prophets and their nations. This includes prophets like Nuh (peace be upon him), Ibrahim (peace be upon him), and Musa (peace be upon him), as well as the downfall of those who defied them.

Two: The history of Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).

Three: The general history of the four Caliphs and the administrations that followed, such as the Umayyads and Abbasids.

## Isra'iliyyat

'Isra'iliyyat' refers to reports regarding their religion and war histories that were understood by the desert-dwelling Jews and Christians living in various parts of the Arabian Peninsula before the advent of the Prophet, which they reported after becoming Muslims. Such historical events found in Quranic exegesis and history books are recorded in three ways:

One: Those who narrated Isra'iliyyat with an *isnad* (chain of narration). By recording them with chains of narration, they felt they had fulfilled their responsibility. Ibn Jarir at-Tabari is among those who narrated Isra'iliyyat with chains of narration.

Two: Those who wrote everything they received without recording the chains of narration. Because of this, it is impossible to distinguish the truth from falsehood in the historical fragments found in their books.

Three: Scholars like Shawkani, Rashid Rida, and Alusi, who did not accept Isra'iliyyat for Quranic exegesis and strongly opposed them.

**Approach to Isra'iliyyat**

One: Acceptable.
Those that align with the Quran and the Prophetic traditions fall into the category of acceptable Isra'iliyyat.

Two: Unacceptable.
Those that contradict Islamic principles are unacceptable and cannot be used as evidence.

Three: To remain silent.
If the matters in Isra'iliyyat are neither supported nor contradicted by Islamic sources, one should neither accept nor reject them; rather, one should remain silent about them.

It is narrated from Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Jews used to read the Torah in Hebrew and the Muslims would interpret it in Arabic. Then the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Do not believe the People of the Book, nor disbelieve them. Say: 'We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us and what has been revealed to you'" (Bukhari).

And you may narrate from the Children of Israel, for there is no harm in it. But whoever tells a lie against me intentionally, let him take his seat in the Fire" (Bukhari).

## Historical Books

The main historical books available to us today include Ibn Hisham's *Sirat Rasul*, Ibn Jarir at-Tabari's *Akhbar al-Muluk*, Ibn Kathir's *Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah*, and Imam Dhahabi's *Tarikh al-Islam*. These works are based on the reports of our predecessors.

During the reign of Mu'awiyah (may Allah be pleased with him), he desired to record Islamic history and brought Ubayd ibn Sharyah al-Jurhumi from Yemen to record it, who then wrote the book *Kitab al-Muluk wa Akhbar al-Madin*. Aban ibn Uthman ibn Affan, who died in 105 AH, recorded some aspects of the history of the Messenger (peace be upon him). In it, he compiled hadiths regarding the life of the Prophet. Wahb ibn Munabbih (died 110 AH), Islam ibn Umar ibn Wathad (120 AH), Shurahbil ibn Sa'd (123 AH), Ibn Shihab az-Zuhri (124 AH), and Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr ibn Hazm (135 AH) all made significant contributions to Islamic historical writing.

Islamic military history was significantly recorded by Musa ibn Uqbah (died 141 AH), Ma'mar ar-Rashid (150 AH), and Muhammad ibn Ishaq (151 AH). Al-Waqidi, who died in 207 AH, is an ancient and famous figure in Islamic historical writing. All reports in Ibn Hisham's *Sirah* are through Ibn Ishaq.

## Tabari's History Book

The history book by the famous Quranic exegete and historian Ibn Jarir at-Tabari, who died in 310 AH, is titled *Tarikh al-Akhbar wal-Muluk*. In it, he covers history from the beginning of creation until 302 AH. In the introduction to this book, he says: "The reader of this book should know that the basis for everything I have mentioned here is the reports I have narrated. I attribute them to those who reported them to me. Some of the news here comes from those who lived before. The reader might perhaps dislike these events and express displeasure upon hearing them, because they are baseless and untrue. If so, let them know that I did not invent them. Those who reported to me narrated them. I have only recorded them as I received them."
