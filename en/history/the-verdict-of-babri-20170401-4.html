The Verdict on Babri

Twenty-five years have passed since the Babri Masjid, a symbol of Indian secularism, was demolished by the 'kar seva' of a group of communal fanatics. As the Supreme Court prepares to resume hearings on April 6 regarding the conspiracy case against the masterminds of the demolition—L. K. Advani, Murli Manohar Joshi, Kalyan Singh, and Uma Bharti—we take a look at the investigation.

Besides these individuals, 13 other BJP leaders were also accused in the case. The Lucknow High Court had upheld the Rae Bareli trial court's decision to remove them from the list of the accused. The court's current observations come while considering the appeal filed by the CBI in 2011 and a petition filed by social activist Haji Mahboob Ahmad in 2015 challenging this.

There were two cases related to the demolition of the Babri Masjid. One case (197/1992) was directly related to the demolition of the mosque. The accused in this case were two lakh unnamed kar sevaks. This was a case that carried a maximum prison sentence of ten years. This case was initially heard in the Lucknow court. The second case (198/1992) was related to the conspiracy to demolish the mosque. This case was against Advani, Kalyan Singh, Joshi, Uma Bharti, Vinay Katiyar, and Ashok Singhal, who were stationed near the site and participated in the conspiracy while the kar sevaks demolished the mosque. The most significant charge in this case, which carries a prison sentence of up to five years, was the attempt to incite enmity between different religious groups in a way that would undermine the integrity of the nation. This case was being considered in the Rae Bareli court. On October 5, 1993, the CBI took over both cases and filed them as a single charge sheet. The CBI informed the court that the individuals mentioned in the charge sheet had held a secret meeting at Vinay Katiyar's house and decided to demolish the mosque. However, the Lucknow bench of the Allahabad High Court kept delaying the case without valid reason. In February 2001, the court slowed down the proceedings by pointing out a technical error, arguing that the Allahabad High Court had not been consulted when the CBI took over the conspiracy case from the Rae Bareli court. At the same time, the court did not make any statements that would protect the accused. However, neither the CBI nor the successive state governments took the necessary steps to overcome the court's observation regarding the technical error. As a result, case number 1980, related to the conspiracy, returned to the Rae Bareli court.

Only case number 1970 was subsequently considered in the Lucknow court. This was a case that existed only against those who participated in the demolition of the mosque (the kar sevaks). It did not include the case against the BJP leaders. Citing this logic and pointing out that the case would not stand, Special Judge S. K. Shukla of the Lucknow court withdrew the case against Advani and others on May 24, 2001. Although the CBI filed a revision petition against this, the Allahabad High Court upheld Shukla's verdict on May 20, 2010, and dismissed the CBI's petition. Following this, the CBI made a strong move in the Supreme Court on February 11, 2011. The CBI argued in the Supreme Court that S. K. Shukla had made an 'artificial distinction' in the cases against those who demolished the Babri Masjid. They argued that Shukla had tried to protect them from the conspiracy charge by claiming that the BJP leaders were not directly involved in the demolition of the mosque. They also informed the Supreme Court that a situation should be created to carry both cases together, as was done previously. 'Since the incitement to crime, the execution of the crime, the demolition of the mosque, and the constant attacks on journalists are all interrelated, there is no obstacle to filing a joint case under Section 120B of the Indian Penal Code. The cases should be considered together because the crimes occurred in succession through numerous violations of the law. Evidence cannot be taken to two different courts,' the CBI stated before the court.

It was on March 6 that the Supreme Court made remarks on the CBI's appeal. The Supreme Court stated that the trial court's action of canceling the conspiracy case on technical grounds would not stand. The bench, comprising Justices R. F. Nariman and P. C. Ghose, has directed the CBI to file a fresh charge sheet against all the accused. Although the hearing was moved to March 22, it was postponed to April 6 due to Justice Nariman's absence.

Although the BJP leaders' lawyer, K. K. Venugopal, argued strongly against bringing in a new charge sheet, Justice Nariman did not accept it. 'Prima facie, the accusation stands; we cannot accept the acquittal of the accused. On what grounds were they acquitted? Technical reasons are never a justification for acquittal,' Justice Nariman said. Additional Solicitor General Neeraj Kishan Kaul represented the CBI.

Currently, Uma Bharti is a Union Minister and Kalyan Singh is the Governor of Rajasthan. Murli Manohar Joshi is preparing to become the President after Pranab Mukherjee. Joshi is the first choice on the list of presidential candidates prepared by the BJP. If the verdict goes against him, Joshi's ambition to become President will not be realized. Uma Bharti and Kalyan Singh would also have to resign from their positions.

It was while these arguments were ongoing that BJP leader Subramanian Swamy approached the Supreme Court requesting permission for the construction of the Ram Temple. Supreme Court Justice J. S. Khehar suggested that the issue could be resolved through mediation outside the court. 'Do not see it as a court order, but as a suggestion. Since the matter is sensitive and involves mediation, we can sit together with all parties and try for a possible solution,' Justice Khehar said.

Secular organizations generally rejected Khehar's opinion. Leaders including Babri Masjid Action Committee convener Dr. S. Q. R. Ilyas, Muslim League All India Secretary E. T. Mohammed Basheer MP, Muslim Personal Law Board member Maulana Khalid Rashid, and the Sunni Waqf Board rejected the court's suggestion. The leaders said that the court should deliver a verdict based on the evidence before it regarding the ownership of the land. CPI(M) General Secretary Sitaram Yechury also said that a court verdict is needed in a legal dispute.

The Supreme Court came forward with a mediation proposal after the Allahabad High Court delivered a significant verdict on September 30, 2010, regarding the rights to the land where the Babri Masjid was located. The Allahabad court had ruled that the disputed site should be divided into three and given equally to the Hindu Mahasabha, the Sunni Waqf Board, and the Nirmohi Akhara of the Vaishnavas. However, this verdict is not acceptable to the Hindu Mahasabha or the Sunni Waqf Board. They approached the Supreme Court. On May 9, 2011, the Supreme Court stayed the Allahabad court's verdict and ordered the status quo to be maintained. The second bench of the Supreme Court observed that the Allahabad court's verdict was surprising, as none of the parties had made such a request. With the BJP coming to power in UP, the situation for the construction of the Ram Temple has become favorable, which is why Subramanian Swamy has now approached the court seeking permission for temple construction. The Supreme Court bench has asked Swamy to hold discussions with all sections and inform the court by March 31.

Those who committed the crime must be punished. L. K. Advani's rath yatra passed through the heart of the country. Although Lalu Prasad and Mulayam Singh, the Luv and Kush of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, tried to stop that rath yatra, Advani was able to ignite Hindutva sentiment through it. In an interview with India Today ahead of the kar seva, Advani said that the kar seva would not just be hymns and bhajans, but would involve the use of bricks and shovels. Vinay Katiyar, a Bajrang Dal leader waiting for the verdict along with Advani, said at the time, 'Even if the law stops it or there is a court order, the kar seva will happen.' Even more terrifying was the statement of another 'accused', VHP leader Ashok Singhal. 'If the kar sevaks are stopped, it will be hell here,' Singhal had said.

Important hearings and verdicts related to the Babri Masjid are coming up in the next few weeks. In a situation where state fascism is constantly hunting minorities, only a just decision by the judiciary gives hope to the minorities. December 6 is a dark day in world history. A day when India had to bow its head in shame before the world. This is an opportunity to restore the secular face of our country, which has been tarnished. It is unjustifiable to make claims on a place of worship of a community in the name of events without even historical evidence, to place idols under the cover of night and carry out encroachment, and later to demolish that place of worship and unleash riots across the country. The situation where anarchy prevails instead of the rule of law must be defeated beyond caste, religious, and political differences. We must be able to restore the lost glory of the country and thereby stand tall before the nations of the world.
