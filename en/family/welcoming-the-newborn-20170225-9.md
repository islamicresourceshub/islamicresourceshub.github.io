**Islamic Parenting: 7**

![](/assets/welcoming-the-newborn-20170225-9/25Feb17-12.jpg)

In the previous article, we discussed some important considerations for the period of pregnancy. Now, let us listen for the cry of the newborn. Parents should prepare to welcome their new guest by humbling themselves before Allah and renewing their repentance, keeping in mind the difficulties and pain of childbirth. Observe how Allah describes the moment of Maryam's delivery:

> "So she conceived him, and she withdrew with him to a remote place. And the pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree. She said, 'Oh, I wish I had died before this and was in oblivion, forgotten!' But he called her from below her, 'Do not grieve; your Lord has provided beneath you a stream. And shake toward you the trunk of the palm tree; it will drop upon you ripe, fresh dates. So eat and drink and be contented...'" (19:22-26).

These verses clarify how difficult the moment of childbirth was even for Maryam, the beloved servant of Allah, and how faith, prayer, and hope in Allah alleviated her hardships.

A child opening their eyes to a strange world outside the womb is truly a VIP. Therefore, the sacred texts provide certain protocols for welcoming this VIP. Through this, we begin the journey of Islamic parenting. Let us understand some key points:

## 1. Announcing the Good News
The birth of a child, who announces their presence by crying upon emerging from the womb, is indeed 'good news'. Allah says:

> "O Zakariya, indeed We give you good tidings of a boy..." (19:7).

> "And Our messengers had come to Ibrahim with good tidings..." (11:69). "And his (Prophet Ibrahim's) wife was standing, and she smiled. Then We gave her good tidings of Ishaq and after Ishaq, Yaqub" (11:72).

Believers, who should share in each other's joys and sorrows, ought to rejoice at a birth and respond in a way that brings happiness to the child and the parents, including offering prayers. However, the sacred texts do not prescribe any specific prayers or congratulatory phrases for a newborn.

## 2. Tahneek
Tahneek refers to the practice of rubbing a softened date or honey on the newborn's palate. It is reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) performed this on several occasions and that the Companions used to bring their children to him. Abu Musa (may Allah be pleased with him) says:

> "When a child was born to me, I took him to the Prophet (peace be upon him). He named him Ibrahim, rubbed his palate with a date, prayed for his blessing, and returned him to me." (Bukhari/Muslim)

Imam Bukhari also narrates that the Prophet (peace be upon him) did this when a child was born to Abu Talha and Umm Sulaym. Furthermore, Asma was pregnant when she migrated and gave birth to Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr upon reaching Quba. She says:

> "I went to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and placed the child in his lap. He asked for a date, chewed it, and put it into the child's mouth. Thus, the first thing to enter his stomach was the saliva of the Prophet (peace be upon him). Then he prayed for his blessing. Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr was the first boy born to the Muhajirun in Madinah. The Muslims rejoiced immensely at his birth because the enemies had spread a rumor that the Jews had cast a spell on them so that they would not have any sons." (Bukhari/Muslim)

## 3. Shaving the Head and Sacrifice (Aqiqah)
The seventh day after birth is a significant day in a child's life. It is narrated from Samurah (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> "Every child is mortgaged by his Aqiqah. It is to be slaughtered on the seventh day, and on that day, the head should be shaved and the child named." (Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ahmad)

We see that the Prophet (peace be upon him) commanded the shaving of the initial hair at the time of birth and the giving of silver in charity equal to its weight. Ibn Ishaq narrates from Muhammad ibn Ali, who said:

> "When Hasan (may Allah be pleased with him) was born, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said to his daughter Fatimah: 'Shave his hair.' Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with her) weighed it, and it was a dirham or a little less." 

Along with this, another act is the Aqiqah. The intention is to slaughter an animal and distribute the meat. The Companion Umm Kurz al-Ka'biyyah asked the Prophet (peace be upon him) about the Aqiqah, and he replied:

> "For a boy, two sheep, and for a girl, one sheep. It does not matter if they are male or female." (Ahmad)

Another Hadith narrated by Imam Tabarani (may Allah have mercy on him) states: The Aqiqah is to be slaughtered on the seventh day, or on the fourteenth, or on the twenty-first. Scholars are of the opinion that depending on the family's financial situation, any animal that meets the criteria for sacrifice is acceptable, though sheep were what was slaughtered during the time of the Prophet (peace be upon him). The seventh day is the most virtuous, but if that is not possible, it is accepted whenever one is able to perform it. While the methodology for distributing the meat is the same as that of the Udhiyah (sacrifice of Eid), it is also sufficient to prepare a meal at home and host a feast, provided that the participation of the poor is ensured.

## 4. Circumcision
This is the Prophetic tradition of removing the foreskin. It is a divine instruction that helps men achieve cleanliness and maintain health. In this modern age, with better medical facilities, it is better to perform this in the days following birth. The Prophet (peace be upon him) counted circumcision as part of the natural disposition of humans (Fitrah). (Bukhari/Muslim)

Imam Bayhaqi narrates a Hadith from Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him) stating that the Prophet (peace be upon him) performed the Aqiqah and circumcision for Hasan and Husayn (may Allah be pleased with them) on the seventh day. This practice began with Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) and later became the excellent tradition of the Prophets. Authentic Hadiths mention that if a person enters Islam, they should be circumcised regardless of their age, all of which highlights the necessity and naturalness of this act. This is precisely why jurisprudence books discuss matters like shaving the hair, Aqiqah, and circumcision in detail. Only when these acts are performed with a realization of their significance can one be worthy of the reward from Allah.
