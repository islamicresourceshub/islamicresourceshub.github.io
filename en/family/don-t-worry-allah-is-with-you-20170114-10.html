A Husband's Alcoholism

Is it wrong to live with an alcoholic husband?

Alcohol is strictly forbidden by the Quran and the Prophetic traditions (Hadith). There is no disagreement within the Muslim community that it is prohibited. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"Every intoxicant is khamr (alcohol), and every khamr is forbidden." (Muslim 37:35).

It has also been stated by the Prophet (peace be upon him) that those who consume alcohol will be given the discharge of the inhabitants of Hell to drink in the Hereafter. These facts cannot be ignored.

However, for a wife, there is no prohibition against living with such a husband, because one person is not punished for the sins committed by another.

"And no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another." (Fatir: 18).

A wife may receive reward for living with an alcoholic husband while patiently enduring everything. The fact that alcohol consumption does not make a person a disbeliever is the reason the marital bond remains valid.

Nevertheless, there are some important things this sister must understand. One cannot have high expectations from a husband who has made alcohol—the mother of all evils—a habit, as long as it persists. As children grow up, this will negatively affect their self-esteem and character. It will also pose a major obstacle to their future marriages and lives. Therefore, it is wise to seek solutions quickly. There is no point in creating unnecessary difficulties by blaming and insulting him. Results can be achieved through loving behavior, sincere advice, effective methods, and the positive intervention of relatives, friends, and religious scholars. Prayer is also the greatest remedy. According to the principle of countering evil with good, behaving even better can also bring about change.

You may try to bring about a change of heart through methods such as abstaining from the marital bed. De-addiction centers with a good moral environment can also lead to significant changes.

The Widow and the Orphan

My husband has passed away, and I have a son and a daughter. My son quit his studies. He has a small job. Things haven't turned out as expected. What should I do to set him on the right path?

This is a question from a widow who came in person with her nineteen-year-old son. I had the opportunity to speak with the boy in detail. Some symptoms of mental illness have truly begun to appear in him. It was the death of his father when he was in the third grade that broke him. Even today, that separation troubles him.

Whenever his father used to come home, he would bring snacks (bakery items) for tea. Even now, when he drinks tea, he remembers his father. His eyes fill with tears. From the mother's conversation, it was clear that she played a significant role in how his orphanhood weighed down his mind. The mother has her own problems and difficulties as a widow. If mothers do not know how to raise children without the father's presence becoming a problem, they will lose not only the husband but also the children who should have been their support. This is what happened. The son would come and ask, "Mom, I need ten rupees." The mother's response would be, "Child, where can I get money for you? Your father is not here, is he? Does someone have to give it to me first?"

But she does not realize that she is reminding her son of his father's absence. The mother's mental distress leads her to blame him repeatedly. Even then, the mother says, "When your father died, my hope was in you. But you quit your studies. What will we do now?" The painful thought that he had to bear the burden of his father at such a young age breaks him further.

What a widowed mother needs—or rather, what everyone who has faced tragedies in life needs—is to be content with what they have and to be able to live with self-confidence and courage. After her husband's death, this sister received a house of her own. Of her two children, one is a boy and the other a girl. A voluntary organization covers their living expenses. Currently, there are no specific deadly diseases or difficulties. Yet, despair and dissatisfaction are clearly visible on her face!

If widows live with hope and happiness, seeking reward for their pain, that happiness will be reflected in their children. If they live with despair, blame, and complaints, the children will feel as if they have lost both their father and their mother, and they will become mentally broken. That is what happened to this boy.
