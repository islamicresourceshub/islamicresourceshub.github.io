It is believed that Earth is the only place in the universe where life exists. Like humans, animals and plants depend on water. Animals and plants consume only the amount of water necessary to sustain life. Humans, however, use water for drinking, bathing, irrigation, industry, and a multitude of other purposes. Since the dawn of time, the economic and technological rise and fall of humanity have been linked to the availability of water.

## Water Availability and Challenges

Most of the Earth's water exists as seawater or ice. This means that the amount of water available for use is very small. Due to pollution and a lack of planned water management, drinking water has become a life-or-death issue in most countries today.

## Water Scarcity in Kerala

Kerala is a state that receives abundant water through rain. With 44 rivers, both large and small, 20 freshwater lakes, and countless streams, ponds, and wells, Kerala should not face water scarcity. Yet, Kerala has been facing drought since 1983. This is not happening because of a lack of rain. Kerala receives an average of 3,000 millimeters of rainfall, which is approximately 120 million cubic meters of water. Sixty percent of this water flows away into the sea immediately. To store water, we need topsoil, and to protect the soil, we need forests. Kerala once had 44 percent forest cover; today, it is only 9 percent.

## Groundwater Depletion

With the onset of water scarcity, the extraction of groundwater began. Every day, many new borewells are being dug. Even during the monsoon season, groundwater recharge is not happening. Wetlands and paddy fields are the primary means of groundwater recharge, but we have destroyed them all in the name of development. In 1975, Kerala had 875,000 hectares of farmland; today, only 215,000 hectares remain.

If we do not protect our water sources—rivers, streams, brooks, ponds, lakes, embankments, and wetlands—and harvest rainwater, we will have to face an acute water crisis.

## The Importance of Rainwater Harvesting

If rainwater is used effectively, water scarcity can be resolved. The main methods for rainwater harvesting include:

- Water pits
- Retaining walls
- Wetland construction
- Terracing
- Rooftop rainwater harvesting

Studies indicate that one-third of the world's population currently suffers from a shortage of drinking water, and this figure is expected to reach 50 percent by 2032. Only through planned water usage, water conservation, and rainwater harvesting can we overcome this crisis.

## The Islamic Perspective

**Islam** places great importance on water conservation.

> The Prophet commanded that one should not be wasteful even if performing ablution from a flowing river. Islam does not approve of extravagance in any matter.

Many people in Kerala have a habit of wasting water while bathing, washing clothes, or watering plants without realizing its value. This behavior should never be found in a true believer.

Water is a part of the Creator's mercy and a magnificent blessing and sign. **Allah** says:

> "And Allah has sent down rain from the sky and given life thereby to the earth after its lifelessness. Indeed in that is a sign for a people who listen." (16:65).
