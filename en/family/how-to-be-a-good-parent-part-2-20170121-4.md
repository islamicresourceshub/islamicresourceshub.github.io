# Part 2

![](/assets/how-to-be-a-good-parent-part-2-20170121-4/21Jan17-15.jpg)

**Islamic parenting** is the process of shaping a human being who can live according to the commands of the Creator and thus attain the capacity for Paradise in the afterlife. The teachings of the Prophet (PBUH) contain numerous ingredients and virtues that parents aiming for this goal must necessarily internalize and maintain.

Children grow, but parents and teachers often complain that they are neither compliant nor malleable. While these virtues should be common to every Muslim, they are indispensable for parents. This is because children do not just watch and evaluate their parents; rather, they observe and imitate them. Therefore, let us understand some qualities that we can acquire and that are not difficult to emulate:

1. Patience and Composure: We know that parenting is not a task that can be completed in a short period. It is a process that spans eighteen years or more. Parents are forced to go through numerous situations and make many decisions. Patience and composure are virtues that guide us through this. Once, when the Prophet (PBUH) saw his companion **Asad Abdul Qais**, he said:

   > "You possess two qualities that Allah loves: patience and composure."

   We must adopt a style where we evaluate matters with composure and respond slowly and patiently rather than making impulsive decisions. This is when children truly experience a parent or teacher. The linguistic style of the 132nd verse of **Al-Baqarah**, which commands the Prophet to enjoin prayer upon his family and remain steadfast in it, is worth studying. We can glean its practical methodology from the family life of the Prophet (PBUH).

2. Maintain Kindness and Compassion, and Abandon Harshness and Cruelty: Kindness is an essential quality for successful parenting. The absence of the kindness and compassion that should have been received from parents and teachers is often the cause of distorted personality development in many people. **Aisha (RA)** narrates that the Prophet (PBUH) used to say that even if we understand the ill intentions of someone interacting with us, we should respond with kindness. Once, a Jew (as was their custom) greeted the Prophet (PBUH) and his wife Aisha (RA) with malicious intent, saying 'Assam Alaikum' (may death be upon you), which sounds very similar to the Islamic greeting 'Assalamu Alaikum'. When Aisha (RA) understood this and replied with words meaning that death and a curse be upon him as well, the Prophet (PBUH) said:

   > "O Aisha! Allah is Kind and He loves kindness. Through kindness, one can achieve what cannot be achieved through harshness. What cannot be obtained through force can be obtained through kindness." (Muslim).

   In another narration, Aisha (RA) quotes the Prophet (PBUH) as saying:

   > "Allah is Kind, and He loves kindness in all matters." (**Bukhari**, **Muslim**).

   Furthermore, the Prophet (PBUH) taught:

   > "Kindness is not found in anything but that it beautifies it, and it is not removed from anything but that it makes it ugly." (Muslim).

   We often show kindness to others for fear of lowering our own status, yet we fail to show kindness to our own children and family members.

## Let us look at the scene of waking children up in the morning

We often try to wake our children by shouting, yelling, or making threats. Instead, try changing your style to one where the mother or father goes to them, gently taps them to wake them, says 'Salam', and if possible, lies down with them for two minutes, hugging them and reciting the morning prayer aloud. You will see that the goal is achieved without any vocal explosions. The reason is simple: by satisfying their desire to be touched by their parents for even two minutes, they find it easy to surrender their sleepiness to your request.
