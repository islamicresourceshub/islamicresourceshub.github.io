## Experiences from School Annual Days

For the past few years, I have been invited to their school's annual day. They distribute prizes to every child who participates in the competitions. These prizes are sponsored by a friend of mine. Yet, this friend has never once attended the school's annual day. The reason is that it is a school for children with intellectual disabilities, and he simply cannot bear to watch them.

One particular sight I witnessed on one of these annual days has never faded from my memory.

## Scenes on the Stage

A group of children was performing on stage, presenting an art form similar to Oppana. Dressed in vibrant costumes and makeup, they were dancing when one boy's dhoti began to slip off. This caused quite a bit of anxiety among the audience. However, whether he realized it or not, he kept on dancing. Even though a teacher from the wings tried to signal him, he did not seem to understand. Finally, the teacher walked onto the stage, moving along with his dance steps, and gently adjusted his clothes. He just kept dancing. Since he was not a child of typical cognitive development, the slipping of his clothes did not bother or embarrass him at all.

## Unexpected Incidents

During the annual day of the previous year, I was given the opportunity to speak. While the Municipal Council Chairman was delivering his inaugural address, one of the students in the audience suddenly threw himself to the ground, rolling around and screaming hysterically. The child was dressed in colorful costumes and makeup, ready for his stage performance. Although his friends and parents tried to intervene, he refused to get up or stop crying. The teachers stood there helpless and bewildered, worried that the entire event would be disrupted.

Eventually, the chief guest paused his speech. The headmistress stepped down from the stage and walked over to the boy. As if by a miraculous touch or some magic words, the child stopped crying instantly. He went back and sat in his chair, waiting for his turn as if nothing had happened.

## Being Grateful

When my turn came to speak, the thoughts I had prepared completely vanished from my mind. I managed to say a few words, reflecting on how the Creator tests us by giving us children with intellectual challenges, and how the love and care shown by the mothers and teachers of these children are truly exemplary.

By the time I finished speaking, my eyes were filled with tears. The Creator has blessed most of us with children free from any physical or mental disabilities. How deeply blessed we are! But do we ever show gratitude for this?
