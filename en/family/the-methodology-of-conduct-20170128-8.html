The Methodology of Conduct

അഷ്‌റഫ്‌ എകരൂൽ · 2017-01-28 · Family & Society

In the previous issue, we read about the qualities and habits that believers seeking excellence in parenting should internalize. Let us add a few more to that list.

3. Giving the choice between two options (provided there is nothing sinful involved)

This is something many parents overlook. If our children suggest a choice that is not sinful to complete a task we have requested or desired, allowing them to do so helps them feel a sense of their own identity. Often, the insistence that things must be done "exactly as I said" creates a gap between parents and children, which in turn gives birth to disobedience.

Aisha (RA) said about the Prophet (SAW):

Whenever the Prophet was given a choice between two matters, he would choose the easiest of the two, as long as it did not involve anything sinful... (Bukhari, Muslim).

If we take this as a parenting tip, we can experience its benefits. Let us take an example: we ask our son to go to a shop a little distance away to buy something. He immediately asks, "Can I go by bicycle?" If there are no particular dangers at that moment, and we do not insist, "No, you must walk," there are two benefits. First, it saves time. Second, it gives the child a sense of psychological elevation because his interest was considered.

4. A heart full of mercy

This is essential for every parent. Our commands and decisions should be moistened with mercy. Once children experience this, speed and sincerity in obeying us will begin to sprout. Those who were with the Prophet (SAW) were able to experience the mercy of his heart.

Malik bin Huwayrith (RA) says:

We were a group of young men of similar age who went to the Prophet (SAW) and stayed with him for about twenty nights (to learn). The Prophet (SAW) was merciful and kind. He sensed that we had begun to long for our families. He asked us about the families we had left behind. Then he said to us: Go back to your families and stay with them. Teach them and do good to them. Pray in such and such a way at such and such times. When the time comes, one of you should call the Adhan, and the eldest among you should lead the prayer (Bukhari, Muslim).

5. Flexibility and ease

When dealing with children, one should have a flexible approach. Do not hold on to a rigid, stone-like method. By flexibility and ease, we mean that we should not build fences and restrict ourselves within the vastness and permissions that the religion has provided. Ibn Mas'ud (RA) says that the Prophet (SAW) said:

Shall I tell you who is forbidden from the Fire? It is forbidden for everyone who is flexible, humble, simple, and kind-hearted (Tirmidhi).

We are aiming for a generation of believers who grow up experiencing that "religion is not a source of tension" while remaining within the permissible limits of the faith.

6. Controlling anger

Repeated outbursts of anger and frantic insistence are bad habits that produce negative results in child-rearing. This is a matter about which the Prophet (SAW) repeatedly warned his community. The task of child-rearing is a long journey, and these two traits are obstacles in this journey that should be pleasant. To a companion who came seeking advice, the Prophet (SAW) repeated three times: "Do not get angry" (Bukhari).

As the Prophet (SAW) taught, our ability to control anger is also a criterion for measuring our strength and courage. A narration from Abu Hurayrah (RA) states:

The strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling; rather, the strong person is the one who controls himself when he is angry (Bukhari).

Parents who have to interact with their children constantly must always remember this.

7. Maintaining a moderate stance

As in any field, adopting the moderate stance taught by Islam is essential in child-rearing. This is a vital quality in parenting. Extremism and neglect both lead to ruin. Whether saying no, commanding to do something, or expressing love, a moderate approach must be maintained.

When a companion said that he would be late for the mosque because the person leading the morning prayer was making it too long, the Prophet (SAW) said in anger:

O people! Among you are those who drive people away by making them hate the prayer. If any of you leads the prayer, make it short. For behind you are the elderly, the children, and those who have other needs (Bukhari, Muslim).

We must not forget to consider this stance in the responsibilities we entrust to children, the entertainment we allow, and basic needs like eating and sleeping.
