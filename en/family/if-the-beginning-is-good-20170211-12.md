## Islamic Parenting: 5

![](/assets/if-the-beginning-is-good-20170211-12/11Feb17-15.jpg)

In previous installments, we understood some of the things the Creator has taught regarding offspring. Parents must internalize these before embarking on the mission of parenting. Since this knowledge comes from the One who is beyond the constraints of time and space, successful Islamic parenting is impossible without considering these teachings.

## Where to Begin?

This is a natural question. The correct answer is: from where you are. You could be anyone—single, a newlywed, or already a parent. The Islamic perspective on any matter is to start correcting from where you currently stand. Life is not a lamentation of losses, but an utilization of opportunities.

If you make the beginning good and start early, you can certainly ensure a good and timely harvest. The truth is that good preparation lightens the hardships and pains of this journey. Allah, who made human life safe and peaceful by providing laws and instructions that align with human nature, has also provided guidelines to build a solid foundation for parenting that surpasses any worldly study and is flawless. Let us examine some of them.

1. Make a Good Choice: The first step for those who dream of good offspring is to exercise great caution and care in choosing a life partner and to consider divine guidance. There is no ambiguity in the light Islam provides on this path. It is narrated from Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'Choose well for your seed' (Ibn Majah, Hakim).

Multiple narrations to this effect have been cited as being said by the Prophet (peace be upon him) in different contexts. It is the duty of a believer to pay attention to the criteria for selection. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'A woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, and her religious commitment. Choose the one who is religiously committed and you will succeed, even if your hands are covered in dust' (Bukhari).

Scholars have noted that 'even if your hands are covered in dust' implies 'even if you have to experience poverty.' Another Hadith states: 'If someone whose religion and character you are pleased with comes to you for marriage, then marry him. If you do not do so, there will be widespread corruption on earth' (Tirmidhi).

The Quranic declaration that 'corrupt women are for corrupt men, and corrupt men are for corrupt women' (24:3) highlights the necessity of making faith and ideology the primary criteria in choosing a life partner. This means that along with the question of whether they are suitable as a spouse, a believer must also ask the additional question of whether they are suitable to be a mother or father to their children. Allah says:

> 'Corrupt women are for corrupt men, and corrupt men are for corrupt women. Good women are for good men, and good men are for good women' (24:26).

The Prophet (peace be upon him), while emphasizing the necessity of good offspring, makes us understand the need for a capable partner in managing the family.

It is narrated from Anas bin Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'Marry those who are loving and prolific, for I will be proud of your numbers before the other nations on the Day of Resurrection' (Ibn Hibban).

Therefore, a good choice is indeed important. A farmer seeking good soil is not being greedy or unnecessary; rather, it is a firm step toward achieving the goal.

2. Start with Hope and Prayer for Offspring: Along with marriage being a permissible way to fulfill human sexual needs, the continuation of the human race is also its purpose.

Allah says:

> 'And Allah has made for you mates from among yourselves and has made for you, from your mates, sons and grandsons' (16:72).

The intention here is that even during the sweet early days, there should be hope and prayer for righteous offspring. This is also the model of the Prophets. Look at the prayer of Prophet Zakariya (peace be upon him):

> 'At that, Zakariya called upon his Lord, saying, "My Lord, grant me from Yourself a good offspring. Indeed, You are the Hearer of supplication"' (3:38).

**Imam Qurtubi**, while explaining this verse, says: 'This is evidence that asking for a child is the tradition of the Messengers and the truthful.' There is also a hint in the prayer of Prophet Zakariya (peace be upon him) that one should not abandon the hope and prayer for children even if health begins to decline and age increases.

There is even a chapter in **Sahih al-Bukhari** titled 'Seeking children who are prepared for striving in the way of God.' The Prophet (peace be upon him) also taught that the physical intimacy between spouses should be accompanied by prayer for children who are blessed with divine grace and untouched by the touch of the devil, and that it should be done while remembering the name of Allah.

It is narrated from Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

> 'If any of you, when intending to approach his wife, says: "In the name of Allah, O Allah, keep the devil away from us and keep the devil away from what You bestow upon us," and if it is decreed that they should have a child from that union, the devil will never be able to harm him' (Bukhari, Muslim).
