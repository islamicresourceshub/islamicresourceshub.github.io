# Prevention is Better than Regret...

**Ashraf Ekarul**

We have been discussing the reasons why children go astray and the Islamic solutions to these issues. Let us now examine other factors that require the attention of parents and society:

4. Poor Parental Conduct: When searching for the reasons why children lose their way, we often find that the culprit is closer than we think. Observers who have studied juvenile delinquency suggest that, in many cases, it is the parents themselves who create these troubled children. 

Children who feel their home is a prison due to constant scolding, anger, excessive discipline, and a lack of regard for their personality, age, or self-respect, often begin to react to life with a sense of vengeance. Their journey to find a world of solace away from home marks the beginning of their descent. 

Many parents and teachers fail to take seriously the teachings of Islam, which instruct us to treat children with dignity and gentleness. When childhood and adolescence are wounded by excessive punishment and humiliation, children may reach the dangerous conclusion that morality and family structures are merely oppressive and anti-human. Consequently, they adopt an independent lifestyle, begin to disregard rules, and view law-breaking as trivial.

Allah and His Messenger teach us to treat all people with kindness. We are advised to control our anger and are encouraged to make patience and forgiveness a part of our character. We are reminded not to abandon compassion, with warnings that even a harsh heart will drive people away from us. Can we assume that this does not apply to our own children? Should we not be implementing these values in our own homes first? Allah says: “Indeed, Allah orders justice and good conduct and giving to relatives...” (16:90).

Describing the qualities of the righteous, Allah says: “Who restrain anger and who pardon the people - and Allah loves the doers of good” (3:134).

“And speak to people good words...” (2:83). “(O Prophet), if you had been rude and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you” (3:159).

The Prophet ﷺ reminded us: “Indeed, Allah loves gentleness in all matters” (Bukhari). This is what must be practiced in our homes.

5. Unstructured Leisure Time: A passion for play is part of the biological nature of children and adolescents. As they grow, this interest grows with them. They love to roam in nature with their peers. Parents must be able to recognize this natural inclination and utilize it constructively. We should intentionally create opportunities for harmless recreation that provides physical and mental refreshment, or guide them toward such environments. Otherwise, they will naturally seek their own paths, often falling into the company of bad influences. This leads to them becoming a headache for their families and society. Investigations into children caught stealing in some Kerala cities revealed that they lacked constructive engagement.

Adolescents should participate in camps and study circles. A weekly package could include Quran memorization classes and computer training. Through such Islamic upbringing, children can navigate their childhood and adolescence safely. 

However, in our society, we often lose touch with children once they finish their seventh-grade Madrasa education. We only call this ‘lost’ generation to Islamic institutions for occasional Quran classes or Iftar gatherings. If, along with Islamic institutions, our local Mahallas provided systems that satisfy these natural developmental interests, we would not see the rise of the ‘new-gen’ youth who escape the guidance of their parents and elders.

6. Bad Company: Islam does not teach us to keep children isolated like ornamental fish. Islam recognizes that socializing and having friends is part of human nature and provides the necessary guidance for it. It views friendship as a platform for sharing goodness. While encouraging companionship with good people, it also warns of the dangers of bad company. Children often go astray because parents fail to keep an eye on their friendships. The solution is to encourage good friendships and make them aware of the dangers of bad ones. Allah says: “And the Day the wrongdoer will bite on his hands [in regret] he will say, ‘Oh, I wish I had taken with the Messenger a way. Oh, woe to me! I wish I had not taken that one as a friend. He led me away from the remembrance after it had come to me. And ever is Satan, to man, a deserter’” (25:27-29). “Close friends, that Day, will be enemies to each other, except for the righteous” (43:67). This verse teaches us that Allah only approves of righteous friendship.

The Prophet ﷺ said: “A person is upon the ‘religion’ of his friend, so let each of you look at whom he befriends” (Tirmidhi). This is a command that many parents ignore. That neglect is one of the primary factors in children going astray.
