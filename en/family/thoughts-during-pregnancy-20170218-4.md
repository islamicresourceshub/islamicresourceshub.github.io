**Islamic Parenting: 6**

![](/assets/thoughts-during-pregnancy-20170218-4/18Feb17-12.jpg)

We previously discussed the significance of a good beginning, filled with prayer and hope. With the happy news of a spouse's pregnancy, those prayers are fulfilled, and hopes begin to blossom. There are certain things that couples awaiting a child must necessarily know.

## Pregnancy and the Decree of Allah

References to pregnancy and the unborn child can be found in the **Quran** and the sayings of the Prophet. Most important among these are the Creator's references regarding the creation of the child. The timing of the child's creation, the determination of gender, and physical features are all within the exclusive authority of Allah. Human destiny ends where the utilization of the material resources provided by Allah to man concludes. Everything related to the child—time, gender, color, form, shape, and health—happens in the womb according to the discretion and decree of Allah. These may sometimes be beyond or contrary to human expectations and experiences. A true believer is one who accepts whatever Allah decrees with joy, without anxiety. Allah says:

> “And your Lord creates what He wills and chooses; not for them was the choice...” (28:68).

Regarding the pregnancy of Maryam, Allah said:

> “She said, ‘My Lord, how will I have a child when no man has touched me?’ [The angel] said, ‘Such is Allah; He creates what He wills. When He decrees a matter, He only says to it, “Be,” and it is.’” (3:47).

> “To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth; He creates what He wills. He gives to whom He wills female [children], and He gives to whom He wills males. Or He makes them [both] males and females, and He renders whom He wills barren. Indeed, He is Knowing and Competent.” (42:49,50).

This means that no one has any reason to blame another regarding the gender, color, or physical appearance of children. Couples must understand that showing resentment toward a child who arrives at a time one did not intend is contrary to Islamic teachings and, therefore, contrary to humanity.

Allah said:

> “It is He who forms you in the wombs however He wills. There is no deity except Him, the Exalted in Might, the Wise.” (3:6).

> “Allah knows what every female carries and what the wombs lose [prematurely] or exceed. And everything with Him is by due measure.” (13:8).

## The Wonders of Creation

With the onset of pregnancy, it is natural for couples to begin calculations and conversations surrounding the coming child. Sometimes, these calculations can lead to a negative attitude in one of the partners. It is a fact that in some families, the excitement and joy felt upon hearing the news of a boy's birth are not mirrored when the news of a girl's birth is received.

The unique systems of the Creator behind the creation of a child are deeply thought-provoking. The Quran explains how a human being is formed from what is considered a trivial drop of fluid. Insights into this help couples understand the value of the offspring and prevent negligence in the responsibility of caring for the child; it may also prevent the variations in the quality of the offspring from fostering unjust pride or groundless feelings of shame in parents. Observe some of the verses revealed on this subject:

> “So let man observe from what he was created. He was created from a fluid, ejected, emerging from between the backbone and the ribs.” (86:5-7).

> “Indeed, We created man from a sperm-drop mixture that We may test him; and We made him hearing and seeing.” (76:2).

> “He creates you in the wombs of your mothers, creation after creation, within three darknesses. That is Allah, your Lord; to Him belongs dominion.” (39:6).

> “O Mankind, if you are in doubt about the Resurrection, then indeed We created you from dust, then from a sperm-drop, then from a clinging clot, and then from a lump of flesh, formed and unformed - that We may show you. And We settle in the wombs whom We will for a specified term, then We bring you out as a child...” (22:5).

> “Then We placed him as a sperm-drop in a firm lodging. Then We made the sperm-drop into a clinging clot, and We made the clot a lump [of flesh], and We made [from] the lump, bones, and We covered the bones with flesh; then We developed him into another creation...” (23:13,14).

## The Greatness of Motherhood

All these extremely subtle and complex phenomena occur in the womb of one's spouse. Therefore, she is worthy of consideration and care as a pregnant woman. Allah makes us aware that pregnancy and childbirth are difficult tasks:

> “His mother carried him, [increasing her] in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years.” (31:14).

> “His mother carried him with hardship and gave birth to him with hardship...” (46:15).

Through these two verses, the Creator Himself introduces the fatigue of the pregnancy period and the difficulty of childbirth. Therefore, Muslim couples should view pregnancy and the difficulties of that stage with a kind heart, anticipating the fatigue and hardship and embracing the greatness of the mission entrusted by Allah. We must not be among those who react to a pregnancy that occurred at an 'unwanted time' in a way that displeases Allah. Often, this 'unwanted time' has no justification whatsoever. Exams waiting to be written, a wedding to take place in the family, pre-planned foreign trips, or vacations—these are often the reasons for such resentment. Such people are foolish, unaware of the value and status of Allah's gift.

There is another lesson for us in the divine indications regarding the pregnant woman's hardship: the husband must be able to see and recognize the fatigue and difficulty his spouse is experiencing; she is in dire need of the shade of love and comfort. As the days of pregnancy progress, the mother who carries the weight of the child and the husband who carries the weight of caring for her should intensify their prayers for a righteous child. Allah says:

> “It is He who created you from one soul and created from it its mate that he might dwell in security with her. And when he covers her, she carries a light burden and continues therein. And when it becomes heavy, they both supplicate to Allah, their Lord, ‘If You should give us a good [child], we will surely be among the grateful.’” (7:189).

In short, for couples who move forward with firm conviction in divine guidance, prayer with determination, and appropriate care, childbirth will not be a source of endless anxiety; it will surely be a source of endless hope!
