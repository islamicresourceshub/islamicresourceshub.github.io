The Departure of the Prophet

സയ്യിദ് സഅ്ഫര്‍ സ്വാദിക്വ് മദീനി · 2018-05-05 · Seerah & Biographies

The mission of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ marked the conclusion of the chain of prophethood. Through this Prophet, Allah completed His religion. The Prophet ﷺ was born and lived for fifty-three years in Makkah, and he passed away in Madinah.

Hadith collections record that the Prophet ﷺ warned that his death would be the greatest calamity to befall the Muslims.

The Cessation of Revelation

Umm Ayman (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated: When the Prophet ﷺ passed away, she wept. When asked why she was crying, she said: "By Allah, I knew that the Prophet ﷺ would die. But what made me weep is that the revelation from the heavens has ceased" (Ahmad).

Abu Burdah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The stars are a source of security for the sky; when the stars disappear, what has been promised to the heavens will come to pass. I am a source of security for my companions; when I am gone, what has been promised to my companions will come to pass. My companions are a source of security for my community; when my companions are gone, what has been promised to my community will come to pass" (Muslim: 2531).

Explaining this hadith, Imam Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him) states in his commentary: "'I am a source of security for my companions; when I am gone, what has been promised to my companions will come to pass' refers to the clear warnings the Prophet ﷺ gave regarding tribulations, wars, apostasy among the Bedouins, and divisions among the Muslims. All of these have already occurred" (Sharh al-Nawawi: 8/316).

The Approach of Death

Through numerous verses in the Holy Quran, the Prophet ﷺ understood that his departure was near even before death arrived.

"Indeed, you are to die, and indeed, they are to die" (Quran 39:30).

"And We did not grant to any man before you eternity [on earth]; so if you die - would they be the eternal ones? Every soul will taste death" (Quran 21:34, 35).

"Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels [to disbelief]? And he who turns back on his heels will never harm Allah at all; but Allah will reward the grateful" (Quran 3:144).

"When the victory of Allah has come and the conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes, then exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance" (Quran 110:1-3).

Ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: "The Surah 'When the victory of Allah has come...' was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ during the middle of the Days of Tashreeq. From this, he understood that his departure was near" (Sunan al-Bayhaqi).

Ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: "Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) asked the companions about the Surah 'When the victory of Allah has come...'. They replied: 'It refers to the conquest of cities and palaces.' Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) asked Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) for his opinion. He said: 'It was an indication to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ regarding his own death and the end of his term'" (Bukhari: 4969).

"This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion" (Quran 5:3).

Explaining this verse, Ibn al-Arabi states in his book Al-Awasim min al-Qawasim: "In this world, what follows a completed matter is only deficiency and imperfection; perfection is found in seeking the pleasure of Allah" (page: 59).

The Prophet ﷺ shared with his companions on more than one occasion that his time had arrived and that he was moving toward the presence of his Lord.

When the Prophet ﷺ sent Mu'adh (may Allah be pleased with him) to Yemen, after giving him advice and instructions, he said: "O Mu'adh! Perhaps you will not see me after this year. Perhaps when you pass by my mosque, you will be passing by my grave." Mu'adh (may Allah be pleased with him) wept upon realizing that the Prophet ﷺ was about to depart. Then, turning toward Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ said: "The closest people to me are the righteous, whoever they are and wherever they may be" (Ahmad).

He even informed his own daughter that he was about to depart from this world.

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated: Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with her) came walking toward the Prophet ﷺ. Her walk was like the walk of the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Welcome, my daughter." Then he seated her to his right. He whispered something to her, and she wept. I asked her: "Why are you weeping?" Then he whispered to her again, and she laughed... I asked her what he had said. Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with her) replied: "I will not disclose the secret of the Prophet ﷺ." After the Prophet ﷺ passed away, I asked her again. She said: "He whispered to me that Jibril (peace be upon him) used to recite the Quran with him once every year, but this year he recited it twice. I understand this to mean that my time has arrived. You will be the first of my family to join me. So I wept. Then he said: 'Are you not pleased to be the leader of the women of Paradise or the leader of the believing women?' So I laughed" (Bukhari: 3624, Muslim: 2450).

Illness and Cause

The beginning and cause of the illness that led to the Prophet's ﷺ death was the consumption of poisoned meat given to him by a Jewish woman years earlier. He had told his companions: "Withdraw your hands from this food. I have been informed that there is poison in it." Later, while on his deathbed, he said: "I still feel the pain of the food I ate in Khaybar. It is as if it is cutting my aorta" (Abu Dawud: 4512).

Umm Bishr (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated: She visited the Messenger of Allah ﷺ while he was on his deathbed and said: "O Messenger of Allah, may my parents be sacrificed for you. There is no reason for your condition other than the food you ate in Khaybar." Her son had eaten that food and passed away. The Prophet ﷺ said: "I do not think it is anything other than that; it is as if it is cutting my veins" (Ahmad).

Through this, the Prophet ﷺ attained martyrdom «Shahadah» at the hands of those who used to kill the prophets. Martyrdom is the most noble of deaths. Along with this, he suffered from illness, which served to elevate his status further.

The Prophet's ﷺ illness began when he was returning from burying one of his companions. Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says:

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ returned to me after burying a funeral in Baqi. I had a headache and was saying: "Oh, my head!" He said: "No, rather, oh, my head! It is very painful." The Prophet ﷺ said: "If you die before me, I will wash you, shroud you, pray over you, and bury you." I said: "If that happens, you will return to my house and live with your other wives, won't you?" The Messenger of Allah ﷺ smiled. Then the illness that led to his passing began (Ahmad, Ibn Majah).

The Prophet ﷺ Chooses Death

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: While addressing the people, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Indeed, Allah gave a servant the choice between this world and what is with Him, and the servant chose what is with Allah." Hearing this, Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) wept. We were surprised by his weeping. Allah had only given a servant a choice; why weep? But the servant who was given the choice was the Prophet ﷺ. Abu Bakr was the most knowledgeable among us" (Bukhari: 3654, Muslim: 2383).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: While in perfect health, the Prophet ﷺ once said: "No prophet dies without being shown his place in Paradise. Then he is given the choice." So, while he was ill, resting his head on Aisha's (may Allah be pleased with her) lap, he would lose consciousness. When he regained consciousness, he would fix his eyes on the ceiling of the house and say: "O Allah, to the Highest Companion..." (Bukhari 4437).

Scholars have expressed different opinions regarding the meaning of "Highest Companion," suggesting it refers to the angels, the prophets, or Paradise. Each group has its own evidence.

The Prophet's ﷺ Illness

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: "When the Prophet's ﷺ illness became severe, he experienced intense pain. He asked his other wives for permission to stay in my house during his illness, and they all granted it. He went out for prayer, leaning on Abbas and another man, with his feet dragging on the ground."

Ubaydullah says: Abdullah ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) asked if I knew who the other man was. He said: "No." It was Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) continues: "When he entered his house, his pain intensified. He said: 'Pour seven skins of water over me, perhaps I will find relief and be able to give my final instructions to the people.' So they seated the Prophet in a large basin belonging to his wife Hafsa (may Allah be pleased with her). They poured water over him, and when he felt some relief, he went out to the people" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: "During his final illness, he tied a cloth around his head, climbed the pulpit, praised and glorified Allah, and then said: 'No one has helped me with their wealth or person as Abu Bakr ibn Abi Quhafa has. If I were to take a close friend «Khalil» from the people, I would have taken Abu Bakr. But the brotherhood of Islam is the most noble. Close all the small doors leading into this mosque, except for the door of Abu Bakr'" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) narrated: "When the illness of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ became severe, Bilal (may Allah be pleased with him) came to call for prayer. The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people in prayer.' I said to him: 'Abu Bakr is a soft-hearted man. If he stands in your place, he will not be able to recite the Quran due to weeping.' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Tell Abu Bakr to lead the prayer.' She repeated her reply. When she repeated it a third or fourth time, he said: 'You are like the women who tried to entrap Yusuf. Tell Abu Bakr to lead the prayer.' So Abu Bakr led the prayer. Then the Prophet ﷺ walked toward the prayer, supported by two men. I saw his feet dragging on the ground because he could not keep them steady. When Abu Bakr saw the Prophet, he tried to step back, but the Prophet ﷺ gestured for him to stay where he was. The Prophet ﷺ sat beside Abu Bakr, and Abu Bakr made the people hear the takbir" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated: "Due to his illness, the Prophet ﷺ did not come out for three days. When the iqamah for prayer was called, Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) went out to lead the prayer. The Prophet ﷺ lifted the curtain of his house. We could see his face clearly. We had never seen a sight more wonderful than the face of the Prophet ﷺ at that moment. He gestured for Abu Bakr to remain as the imam. Then the Prophet ﷺ lowered the curtain. He remained in his house until his death" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Hisham narrated from his father: "When the Prophet ﷺ was ill, he would go to his wives and say: 'Where am I tomorrow? Where am I tomorrow?' He was hoping for Aisha's (may Allah be pleased with her) house. Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: 'When it was my day, he was calm'" (Bukhari).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: "When he was ill, he would recite the Mu'awwidhat (the last three Surahs of the Quran) and blow over himself, wiping his body with his own hands. But during his final illness, I used to recite them, blow over him, and wipe his body with his own hands" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: "When we were talking about the Prophet's ﷺ illness, he said: 'Do not talk about my illness.' We said: 'It is just the patient's dislike for the illness.' When he regained consciousness, he said: 'Everyone among you has spoken about my illness, except Abbas, because he was not present with you'" (Bukhari, Muslim). (To be continued)
