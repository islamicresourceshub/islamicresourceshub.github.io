**Prophet Muhammad (pbuh)** is the greatest human being the world has ever seen. He was a personality who won the respect and love of even his enemies through his exemplary conduct.

His life serves as a model for everyone—for teachers, students, leaders, rulers, and military commanders alike. The message his life conveyed is both timeless and universal. His mission was a mercy to the entire world.

> "And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds" (Quran).

Observe how Allah introduces the Prophet (pbuh), who taught only those principles that provide goodness in both worlds:

> "Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet, whom they find written in what they have of the Torah and the Gospel, who enjoins upon them what is right and forbids them what is wrong and makes lawful for them the good things and prohibits for them the evil and relieves them of their burden and the shackles which were upon them. So they who have believed in him, honored him, supported him and followed the light which was sent down with him - it is those who will be the successful. Say, [O Muhammad], 'O mankind, indeed I am the Messenger of Allah to you all, [from Him] to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth'" (Al-A'raf 157, 158).

In every sphere of life, there is a sublime model in his conduct:

> "There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent pattern for anyone whose hope is in Allah and the Last Day and [who] remembers Allah often" (Al-Ahzab 21).

## The Prophet's Lifestyle and Simplicity

He never told a lie in his life. He never deceived anyone. He never broke a promise. He did not view the goal of life as material gain. In every matter, he acted with the hereafter in mind.

> The Prophet (pbuh) said, "What have I to do with this world? My connection with this world is only like a traveler who rests for a while under the shade of a tree and then moves on" (**Tirmidhi**).

It is when one prioritizes worldly life that the desire to defeat others arises, leading to stubbornness, hostility, hatred, lies, and hypocrisy. Otherwise, a person's heart remains pure. Earning through forbidden means, greed, and avarice all stem from viewing this world as greater than the hereafter.

**Aisha (ra)** says: "The Prophet (pbuh) never filled his stomach with bread and meat unless he had guests." He lived for days at a time on nothing but water and dates. Even at the time of his death, his armor was mortgaged at a Jewish man's house.

## Devotion in Worship

Yet, none of this hindered him from fulfilling his duties to Allah or performing acts of worship. He would pray at night until his feet became swollen. When asked about this, the Prophet replied, "Should I not be a grateful servant, Aisha?"

- He would sleep in the first part of the night and pray in the latter part.
- He would rise immediately upon hearing the call to prayer.
- If he was in a state of major ritual impurity, he would bathe; otherwise, he would perform wudu and head out for prayer.
- After the Subh prayer, he would remain in the place of prayer until the sun had risen well.
- He would pray four rak'ahs of Duha prayer, sometimes even more.

He would weep during prayer, with a sound like a boiling pot on a stove. His tongue was constantly occupied with the remembrance of Allah.

## Approach to Fellow Beings

Not only in his relationship with the Creator but also in his dealings with fellow beings, the Prophet (pbuh) was deeply attentive. His character, conduct, and culture were all such that they attracted everyone.

> Allah said, "And indeed, you are of a great moral character." His wife Aisha (ra) said, "His character was the **Quran**."

He spoke slowly and clearly so that listeners could understand. There was always a smile on his face. There was not a trace of arrogance in him; he was the embodiment of humility.

> It is highly noteworthy that he declared, "Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated in praising Jesus, son of Mary. I am only a servant of Allah and His Messenger."

He lived among the common people. He traveled with them. He did everything as one of them.

> "If someone were to gift me a bone without meat, I would accept it. If they invited me to eat it, I would accept that invitation." These words of the Prophet (pbuh) are a sign of his equality and humility.

**Anas (ra)** says: "I served the Prophet for ten years. He never said a harsh word to me. He never asked me why I did something I had done, nor why I had not done something I had not done."

He was not a person who spoke or acted obscenely. He was not among those who created noise in the marketplace. He did not counter evil with evil; his habit was to forgive and overlook. He never hurt anyone with his own hand, except in a battle for the sake of Allah.

## Mercy and Family Life

He taught that one would be rewarded for showing mercy to any living creature with a beating heart, and he embodied this in his own life. He instructed that when traveling with camels in the summer, one should move quickly, and in the winter, one should move slowly so the camels could graze. He encouraged showing mercy even to birds and ants.

He gave to whoever asked; he never said no. He maintained dignity both at home and in society—something many find difficult today! He would wash his own clothes, milk his goats, and help his wives with household chores. He would leave for the mosque upon hearing the call to prayer. He had nine wives when he passed away, and none had any complaints because he lived with justice. He shared his love equally among them all.

> He demonstrated the principle, "The best of you is the best to his wife," in his own life.

**Aisha (ra)** says: "He would take the cup I had drunk from and drink from it. He would take the meat I had bitten and bite from the same spot. Even when he performed wudu and was leaving for prayer, he would give me a kiss on the way. I and the Messenger would bathe together. He would rest his head on my lap. I used to comb his hair."

These are the radiant moments of the exemplary life of a man who held the authority of a nation and was the Prophet for all people until the Last Day.

After returning from the Battle of Khaybar, his marriage to Safiyyah (ra) took place. He offered his knee for her to step on to mount the camel. Despite having more than one wife, there was no family strife because of it. Even during travels, he would draw lots among his wives to ensure justice. He encouraged and motivated his wives in worship. In the middle of the night, he would wake them up for prayer.

He would kiss children, caress them, and play with them. When Ja'far (ra) was martyred in battle, the Prophet (pbuh) hugged his children and wept. His love for Fatimah (ra) was beyond words. We must remember that this exemplary life was lived in an era when girls were neglected.

He provided guidance to young children as needed. He told **Ibn Abbas (ra)**: "Be mindful of Allah, and He will protect you. Be mindful of Allah, and you will find Him before you. If you ask, ask of Allah; if you seek help, seek help from Allah."

He taught children good manners, such as eating in the name of Allah, eating with the right hand, and eating from what is in front of them. He maintained an approach of dignity, patience, and forgiveness toward neighbors, enemies, and those who did wrong. He never oppressed anyone unjustly. He never seized what belonged to others.

> There is no other person in history known to have prayed for those who persecuted him, saying, "O Allah, forgive my people, for they do not know."
