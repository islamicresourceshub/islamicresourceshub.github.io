Prophets are Human Beings

Hussain Salafi, Sharjah

Prophets are the most noble of all creations. Yet, they are human beings. Often, the clergy has deconstructed the image of the Prophets by inventing excessive praises. This writing exposes such fallacies.

There are some in our land who, in the name of glorifying the Prophet, elevate him above human nature, claiming he was created from light and had no shadow. In the Mawlid books they hold in high esteem, one can find the following:

“The shadow of the Prophet did not fall upon the earth, for he was light, both manifest and hidden” (Ja’la Muhammad Mawlid).

Consider this line from a Mawlid book: “O Prophet, surely there is no need for lamps in the house where you reside.”

However, lamps were indeed lit in the Prophet’s house. We cannot find any authentic hadith that claims lamps were never used. As evidence, they provide this report in their Mawlid books:

‘Aisha was sewing clothes before dawn (the time we eat Suhoor). The needle fell, and the lamp went out. At that moment, the Prophet ﷺ entered, and the house became illuminated. By the light of the Prophet, ‘Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) saw the needle. She laughed. Then the Prophet ﷺ said: “Woe to the one who does not see me on the Day of Resurrection.” ‘Aisha asked: “O Messenger of Allah, who is the one who will not see you?” The Prophet ﷺ replied: “The miser!” ‘Aisha asked: “Who is the miser?” The Prophet ﷺ replied: “The miser is the one who, upon hearing my name, does not send blessings (Salawat) upon me.”’

They also propagate a claim attributed to Hassan (may Allah be pleased with him): “When I looked at the light of the Prophet ﷺ, I covered both my eyes for fear of losing my sight.”

Another reason they give for the claim that the Prophet ﷺ had no shadow is that “it would prevent the disbelievers from stepping on his shadow, which would be an insult to the Prophet ﷺ!”

There is no doubt that the radiance of the Prophet’s face and his beauty were unparalleled. However, this does not mean his very nature was light. There is no scriptural support for this. None of these reports are authentic. In fact, what has come down through authentic chains of narration is that the Prophet ﷺ did have a shadow. If the concern is that disbelievers might step on his shadow, consider what the enemies did to the Prophet ﷺ! They placed camel entrails on him, he was injured at Uhud, his tooth was broken... are these not far more serious than stepping on a shadow?

It is noteworthy that even among the groups who argue that the Prophet ﷺ had no shadow, opposing voices have begun to emerge. The current shift in stance is that there were times when he did have a shadow. Another faction among them even labels this shift as a step toward disbelief (Kufr). In short, this “no-shadow” claim, which lacks scriptural support, continues to burn as a controversy.

They also believe that the Prophet ﷺ did not yawn, and that his excrement was not impure but rather possessed excellence. Sanad Malinal states: ‘A companion searched for the Prophet’s excrement for a long time, found it, and consumed it. The news reached the Prophet ﷺ, and he said: “Whoever’s stomach touches my stomach, Allah has forbidden him from the Hellfire.”’

This is a baseless, fabricated story.

Faith should not be formed on the basis of such false reports. Nor should one deny matters established by clear evidence. Observe how the Prophet ﷺ is insulted through lines written to glorify him:

The Prophet’s waste is pure, and so is his blood The earth swallows the Prophet’s waste, and from it, sweet basil grows

All of this is contrary to the scriptures. When the Prophet ﷺ went to relieve himself, Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) would go with water to help him clean himself. This is established in the Hadith. In other words, the Prophet ﷺ and the companions understood that his waste was impure. Why else would the Prophet ﷺ clean himself after relieving himself? All of this is excessive praise of the Prophet. The Prophet ﷺ himself forbade excessive praise. The Prophet ﷺ said:

“Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated in praising the son of Mary. I am only a slave of Allah, so call me the slave of Allah and His Messenger” (Bukhari).

Some misinterpret this hadith to justify excessive praise, claiming that the intention is only to praise him as the Christians praised Jesus (peace be upon him) by calling him God or the son of God. But that is not the reality.

One must not attribute any quality to the Prophet ﷺ that Allah and His Messenger have not taught. The Prophet did not give us permission to do so; rather, he forbade it. In Sahih al-Bukhari, we find: “Once, when the Prophet ﷺ entered a wedding gathering, the children said about him: ‘Among us is a Prophet who knows what will happen tomorrow.’ Hearing this, the Prophet ﷺ immediately said to stop it...”

Here, the children attributed a quality to the Prophet ﷺ that should not be attributed to any creation, including the Prophet ﷺ himself. The Prophet ﷺ immediately forbade it. But does the Prophet ﷺ have no special qualities? Is he just an ordinary human being like us?

The Prophet ﷺ has many special qualities. We can understand some aspects of this from the explanation of Sheikh Nasiruddin al-Albani:

“The Prophet ﷺ had special qualities, such as hearing the speech of Jibril (peace be upon him). Others did not see Jibril or hear his speech. This is established in Bukhari and other sources. The Prophet ﷺ once told ‘Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her): ‘Jibril has sent you greetings.’ She replied: ‘O Messenger of Allah, upon him be peace.’ The Prophet ﷺ saw what we do not see. These special qualities of the Prophet ﷺ are established by authentic evidence. They are not established by weak evidence, analogy, or personal opinion. There are two groups of people regarding this. One group denies many of his special qualities that are established by authentic chains of narration, claiming they are not Mutawatir (widely transmitted) or that they do not fit their intellect. The other group invents many baseless, unestablished things, such as that he was the first creation, that he had no shadow on earth, that his footprints would not appear on the ground, or that his footprints would be imprinted on any stone he stepped on. The middle path is to say the following:

Evidence from the Quran, Sunnah, and Ijma (consensus) confirms that the Prophet ﷺ was a human being. Therefore, it is not permissible to attribute any quality or characteristic to him without evidence from the Quran and Sunnah. If it is established, it is mandatory to accept it. It is not permissible to refute it with any philosophy, whether it be science or intellect. The sad part is that they accept what they like from the scriptures and reject what they dislike. It is also sad that some of these people are considered knowledgeable, and some have even earned high degrees in Sharia! Truly, we belong to Allah, and to Him we shall return. We seek refuge in Allah from the harm of both these groups who have crossed the limits” (Silsilat al-Sahihah).

Everything regarding the nature and character of the Prophets is perfect and free from defects. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani says in Fath al-Bari:

“Indeed, the Prophets are at the pinnacle of perfection in their creation and character. Whoever attributes a defect to a Prophet is harming him. Therefore, one should fear Kufr (disbelief) for anyone who does this” (Fath al-Bari).

One must not belittle the Prophets or question their nobility. Allah warns us against this. Allah says:

“O you who have believed, be not like those who annoyed Moses; then Allah cleared him of what they said. And he was, in the sight of Allah, distinguished” (33:69).

Allah saved Moses (peace be upon him) and the believers with him from Pharaoh, and drowned Pharaoh and his followers in the sea. Later, it was the Israelites who harassed Moses (peace be upon him) in various ways. The Quranic verse does not specify how the Israelites harassed him. There is no doubt that the authoritative explanation of the Quran is that of Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). The Prophet (peace be upon him) explained this matter. Imam Bukhari and Imam Muslim recorded it in their Sahihs:

Narrated Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: “The Children of Israel used to bathe naked, looking at each other. But Moses (peace be upon him) used to bathe alone. They said: ‘By Allah, nothing prevents Moses from bathing with us except that he has a scrotal hernia.’ Once, Moses (peace be upon him) went to bathe and placed his clothes on a stone. The stone ran away with his clothes. Moses (peace be upon him) ran after it, saying: ‘O stone, my clothes! My clothes!’ The Israelites saw him and said: ‘By Allah, Moses has no defect.’ The stone stopped so that they could see him, and he took his clothes. He then began to beat the stone. Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him) said: ‘By Allah, there were six or seven marks on the stone from the beating’” (Muslim). (There are those who mock or deny this hadith. There is nothing unbelievable in it. It was merely a measure Allah took to convince the people that he was free from the defect they accused him of. Did Allah not elevate his status through miracles when they previously called him a sorcerer?)

There are some groups who hold beliefs that attribute the qualities of Allah to the Prophets. This includes Christians who believe Jesus (peace be upon him) is God, the son of God, or one of the Trinity (Father, Son, and Holy Spirit). Observe what the Quran says about their claim and the warning given to them:

“They have certainly disbelieved who say, ‘Allah is the Messiah, the son of Mary’...” (5:72).

“They have certainly disbelieved who say, ‘Allah is the third of three’...” (5:73).

“And they say, ‘The Most Merciful has taken [for Himself] a son.’ You have done an atrocious thing” (19:88, 89).

None of the Prophets claimed that they should be worshipped or that they possessed any of Allah’s attributes. It is noteworthy that the Quran describes the Prophet (peace be upon him) himself answering Allah’s question on the Day of Resurrection:

“And [beware the Day] when Allah will say, ‘O Jesus, Son of Mary, did you say to the people, “Take me and my mother as deities besides Allah”?’ He will say, ‘Exalted are You! It was not for me to say that to which I have no right. If I had said it, You would have known it. You know what is within myself, and I do not know what is within Yourself. Indeed, it is You who is Knower of the unseen. I said not to them except what You commanded me - to worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord. And I was a witness over them as long as I was among them; but when You took me up, You were the Observer over them, and You are, over all things, Witness’” (5:116, 117).

We have understood that Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) himself forbade undeserved praise. Yet, there are those who attribute many of Allah’s qualities to the Prophet (peace be upon him). One can see people believing that the Prophet (peace be upon him) sees everything and is present everywhere, and thus they call upon him. Observe the claim of those who hold such un-Islamic beliefs: “The Prophet (peace be upon him) is the one who is constantly watching over us” (Sunnath Magazine).

Only Allah is the One who can see all and know all. This is the attribute they are attributing to the Prophet (peace be upon him). This same attribute is also given to many others, like Sheikh Jilani (may Allah have mercy on him), in various Mawlid books.
