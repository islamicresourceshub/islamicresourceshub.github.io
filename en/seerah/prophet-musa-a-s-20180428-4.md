Allah has mentioned the name of Prophet Musa (peace be upon him) in about 136 places in the Quran. Allah describes his history in detail in chapters such as Surah Al-Baqarah, Surah Al-A'raf, Surah Taha, and Surah Al-Qasas. In several other chapters, his life is mentioned or alluded to more briefly.

Musa (peace be upon him) was born as the son of Imran, descending from the lineage of Prophet Ya'qub (peace be upon him). Allah chose Musa (peace be upon him) for a people who had received many blessings from Him. They are known as the 'Children of Israel'.

In the Quran, we see Allah reminding the Children of Israel of the blessings He bestowed upon them multiple times:

"O Children of Israel, remember My favor that I have bestowed upon you and that I preferred you over the worlds" (Quran 2:47).

When it is said, "I preferred you over the worlds," it does not mean they were the most excellent of all humanity for all time; rather, it means they were the most favored among the people of that era. This is because, regarding the community of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, Allah has specifically stated that they are the best nation produced for mankind.

Musa (peace be upon him) was subjected to many hardships and trials. In Surah Taha, we see Allah specifically mentioning this to Prophet Musa (peace be upon him):

"...And We tried you with a great trial..." (20:40).

The kings of the Children of Israel were the Pharaohs, or Fir'awns. 'Fir'awn' is a title of office. These kings were tyrants and arrogant. Historians suggest that the king during the time of Prophet Musa (peace be upon him) was Ramses II. This particular Pharaoh, having reached the peak of arrogance, claimed to be the lord. It was to such a defiant ruler that Allah sent Prophet Musa (peace be upon him).

Even in our modern era, which is often celebrated as a time of democracy and progress, we are aware of the nepotism and oppression of the poor by rulers in many lands. If that is the case today, one can only imagine the nature of the dictatorial rule of the tyrant Pharaoh!

The original inhabitants of Egypt were the Copts. They were considered the ruling class and the elite.

'Israel' is another name for Prophet Ya'qub (peace be upon him). After Prophet Yusuf (peace be upon him) became a minister in Egypt, Ya'qub (peace be upon him) and his children settled there. In short, the Children of Israel were immigrants to Egypt. At that time, Egypt consisted of the native Copts and the immigrant Children of Israel. The latter were good people who followed the path of Ya'qub (peace be upon him) and Yusuf (peace be upon him).

However, later generations of the Children of Israel strayed from the path of Ya'qub (peace be upon him) and Yusuf (peace be upon him). Ideological deviation and immorality emerged among them. When they deviated from Tawhid—the oneness of God—as a punishment, their governance fell under the tyranny of the Copts. They were oppressed to the extreme, treated like slaves, and denied even the status afforded to animals.

We must also understand the warning given by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that if one turns away from true faith, they will be tested by tyrannical rulers.

Even today, we can see the great pyramids in Egypt. The size of their stones is astonishing. We might even doubt whether modern equipment could lift them. The pyramids were built with such massive stones. The Pharaohs used the Children of Israel to transport these stones to such heights. The Pharaohs truly displayed the arrogance of their power. The Children of Israel suffered brutal persecution at the hands of the Egyptian ruling class. The Copts did not show even the compassion one might show to a donkey carrying a load. It was during this time that the great Prophet Musa (peace be upon him) was born.

The Children of Israel increased in number in Egypt. This growth created fear among the Copts. They also began to feel jealous of their increasing numbers. The core of their fear was that if the number of Israelites grew, the foundation of their throne would be shaken. They calculated that if that happened, the rule of the Copts would be lost, and the Israelites would end up ruling Egypt.

Pharaoh summoned the nobles to his court. He informed them of the great problem they were facing. He proposed a solution and issued an order to implement it: kill all the newborn boys among the Children of Israel. This was the command issued by the tyrant Pharaoh. Allah describes the severe persecution the Children of Israel faced from Pharaoh:

"Ta-Sin-Mim. These are the verses of the clear Book. We recite to you from the news of Musa and Pharaoh in truth for a people who believe. Indeed, Pharaoh exalted himself in the land and made its people into factions, oppressing a sector among them, slaughtering their [newborn] sons and keeping their females alive. Indeed, he was of the corrupters" (Quran 28:1-4).

The extent of the extreme cruelty Pharaoh showed toward the Children of Israel is clear without further explanation.

History tells us that when this cruelty of keeping girls alive and slaughtering boys continued, there was some discussion among the Copts. Since all newborn boys were being killed and the adults were dying off one by one, naturally, there was no one left to do the slave labor. They discussed this to find a solution. They finally reached a decision: if they killed the boys born in one year, they would let the boys born in the following year live. Prophet Harun (peace be upon him) was born in the year they decided not to kill, so there was no problem regarding him. Musa (peace be upon him) was born in the year they decided to kill.

Tyrannical rulers cannot continue their ways forever. Allah sees, hears, and knows the pain of the oppressed, their aching hearts, and their minds longing for help. This dominance and injustice can only last until the time appointed by Allah. Afterward, Allah takes appropriate action.

Allah had already decided to liberate the Children of Israel from the oppression of Pharaoh and his people:

"And We wanted to confer favor upon those who were oppressed in the land and make them leaders and make them inheritors" (Quran 28:5).

Allah decided to show mercy and kindness to the oppressed, to end the tyranny of the Copts over them, and to make the Children of Israel the inheritors of their land.

"And [We wanted] to establish them in the land and show Pharaoh and [his minister] Haman and their soldiers through them that which they had feared" (Quran 28:6).

Pharaoh decided to slaughter all newborn boys because he feared that the Israelites would seize their power and influence. However, Allah's intention was to bring upon them exactly what they feared. Allah decided that the arrogant Pharaoh, his arrogant minister Haman, and their army would experience it.

We understand the decision made by Pharaoh and his people. What must have been the state of mind of the pregnant women of that time? Could there be any woman among the Children of Israel who did not fear losing her child if it were a boy? Never!

The mother of Musa (peace be upon him) became pregnant. She felt great fear and anxiety—the fear any mother would naturally feel for the future of her child.

"And We had already conferred favor upon you another time, when We inspired to your mother what We inspired, [saying], 'Cast him into the chest and cast it into the river, and the river will throw it onto the bank; there will take him an enemy to Me and an enemy to him.' And I bestowed upon you love from Me that you would be brought up under My eye" (Quran 20:37-39).

In this verse, Allah reminds Musa (peace be upon him) of a great favor He bestowed upon him. Even as an infant, he received Allah's special protection and help. Allah describes how this happened here.

Allah inspired the heart of Musa's (peace be upon him) mother to place the baby in a chest immediately after birth and then cast it into the river. It is not something a mother can do with courage—to place her own flesh and blood in a chest and cast it into a river. But Allah gave her the courage and the comfort that He would save the child.

There is no doubt that the king, Pharaoh, who arrogantly claimed to be the most high lord, was a polytheist and a disbeliever. Therefore, he was an enemy to Allah and to Musa (peace be upon him). Allah inspired the heart of Musa's (peace be upon him) mother that this very enemy would take the child, that he would be raised under His supervision, and that He had cast special love upon him.

When it is said that He inspired the mother, it is not the same as the revelation Allah gives to prophets. We remember that when we explained the matters of faith in prophets, we clarified that what Allah gives to prophets is distinct.

In Surah Al-Qasas, the inspiration Allah gave to the mother of Musa (peace be upon him) is described as follows:

"And We inspired to the mother of Musa, 'Suckle him; but when you fear for him, cast him into the river and do not fear and do not grieve. Indeed, We will return him to you and will make him [one] of the messengers'" (Quran 28:7).

The Holy Quran explains in one place what it summarized in another. It explains in detail in one place what it stated unconditionally in another. This is a characteristic of the Quran.

This verse is more detailed than what appeared in Surah Taha. Allah inspired the mother of Musa (peace be upon him) that she should nurse the baby, and if she feared that Pharaoh would kill him, she should place him in a chest and cast him into the river. Allah inspired her heart that she should not fear or grieve, that He would return him to her so that he would be raised by her own hands, and that He would later make him one of the messengers.

It is noteworthy that in this verse, Allah gives two commands (suckle him; if you fear for him, cast him into the river), two prohibitions (do not fear and do not grieve), and two glad tidings (indeed, We will return him to you and will make him one of the messengers).

The literary beauty and sentence structure of the Quran are a treasure trove of wonders. To this day, no one in the world has been able to accept the challenge of bringing something equal to the Quran, and no one ever will. This Quran is such a great divine sign.

The miracles given by Allah to previous prophets ceased with their time. This is because all the prophets before Muhammad ﷺ were sent only to specific communities or countries. However, Allah has sent Muhammad ﷺ to all people until the end of time. Therefore, the miracle Allah gave to Muhammad ﷺ must remain until the end of the world. That is the Holy Quran. The Quran will continue to unveil its miracle before the world so that all people on earth may recognize that Muhammad ﷺ is the messenger of Allah and the final prophet. (To be continued)
