Prophets and Trials

ഹുസൈന്‍ സലഫി, ഷാര്‍ജ · 2017-03-25 · Seerah & Biographies

Trials Faced by the Prophets

All the Prophets appointed by the Creator to guide humanity were subjected to various forms of trials. These experiences served only to strengthen their faith.

There is no Prophet who did not face trials.

  • Those who endured the persecution of enemies
  • Those who faced mockery and stoning
  • Those who had to leave their homes and lands
  • Those who were besieged
  • Those who waited until old age to be blessed with children...

Prophets faced numerous such trials. This is sufficient evidence for those who reflect that they were indeed human beings. Observe what the Holy Quran tells us about some of the trials experienced by the Prophets:

Imprisonment - Yusuf (AS)

He was imprisoned, innocent and without having committed any wrong.

''فَلَبِثَ فِي السِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ'' (12:42).

Expelled from the Land - Ibrahim (AS)

Because he proclaimed the faith he had embraced to the people, he was expelled from his home and his land, Iraq.

''وَقَالَ إِنِّي ذَاهِبٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّي سَيَهْدِينِ'' (37:99).

We are also familiar with the Prophet (PBUH) migrating from Makkah to Madinah.

Many Prophets Were Killed

''أَفَكُلَّمَا جَاءَكُمْ رَسُولٌ بِمَا لَا تَهْوَىٰ أَنفُسُكُمُ اسْتَكْبَرْتُمْ فَفَرِيقًا كَذَّبْتُمْ وَفَرِيقًا تَقْتُلُونَ'' (2:87).

Those Who Suffered Illness

Observe what the Quran says about the illness of Prophet Ayyub (AS):

''وَأَيُّوبَ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُ أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ الضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ * فَاسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُ فَكَشَفْنَا مَا بِهِ مِن ضُرٍّ ۖ وَآتَيْنَاهُ أَهْلَهُ وَمِثْلَهُم مَّعَهُمْ رَحْمَةً مِّنْ عِندِنَا وَذِكْرَىٰ لِلْعَابِدِينَ'' (21:84,85).

How Much Was the Prophet (PBUH) Tested?

How much was the Prophet (PBUH) tested? When an arrow struck him at Uhud, blood flowed; he fell into the traps set by his enemies. Due to poverty, he often had to survive on only dates and water to satisfy his hunger. He was subjected to severe illnesses. The intensity of the fever that afflicted the Prophet (PBUH) can be seen in this Hadith:

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (RA) entered upon the Prophet (PBUH) while he was suffering from a fever. He placed his hand on the Prophet (PBUH) and felt the heat even through the blanket. He asked, "O Messenger of Allah, how severe your fever is!" The Prophet (PBUH) replied, "We (the Prophets) are like this. Our trials are doubled, and our rewards are also doubled." (Ibn Majah).

Through this, the Prophet (PBUH) teaches us that being a Prophet does not mean being exempt from trials; rather, they face the most severe of trials.

From Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (RA), he said:

"The Prophet (PBUH) was asked who among the people faces the most severe trials. The Prophet (PBUH) said: 'The Prophets, then those like them, then those like them. Each person is tested according to their level of faith. If a person is firm in their faith, their trials increase in intensity. If they are weak in their faith, the intensity of their trials is lightened. A servant will continue to be tested until they walk upon the earth free of any sins.'" (Tirmidhi)

The Prophets Were Perfect Human Beings

Prophets were indeed perfect human beings. They were people who ate food. They worked for their livelihood. When Muhammad (PBUH) was a young man, he was an honest merchant. Similarly, the Prophet (PBUH) also tended sheep.

They asked the Prophet (PBUH): 'Did you tend sheep?' The Prophet (PBUH) asked: 'Is there any Prophet who has not tended sheep?' (Bukhari, Muslim).

From this Hadith, we understand that all Prophets tended sheep. Musa (AS) tended sheep for many years. When he arrived in Madyan, the dowry he agreed upon to marry the daughter of an elder there was to tend sheep.

When Allah asked Musa (AS) about the staff in his hand, his reply was: "...I beat down leaves with it for my sheep..." (20:18).

Regarding the wisdom behind all Prophets tending sheep, Ibn Hajar (RA) states in Fath al-Bari while explaining the rationale: "It is to instill humility in them, to provide solitude for their hearts, and to prepare them with the capacity to lead people."

Dawud (AS) was a Prophet who crafted armor to defend against attacks from enemies on the battlefield. "And We taught him the making of coats of mail for you, to protect you from your violence (in war). Will you then be grateful?" (21:80).

It is mentioned in a Hadith (Muslim) that Prophet Zakariyya (AS) worked as a carpenter. If the Prophets were people who earned their living through such labor, this informs us that they were like us—possessing hunger, thirst, joy, sorrow, pain, and emotions. (To be continued)
