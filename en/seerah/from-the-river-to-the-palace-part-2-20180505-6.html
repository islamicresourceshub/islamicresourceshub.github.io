From the River to the Palace

ഹുസൈന്‍ സലഫി, ഷാര്‍ജ · 2018-05-05 · Seerah & Biographies

(Prophet Musa (peace be upon him): 2)

"Then the family of Pharaoh picked him up (from the river), so that he would become an enemy and a source of grief for them. Indeed, Pharaoh, Haman, and their armies were sinners" (Quran 28:8).

The Cruelty of Pharaoh and the Salvation of Musa (peace be upon him)

It was out of fear of losing their power that Pharaoh, Haman, and their soldiers decided to slaughter all the newborn boys of the Bani Isra'il. Many infants were murdered, and many parents were left in tears. Although that cruel king did all of this, only the decree of the King of kings—the Almighty—would come to pass. Musa, a boy born to the Bani Isra'il, grew up in the palace of the cruel ruler Pharaoh, living off his wealth and enjoying the comforts of his court. The very thing they feared was destined to happen through this child. They were raising him in the palace for that very purpose, all while remaining completely unaware of the truth.

Following the inspiration from Allah, the mother placed the baby in a chest and set it adrift on the river. The chest floated until it reached the front of Pharaoh's palace. The queen noticed it, retrieved it, and brought it inside. When they opened the chest, they found a beautiful baby boy! The queen felt a sense of affection for the child. However, Pharaoh, the king, felt it would be better to kill him. From Pharaoh's words, his wife understood that he suspected the child might be from the Bani Isra'il and not from their own people, the Copts, and that he intended to have him killed. Immediately, Pharaoh's wife said:

"...He will be a comfort to the eye for me and for you. Do not kill him; perhaps he may benefit us, or we may adopt him as a son. And they perceived not" (Quran 28:9).

Thus, Musa (peace be upon him) grew up in the very palace of the king who was killing the newborn boys of the Israelites. This is just one of the clear examples that no matter what agendas people plan, only the decree of Allah will be fulfilled.

When Pharaoh decided to kill him, his wife opposed it. Respecting her wishes, Pharaoh gave permission to spare his life and raise him in the palace. Little did they know what was destined to happen through Musa (peace be upon him)!

This is the significant stage of Musa (peace be upon him)'s infancy as described in Surah al-Qasas. It was his mother who set him adrift on the river. Could that mother ever find peace after such an act?

The Faith and Patience of the Mother

"And the heart of the mother of Musa became empty (of all other thoughts). She was about to disclose his case, had We not strengthened her heart, so that she would be of the believers" (Quran 28:10).

Fearing that the king would kill her flesh and blood, she had placed him in a chest and set him on the river, as inspired by Allah. She had no information about the child's condition. She did not know where her precious son was. Filled with anxiety, that mother could think of nothing but her child.

Allah granted her heart great steadfastness. She did not act impatiently or do anything that would cause Allah's displeasure.

If Allah had not granted her heart this assurance, what would have happened? She would have rushed out of the house in a panic, searching for the child. The whole land would have learned that a child born to the Bani Isra'il had been set adrift on the river. If they had found the child, they would have killed him.

Anyone who possesses Tawhid—the belief in the oneness of Allah—will never despair of Allah's decree in any situation. They will remain patient with His decree, place their trust in Him, and seek help from Him alone. Because Musa (peace be upon him)'s mother had unwavering faith in Allah, she was able to remain patient with His decree. This is something only those with Tawhid can do. Those who expect benefit or harm from others besides Allah through supernatural means will never attain this peace of mind.

The Sister's Mission and the Miracle of the Child

Musa (peace be upon him) had an older sister. The mother said to her:

"...Follow him. So she watched him from a distance, and they did not perceive" (Quran 28:11).

Following her mother's command, she left the house in search of the child. She saw people gathered around a baby, talking about him. Those standing around the child did not realize she was his sister. Musa (peace be upon him)'s sister approached the child. At that moment, the baby was crying uncontrollably due to thirst and hunger. He was a nursing infant, after all, and needed breast milk. Many women known to them were brought in to nurse him. They all tried to feed the child, but he refused to drink from any of them. Even those in the palace and the local people tried, but the child would not accept the milk.

"And We had forbidden him from wet nurses before, so she (the sister) said: Shall I direct you to a household that will rear him for you, and they will be sincere to him?" (Quran 28:12).

Despite his hunger and thirst, the child would not drink from any of the women who came to nurse him. This was a miracle ordained by Allah. It meant that Allah prevented the child from drinking the milk of any woman other than his biological mother.

When the people heard the words of Musa (peace be upon him)'s sister, it brought great joy to everyone there. They accepted her suggestion.

Based on Allah's magnificent decree and wisdom, Musa (peace be upon him) was returning to the lap of his own mother. Allahu Akbar. It is clear that no matter what the creation decides to implement, it is impossible to override the decree of Allah.

When Allah inspired Musa (peace be upon him)'s mother to put the child in a chest and set him on the river, He had already promised, "Indeed, We will return him to you." Allah was now fulfilling that promise.

"So We restored him to his mother that she might be comforted and not grieve and that she would know that the promise of Allah is true. But most of the people do not know" (Quran 28:13).

Musa (peace be upon him) Returns to His Mother's Lap

Following the magnificent decree of Allah, which is free from any error or flaw, the mother received her child back.

There were specific reasons for returning the child to his mother:

  • To bring comfort to the mother's eyes
  • To remove her grief
  • To let her know that the promise of Allah is true

The Depth of Allah's Decree

Allah implements His decree through paths that we cannot fathom or imagine. When Musa (peace be upon him)'s mother placed the infant in a chest and set him on the river, she could never have known that this would be the outcome.

We often seek help from Allah for many things. When we ask how a matter will be fulfilled, no servant knows the answer. It is only when the matter is fulfilled that the humble servant realizes it. This is how Allah's interventions work. The belief that any created being can intervene in the unseen or through supernatural means is Shirk (associating partners with Allah). This is because only the Creator can intervene through the unseen; no created being has that power.

We decide many things. We often avoid certain things due to difficulty or distress. We think that what we have chosen is best. However, sometimes what we see as not good is beneficial for us, and what we see as good is harmful to us. Only Allah knows the pros and cons of every matter perfectly.

Upbringing and Nursing in the Palace

Musa (peace be upon him) lived in Pharaoh's palace for some time. We do not find anything in the Quran or Hadith that confirms exactly how long he stayed there. However, we understand from what Pharaoh says to Musa (peace be upon him) later on that he spent many years there.

Those in the palace had no idea about the woman nursing Musa (peace be upon him). She was known among them as Musa's mother. Although they called her that in the sense of a foster mother, she was not merely a foster mother, was she?

She held the great position of nursing and raising the child who was growing up in the palace. She must have received many worldly benefits from the royal palace for this.

The Growth and Wisdom of Musa (peace be upon him)

Musa (peace be upon him) grew up in the palace. Allah granted him wisdom and knowledge to make decisions.

"And when he attained his full strength and was mature, We gave him judgment and knowledge. And thus do We reward the doers of good" (Quran 28:14).

It is important to understand that Musa (peace be upon him) had not yet become a Prophet at this time. One might wonder what it means when it says Allah gave him wisdom and knowledge. Imam Mujahid (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"This refers to the knowledge, intelligence, and actions before Prophethood."

Ibn Ishaq (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"Knowledge of his religion and the religion of his forefathers, knowledge of religious matters, their laws, and their punishments" (Tabari).

From the explanations of the great scholars, we understand that the statement about Him giving him wisdom and knowledge refers to the time before Prophethood. However, there are also scholars who have expressed the opinion that it refers to Prophethood itself. (To be continued)
