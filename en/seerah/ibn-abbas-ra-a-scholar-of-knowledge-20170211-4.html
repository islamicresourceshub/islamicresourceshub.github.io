Ibn Abbas (ra): A Scholar Who Embodied Knowledge in His Life

അര്‍ഷദ് താനൂര്‍ · 2017-02-11 · Seerah & Biographies

A Fountainhead of Knowledge

The companion Ibn Abbas (ra) was a true fountainhead of knowledge. As the cousin of the Prophet (saw), the importance he placed on learning is truly astonishing. Let us take a glimpse into his history.

"When I saw him, I realized he was the most handsome of people. When he spoke, his speech convinced me that he was the most eloquent. And when I spoke with him, I recognized that he was the most knowledgeable person," said the senior Tabi'i scholar Masruq ibn al-Ajda' (ra) regarding Ibn Abbas (ra).

Ibn Abbas (ra) was born three years before the Hijra. He was a boy of only thirteen when the Prophet (saw) passed away. Yet, in a short span of time, he became a beacon of light for the Muslim world.

Allah says: "He gives wisdom to whom He wills, and whoever has been given wisdom has certainly been given much good" (2:269).

Ibn Abbas (ra) was a marvel in whom all branches of knowledge converged. People would gather in front of his house early in the morning. After performing wudu, he would come out to them.

  • First was the turn of those who came to learn the Quran. He would call them, and his house would be filled with students.
  • Then followed the study of Quranic exegesis.
  • After that, jurisprudence.
  • Then, the laws of inheritance.
  • Finally, the study of the Arabic language, where he would discuss obscure expressions and poetry.

His teaching would continue until evening. While the students changed, the one imparting knowledge remained the same Ibn Abbas (ra), for whom the Prophet (saw) had prayed, placing his hand on his head and saying, "O Allah, grant him deep understanding of the religion."

Humility is an Ornament

As knowledge increases, a believer must become more humble. Seeking knowledge should not be for the sake of arguing with the ignorant or competing with scholars.

Ibn Abbas (ra) was always a humble student. Having grown up under the guidance of the Prophet (saw), he possessed all the qualities of an ideal student. Once, Ibn Abbas (ra) heard a hadith. Without delay, he set out for the house of Zayd ibn Thabit (ra), who had narrated it. However, he found him taking a midday nap. Ibn Abbas (ra) did not wake him up and instead sat outside.

The sun was blazing in the sky, and a hot wind was blowing outside the house. Had he called out to Zayd (ra), he would have gladly invited the Prophet's cousin inside. But the humble Ibn Abbas (ra) decided to spread his cloak on the scorching desert sand and use his hand as a pillow, waiting for him to wake up. When Zayd (ra) woke up and opened the door, he was astonished to find the Prophet's cousin sleeping there with his clothes covered in dust! He said to Ibn Abbas (ra), "O cousin of the Messenger of Allah! Why did you come here? If you had sent someone, I would have come to you!"

Ibn Abbas (ra) replied, "I am the one in need. Knowledge does not come to us; we must go to knowledge." He then asked him about the hadith and learned it.

Scholars Must Be Respected

Knowledge is the inheritance of the prophets. Whoever acquires it has received a noble share. Just as there is a difference between the Prophet and the lowest of the companions, there is a difference between the learned and the ignorant. Therefore, those who possess knowledge must be honored and respected.

Zayd ibn Thabit (ra) was a scribe of revelation for the Prophet (saw). A leader among the people of Medina, he was a scholar well-versed in jurisprudence, the rules of Quranic recitation, and the laws of inheritance. Once, as he prepared to mount his camel, he was surprised to see Ibn Abbas (ra) holding the reins and walking like a servant. He said to Ibn Abbas (ra), "O cousin of the Prophet! What are you doing? Please, let go of the reins." Ibn Abbas (ra) replied with a smile, "This is how we have been commanded to treat our scholars."

Knowledge is Not Confined to Words

Ibn Abbas (ra) demonstrated knowledge through his life. He had the same enthusiasm for putting goodness into practice as he did for commanding it. He was extremely vigilant in ensuring that evil did not find a place in his life.

He enriched his days with fasting and found time in the silence of the night to prostrate before the Lord of the worlds. Abdullah ibn Mulaykah (ra) said, "Once, I traveled with Ibn Abbas (ra) from Mecca to Medina. We camped somewhere in the middle of the night. Due to the fatigue of the journey, we fell asleep quickly. When half the night had passed, I woke up. There was Ibn Abbas (ra), reciting Surah Qaf."

When he reached the verse, "And the agony of death will bring the truth; that is what you were trying to avoid," he began to weep. He repeated that verse until dawn.

He departed from this world at the age of 71. Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyyah, the son of Ali (ra), led his funeral prayer.
