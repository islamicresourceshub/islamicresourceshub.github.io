Faith is the Foundation of Islam

മൂസ സ്വലാഹി കാര · 2017-01-28 · Aqeedah & Belief

Islam is a religion that teaches lessons, both small and great, based on the foundation of clear faith. The truth that every deed in Islam is backed by firm belief is what distinguishes it completely from false ideologies. Allah informs us that by internalizing this fact, one becomes a true believer:

"O you who have believed, believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Book that He sent down upon His Messenger and the Scripture which He sent down before. And whoever disbelieves in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, and the Last Day has certainly gone far astray" (4:136).

In his explanation of this, Sheikh Nasir as-Sa'di (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"This verse commands us to rectify matters of faith—including sincerity, truthfulness, abandoning evils, and repenting from sins—and to understand the meaning of matters supported by evidence and to believe in them" (Tafsir as-Sa'di, page 125).

Since faith is the foundation of Islam, let us highlight some essential points regarding it.

1. Faith Must Be Free from Doubt

Since the matters to be believed in Islam are free from doubt, the one who believes in them must also be free from doubt. The most important of these matters is belief in Allah and His Messenger (peace be upon him). Allah says:

"The believers are only the ones who have believed in Allah and His Messenger and then doubt not but strive with their properties and their lives in the cause of Allah. It is those who are the truthful" (49:15).

In his explanation, Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"They (the believers) must be firm on the path of truth without doubting or going astray" (Ibn Kathir: 4/1762).

From Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that I am the Messenger of Allah. No one will meet Allah with these two, without any doubt in them, but will enter Paradise" (Muslim).

It is the work of the devil to lead people away from faith. He crafts many schemes for this purpose. Islam has also taught the way to seek refuge from satanic harm. Allah says:

"And say, 'My Lord, I seek refuge in You from the incitements of the devils, and I seek refuge in You, my Lord, lest they be present with me'" (23:97-98).

From Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"The devil comes to each one of you and asks, 'Who created this? Who created that?' until he asks, 'Who created your Lord?' When he reaches that point, let him (the human) seek refuge in Allah and stop" (Muslim).

2. Faith is the Criterion for the Acceptance of Deeds

Acquiring righteous deeds is the only path to success in the Hereafter. Any error in this is an irreparable loss. Allah says:

"But they who believe and do righteous deeds - We will surely assign to them of Paradise [lofty] chambers beneath which rivers flow, wherein they abide eternally. Excellent is the reward of the [righteous] workers" (29:58).

However, if someone acts with a heart where faith has leaked away, Allah will scatter their deeds in the Hereafter. Allah says:

"And when they are thrown into a narrow place therein, bound in chains, they will cry out thereupon for destruction" (25:13).

In his explanation, Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"This is because they lost the religious conditions of sincerity and following the Prophet. Any action without these two is futile" (Ibn Kathir 3/416).

From Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"Allah, the Exalted and Blessed, said: 'I am the most self-sufficient of partners from any shirk (associating partners with Allah). Whoever performs a deed while associating someone else with Me, I will abandon him and his association'" (Muslim/2985).

This means that evil traits such as shirk, innovation, lying, hypocrisy, and mocking religion will not be found in those who have tasted the sweetness of faith.

3. The Prophets Were Those Who Maintained Faith

All the Prophets were those who cared for and guarded faith the best. Their followers followed the same path. It was for the sole reason of holding firm to their faith that they had to endure persecution. Allah says:

"And they resented them not except because they believed in Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Praiseworthy" (85:8).

"And you do not resent us except because we believed in the signs of our Lord when they came to us. Our Lord, pour upon us patience and let us die as Muslims [in submission to You]" (7:126).

It is Allah who favored us with faith. If we fulfill the obligation of living according to it, we will remain in the light and peace that comes from Him. If we disregard it, we will be in clear misguidance. Allah says:

"So is one whose breast Allah has expanded to [accept] Islam and he is upon a light from his Lord [like one whose heart is hardened]? Then woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of Allah. Those are in clear error" (39:22).

4. Deviation in Faith Leads to Errors

Allah says:

"And this is the path of your Lord, [leading] to a straight path. We have detailed the verses for a people who remember" (6:126).

The main advice the Prophet (peace be upon him) gave to the Companions was to hold fast to this path. Allah says:

"And, [moreover], this is My path, which is straight, so follow it; and do not follow [other] ways, for you will be separated from His way. This has He instructed you that you may become righteous" (6:153).

Explaining this, Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"From Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him): 'Allah teaches in this verse that believers should remain united and that factionalism and division are not allowed, while also informing us that unnecessary arguments in religious matters destroyed those who came before'" (Ibn Kathir 2/256).

Those who stand firm on this righteous path are called Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah. There is no doubt that without faith-consciousness, commitment to the sources, and dedication to deeds, one will deviate from this. Many such deviant groups have emerged in society. The list is long, including the Khawarij, Qadariyyah, Rafidah, Jabriyyah, Mu'tazilah, Shi'a, and Sufis.

Even today, grave-worshippers, followers of tariqahs, political Islamists, extremists, and those who reject the authentic sources exist in the midst of society as descendants of such groups. Instead of sitting in despair, believers should understand the gravity of the disease that has afflicted them and seek to cure it; they should take heed of the warning of the Quran. Allah says:

"[Or] of those who have divided their religion and become sects, every faction rejoicing in what it has" (30:32).

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"Except for one group in this community, all others will be in error due to the differences among them. That group is the Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, who hold fast to the Book of Allah, the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace be upon him), and the path of the best generations and the scholars, both ancient and modern" (Ibn Kathir 3/570).

From Umayr ibn al-Hani (may Allah be pleased with him): Mu'awiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) said from the pulpit:

"I heard the Prophet (peace be upon him) say: 'A group of my community will remain standing by the command of Allah. Those who abandon them or oppose them will not harm them. They will be victorious over the people until the command of Allah comes'" (Muslim: 5064).

5. Faith Provides Certainty in the Sources

The main sources of Islam are the Quran and the Hadith, which are its explanation. We must find remedies for problems that arise in religious matters from these. Allah says:

"O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result" (4:59).

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"This is a command that if people have problems regarding fundamental or branch matters of religion, they must refer them back to the Quran and the Sunnah. One who does not accept judgment from them cannot be a believer in Allah and the Hereafter."

The sources do not invite us to things that paralyze us; rather, they invite us to matters that provide awakening. Allah says:

"O you who have believed, respond to Allah and to the Messenger when he calls you to that which gives you life. And know that Allah intervenes between a man and his heart and that to Him you will be gathered" (8:24).

Not accepting what is established through the sources and turning away from them is a grave error. Those who do so face a life of misguidance and the punishment of Hell. Allah says:

"It is not for a believing man or a believing woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decided a matter, that they should [thereafter] have any choice about their affair. And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly strayed into clear error" (33:36).

No one whose heart is firm in faith can embrace the religion while excluding the Quran and Sunnah. They will always be those who respond to the call of Allah and His Messenger. Allah says:

"The only statement of the [true] believers when they are called to Allah and His Messenger to judge between them is that they say, 'We hear and we obey.' And those are the successful" (24:51).

As faith-decay, ideological distortions, and turning away from the sources increasingly gnaw at society, we must strive hard to know the value of the guidance Allah has given us and to protect it.
