# Who Has Deviated in Ideology?

**Moosa Sulaiman Kara**
**☎ : 97473 45768**

> The Creator of the universe is One, and there is none worthy of worship besides Him. This is the fundamental basis of Islam. It is essential for every Muslim to understand and act upon the fact that prayer and all other acts of worship must be directed solely to that Creator. Such individuals are those who have embraced Tawheed and do not engage in actions that contradict it.

I came across an article in the June 16-30, 2026 issue of 'Sunni Mela' titled 'Salafi Reformists Drowning in Shirk.' It is necessary to expose the fallacies presented by a certain Musliyar (religious scholar) in that article, who attempted to establish that the Mujahids, who stand firm on the path of Ahlus-Sunnah, are tainted by Shirk, while claiming that their own path is free from any such corruption.

### Criticism

The real roots of the division lie with those scholars who formed organizations to lead society by invoking the dead, seeking direct help from them, and using their 'Haq' (right), 'Jah' (status), and 'Barakah' (blessing) as intermediaries! Historically, the very birth of the Samastha organization was the first act of discord. They claim that they do not associate partners with Allah and do not make anything an 'Ilah' (object of worship), yet they encourage people to dedicate acts of worship to others besides God.

## Mainstream Muslims and Other Currents

Observe the assessment made by the Musliyar regarding the major Muslim organizations operating within society: "Come to Kerala. There are mainly three currents existing here regarding Tawheed: Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah (mainstream Muslims), Salafis, and Jamaat-e-Islami. Among these, the Salafis themselves have three currents. They even declare each other to be Mushriks (polytheists) and invite other groups to their own version of Tawheed" (page 12).

Those who claim to be the spokespersons of Ahlus-Sunnah are actually followers of the misguided beliefs and rituals of the Shia and Sufi sects! The Shias label everyone who does not stand with them as disbelievers. Similarly, the Samastha followers present those who do not walk with them as misguided and deniers of religion. It is ironic that those who are divided into various factions under different organizational names, despite sharing the same core ideology, are the ones accusing others of having different currents! 

While evidence of their own internal accusations of misguidance and denial of religion is rampant even on social media, they find satisfaction in criticizing others.

Why were the Samastha followers described as 'mainstream Muslims'? Perhaps it is because of their 'large numbers.' If so, understand one thing: throughout history, the followers of truth have always been a minority, while the proponents of falsehood have been the majority! History teaches us this. The Prophet's teachings indicate that there will always be a small group standing on the path of truth, not a majority.

Allah says: "Or do you think that most of them hear or reason? They are not except like livestock. Rather, they are [even] more astray in [their] way" (25:44).

Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) says: "Their condition is worse than that of grazing livestock. Because those livestock know what they were created for. But these people were created to worship Allah alone without any partners. Even after proofs were established for them and messengers were sent to them, they associate partners with Allah."

## The Questions of Prophet Muhammad

That the questions asked by Prophet Muhammad are intertwined with concepts of Shirk, and the fact that the Prophet did not ask with doubt or fear, is the conclusion of some Muslims' questions in history:

> "When interpreting the 'Ilah' mentioned in what Muhammad brought, if you are in doubt, it is sufficient to ask those who read the scripture before you regarding this matter. Then why is there a problem?" (Quran 12).

If one studies more about the One above, this question arises: "...a just law that is with us..." (13:14).

## I Am the One Above

To those who try to distort the face of Islam by accepting individuals who have lost their mental balance and claim, 'I am the one above' or 'I am the one who controls the world,' when they ask 'where is the problem,' the appropriate answer is that the problem lies in the manipulation of ideology by the Sunnis themselves.

Muslims ask again: "Why do they only speak of the abilities given to the Jinn and Angels, and the greatness of the saints, as if they are the ones above?" (Quran 15).

There is no instruction in any scripture that allows interference in the power of the One Above. Since all power and ability belong only to the One Above, He is the only one we should seek help from in any creative matter.

They have not been taught anywhere to rewrite the system established by the One Above for Jinn and Angels, or to harbor beliefs contrary to it. They cannot elevate false revelations to the position of the One Above or make them equal in His names and attributes. Yet, the belief that they are incapable and should not be believed is also propagated. Understanding that this belief, which belongs to the realm of knowledge and intellectual discourse, is not accepted by some is enough to realize the fault.

> "And among the people are those who take other than Allah as equals [to Him]. They love them as they [should] love Allah. But those who believe are stronger in love for Allah. And if only those who have wronged would consider [that] when they see the punishment, [they will realize] that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is severe in punishment" (2:165).

Another question from the Musliyar is: "If we greet the dead while standing by their graves and they hear us, what is the irrationality in asking them to do things for us?" (page 17).

The Musliyar lacks the fundamental knowledge that Allah has not appointed those who have reached the afterlife as intermediaries for those in this world. The etiquette for visiting the graves is to greet them and pray for them. Hearing and responding are matters related to the life of the Barzakh (the barrier between life and death). There is no evidence that one can speak to them. To date, no scholar of Ahlus-Sunnah has been able to point to an Imam who explained, in light of these Hadiths, that the dead can hear our complaints and provide solutions! It is irrelevant for the Musliyars, who lead people to shrines to ask for things that have never been achieved by the hands of creatures, to ask 'what is the irrationality in asking them.' Allah says: "So rely upon Allah; indeed, you are upon the clear truth. Indeed, you will not make the dead hear, nor will you make the deaf hear the call when they have turned their backs retreating" (27:79,80). Only the Samastha followers could believe that others can do what the Prophet (peace be upon him) could not.

Let the Musliyar ask: "It is only the application of abilities given by Allah that happens in the case of the great ones. If the argument is that they are like Allah, wouldn't one have to say that Allah's abilities are also received from someone else? Isn't this belittling Allah and associating Him with His creatures?" (pages 17, 18).

This question from the Musliyar undermines the root of Samastha's belief. This leap from the long-held concept that Allah has graciously given the saints the ability to do anything, to 'abilities given by Allah,' if strengthened, might lead them to the ideology of Ahlus-Sunnah that Allah never gave His power to anyone. The Musliyars who share Allah's attributes with others are the ones who truly insult Allah. If the Musliyar had grasped the verse, "There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing" (42:1), he would not have raised such trivial questions.

# Shirk-Drenched Salafi Tawheed

**Dr. Faisal Ahsani Randathani**

If all Muslims globally are not under the label of monotheism, then the entire Muslim community is not worshipping Allah. The word 'Tawheed' (monotheism), which all Muslims accept as the word of Allah, is what the Prophet Muhammad taught. Yet, how do differences of opinion arise among them regarding Tawheed?

The basis of this discord is the difference of opinion in interpreting the word 'Ilah' in the declaration of Tawheed. Everyone gives the meaning that there is no object of worship other than Allah. Then where is the problem? The problem is that the definition of worship is not clear among Muslims. This definition is accepted by all. But the problems begin with what is meant by 'worship.'

Come to Kerala. There are mainly three currents existing here regarding Tawheed: Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah (mainstream Muslims), Salafis, and Jamaat-e-Islami. Among these, the Salafis themselves have three currents. They even declare each other to be Mushriks and invite other groups to their own version of Tawheed.
