Celebrating the Prophet's Birthday is Indeed Un-Islamic

Adil Nizamuddin (Darul Arqam Al-Hind, Thiruvananthapuram) 📞 : 9656656522

"Whoever introduces anything new into this matter of ours (religion) that is not part of it, it will be rejected" (Bukhari). "Whoever performs an action that is not in accordance with our command, it will be rejected" (Muslim).

Every act of worship and celebration in Islam was defined by Allah through His Messenger. However, there are many innovations currently present in the community that do not belong to it. One such example is the celebration of the Prophet's birthday. The festivities held under the name «Milad-un-Nabi» have no backing from Islamic sources. The Prophet (peace be upon him) did not celebrate such an occasion in his lifetime, nor did his companions. The Tabi'un, who were the successors of the companions, and those who followed them, did not celebrate Milad-un-Nabi. The four renowned Imams were also unfamiliar with it. How, then, can it become a grand celebration?

It is Allah, who sent the Prophet (peace be upon him) as a mercy to the worlds and as the final messenger, who holds the right to permit the celebration of his birth. It is impossible to find such a command in the religious texts. The Prophet (peace be upon him), who lived for twenty-three years after receiving prophethood, did not set an example for this himself. It is clear that in a religion perfected by the pleasure and grace of Allah, there is no justification for this celebration.

After the best of generations, some people introduced innovations in the name of religion for material interests. We must seriously think and examine whether these celebrations, held in the name of love for the Prophet, are in line with the spirit of Islam. We must also reject them.

Only Two Celebrations

According to Islamic sources, Muslims are permitted to celebrate only two Eids: Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.

When the Prophet (peace be upon him) arrived in Madinah, he inquired about the days the people there used to celebrate and declared that Allah had provided them with two better Eids instead. Creating a third Eid under the name «Eid Milad-un-Nabi» is adding something new to the religion.

The Companions and the Prophet's Family Did Not Set an Example

Those who loved the Prophet (peace be upon him) the most were the companions like Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him), Umar (may Allah be pleased with him), Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him), and Ali (may Allah be pleased with him), as well as the Ahl al-Bayt, including Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with her). Many of them lived for decades after the passing of the Prophet. Yet, none of them turned the day of the Prophet's birth into an occasion for celebration.

The companions were those who tried to walk in the footsteps of the Prophet (peace be upon him). Some of them even showed the courage to shield him with their own bodies from arrows aimed at him. Who can claim to have greater love for the Prophet than they did? If celebrating the Prophet's birthday were a virtuous deed, would they have avoided it?

Sunnah and Bid'ah

True love for the Prophet is «Ittiba»—that is, embodying the examples (Sunnah) he showed us in our daily lives. Allah says: "Say, (O Muhammad), if you should love Allah, then follow me, (so) Allah will love you and forgive you your sins. And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful" (Quran 3:31).

Imam Bukhari and Muslim narrate from Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah, and whoever disobeys me has disobeyed Allah."

We see a scenario where money and time are spent on Milad celebrations not taught by the Prophet, while his Sunnahs are neglected.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) gave a strong warning against introducing new things into the religion:

It is narrated from Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever introduces anything new into this matter of ours (religion) that is not part of it, it will be rejected" (Bukhari: 2697).

"Whoever performs an action that is not in accordance with our command, it will be rejected" (Muslim: 1718).

Allah says: "And whatever the Messenger has given you - take; and what he has forbidden you - refrain from" (Quran 59:7).

"O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best (way) and best in result" (4:59).

In the explanation of verse 4:59, the Mufassirs (commentators) have stated that referring to Allah means referring to the Book of Allah, and referring to the Prophet (peace be upon him) means referring to his Sunnah after his passing.

In the matter of the disagreement over the celebration of the Prophet's birthday, we must also return to the Quran and the Sunnah. Returning to them will provide a solution to the division.
