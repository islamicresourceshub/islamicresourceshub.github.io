Duties and Rights

മുസാഫിര്‍ · 2018-05-05 · Fiqh

Legal Wrongs

Violating a legal right or neglecting a legal duty constitutes a legal wrong. Failing to repay a borrowed sum by the agreed-upon deadline is a legal wrong. Driving recklessly on public roads in a way that endangers others is a legal wrong because it is an offense prohibited by law. What about stabbing someone and causing injury? It goes without saying that this is a legal wrong. Theft, encroaching on someone else's land, selling milk diluted with water, and accepting bribes are all legal wrongs. These are all offenses. It is easy to see that these various small and large wrongs and offenses, along with their remedies, are the primary subjects of law.

Civil Court

If we carefully examine the examples mentioned above, we can see that they fall into two categories of wrongs. Failing to repay a loan is a wrong, but if the lender has no grievance, there is no remedy sought. Conversely, if the lender has a complaint, they can approach the civil court. If evidence is presented, the court will rule against the borrower to repay the money, including interest. If the borrower refuses to comply with the verdict, the court will auction their property to recover the amount for the lender.

Criminal Court

Matters of theft and murder are different. Even if the victim—or in the case of murder, the heirs—does not have a complaint, action will be taken against the perpetrator.

  • The police will register a case.
  • An investigation will be conducted.
  • Evidence will be collected.
  • The matter will be brought to the criminal court.
  • A trial will take place in court.
  • If proven guilty, the perpetrator will be punished.

In all of this, the victim does not have to bear any expenses or effort. They only need to appear and provide evidence when summoned by the court, for which they even receive an allowance. This is because such matters are also the concern of the state (the government). Wrongs that are generally harmful to society must be discouraged; that is the purpose of punishing those who commit them. However, if the police do not register a case, one can file a private complaint in the criminal court.

Criminal Laws

Not all wrongs are punishable under the law. Wrongs that are defined in a law as an offense and for which a punishment is prescribed are called crimes. The law concerning crimes is known as criminal law. The Indian Penal Code, which contains about five hundred and thirteen sections, defines over three hundred and fifty crimes, both large and small, along with their respective punishments. This law, enacted during the British colonial period in 1860, remains the most important penal code in India today. Many criminal laws have been passed and implemented since then. Aside from criminal laws, all remaining laws can generally be described as civil laws. It can be said that labor laws and others are parts of civil law.
