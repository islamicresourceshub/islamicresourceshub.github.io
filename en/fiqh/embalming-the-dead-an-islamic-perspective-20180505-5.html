Embalming the Deceased: An Islamic Perspective

അഷ്‌റഫ് എകരൂല്‍ · 2018-05-05 · Fiqh

Islam's perspective is that the human body should be respected not only during life but also after death. Today, embalming is a widespread practice to preserve the biological structure of bodies of those who die in distant lands, as part of the process of bringing them back to their hometowns. How compatible is this with Islamic beliefs and practices? Is it better to bury the deceased at the place of death to avoid complications? This is a research article prepared in light of religious texts.

Islamic Sharia teaches that humans should be honored in death as they are in life

Islamic Sharia teaches that humans should be honored in death as they are in life. Islamic rulings related to the care of the deceased and graves are clear signs of this honor. Observe some of the directives Islam provides for the practical application of this honor:

  1. Close the eyes of the deceased, straighten their limbs, and cover them (with a cloth).
  2. Wash, shroud, and pray over them.
  3. Bury them.
  4. Sitting on graves, stepping on them, and digging them up without a compelling reason are strictly prohibited.
  5. Mutilating a human corpse and dishonoring it is forbidden.
  6. The Prophet ﷺ forbade mentioning anything other than the good aspects of the deceased's past life.

General Ruling on Burial

Burying the deceased as soon as possible after death is one of the communal obligations «fard kifayah» of the Islamic community. Allah has taught humanity its method from the very beginning. Allah says:

''അപ്പോള്‍ തന്റെ സഹോദരന്റെ മൃതദേഹം മറവുചെയ്യേണ്ടത് എങ്ങനെയെന്ന് അവന്ന് കാണിച്ചുകൊടുക്കുവാനായി നിലത്ത് മാന്തി കുഴിയുണ്ടാക്കുന്ന ഒരു കാക്കയെ അല്ലാഹു അയച്ചു. അവന്‍ പറഞ്ഞു: എന്തൊരു കഷ്ടം! എന്റെ സഹോദരന്റെ മൃതദേഹം മറവുചെയ്യുന്ന കാര്യത്തില്‍ ഈ കാക്കയെപ്പോലെ ആകാന്‍ പോലും എനിക്ക് കഴിയാതെ പോയല്ലോ. അങ്ങനെ അവന്‍ ഖേദക്കാരുടെ കൂട്ടത്തിലായിത്തീര്‍ന്നു'' (ക്വുര്‍ആന്‍ 5:31).

It is clear that burial has been inherited by humanity from the earliest generations.

See verse 21 of Surah Abasa in the Quran:

''അനന്തരം അവനെ മരിപ്പിക്കുകയും ക്വബ്‌റില്‍ മറയ്ക്കുകയും ചെയ്തു.''

Imam Qurtubi (may Allah have mercy on him) explains this verse thus: 'He was provided with graves to bury him, as protection from being eaten by wild animals and birds, or as a form of honor' (Tafsir al-Qurtubi). Verses 25 and 26 of Surah Al-Mursalat also indicate the same. Combining all these, it is ruled that burying the deceased is obligatory. This means that keeping the deceased unburied is anti-Islamic and unnatural. The Prophet ﷺ instructed that it should be done as quickly as possible.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

''ജനാസയെ നിങ്ങള്‍ വേഗത്തിലാക്കുക. കാരണം അത് നല്ലതാെണങ്കില്‍ അതിനെ അതിലേക്ക് (പുണ്യത്തിലേക്ക്) എത്തിക്കുന്നതാണ് ഏറ്റവും ഉത്തമം, ഇനി അതല്ലെങ്കില്‍ ഒരു ബുദ്ധിമുട്ട് നിങ്ങളുടെ പിരടിയില്‍ നിന്ന് ഇറക്കി വെക്കുകയും ചെയ്യാം'' (ബുഖാരി, മുസ്‌ലിം).

Another prophetic tradition narrated by Ibn Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) states: The Prophet ﷺ said:

''നിങ്ങളില്‍ നിന്ന് ആരെങ്കിലും മരണപ്പെട്ടാല്‍ നിങ്ങള്‍ അതിന്റെ പിടിച്ച് വെച്ചേക്കരുത്(വൈകിപ്പിക്കരുത്). മറവു ചെയ്യാന്‍ നിങ്ങള്‍ ധൃതി കാണിക്കണം.''

The Prophet ﷺ visited the companion Talhah ibn Bara' during his severe illness and, informing his family of his death, said:

''ഒരു മുസ്‌ലിമിന്റെ മയ്യിത്ത് കുടുംബത്തിന്നു മുന്നില്‍ ഇങ്ങനെ പിടിച്ചുവെക്കുന്നത് (മറവുചെയ്യാന്‍ വൈകിപ്പിക്കുന്നത്) നല്ലതല്ല'' (അബൂദാവൂദ്).

Based on this, Imam Ahmad said: 'Expediting the burial is part of honoring the deceased.'

In summary, Islam's general ruling is to bury the deceased in the earth and not to delay it. One of the fundamental reasons for this is to prevent changes to the deceased's good condition.

Delaying in Necessary Circumstances and Transferring the Deceased from One Place to Another

In necessary circumstances, the burial of the deceased may need to be delayed. (The Prophet ﷺ's burial, for example, took place two days after his death). Examples include situations where an unidentified body needs to be identified, or the cause of death in suspicious circumstances needs to be confirmed. One such situation is the need to transfer the deceased from the place of death to another locality. A summary of the Islamic ruling explained by scholars is provided below.

  1. While burying the deceased in the Muslim cemetery at the place of death is the predominant sunnah, there is no harm in transporting them to another location. (Hanbali scholars like Ibn Abidin (may Allah have mercy on him) hold this opinion. Their evidence: The companions Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Sa'id ibn Zayd ibn Amr died in the region of Aqeeq, and their bodies were brought to Madinah, seven miles away, and buried there. (Imam Malik narrates this in his Muwatta)). However, while acknowledging this incident (perhaps due to specific reasons), the majority of ancient and modern scholars (including Imam Nawawi, Imam Awza'i, and Ibn Mundhir) state that it is not permissible to transport the deceased from one place to another without a very valid reason. This is to prevent hardship for the deceased and to protect the body from undergoing changes. Some examples of valid reasons include:

    1. Fearing dishonor to the deceased at the place of burial, or situations where there is a fear of the body being desecrated due to war or enmity. (In such cases, transporting the body to safe places where Muslims reside becomes obligatory).
    2. Desiring to be near one's own people and to provide an opportunity for relatives to visit and pray.

Scholars connect to this the displeasure expressed by Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her), the sister and Mother of the Believers, when the body of the companion Abdurrahman ibn Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him), who died in Abyssinia, was brought to Makkah for burial. The opinion of Imam Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him) and others (in Al-Adhkar) is that even if the deceased made a will to be transferred to another land, it should not be carried out. A summary of the response given by the Lajnatud Daimah (Saudi Arabia's official fatwa board) to a question on this matter is as follows:

According to the practical sunnah during the time of the Prophet ﷺ and his companions, burial should take place where death occurred. The Prophet ﷺ even commanded that martyrs be buried where they fell. Therefore, transporting the deceased to other lands without valid reasons is not advisable. The reasons for this are:

  1. To follow the practice of the predecessors.
  2. To avoid difficulties.
  3. To protect the deceased from potential changes to the body.
  4. To implement the Prophet's ﷺ command to bury the deceased as quickly as possible.
  5. To avoid procedures that might need to be performed on the body in the name of preservation.

Embalming and Islamic Rulings

What is embalming?

Embalming, in general, refers to the method of using chemical substances to make necessary arrangements, either inside or outside the body, to maintain the deceased's condition when the body needs to be preserved unburied for longer than usual.

In modern times, embalming is also done to preserve bodies from decay when they need to be transported long distances for permissible reasons. In Arabic, this is called «tahnit». In reality, this term was used for anointing the deceased with perfume, but later it began to be technically used for medical embalming.

What is the purpose of embalming a Muslim deceased?

Besides delays for legal matters, embalming may be necessary due to the time taken to transport the deceased from one place to another.

Different types of embalming and their Islamic rulings

Medical experts explain five types of embalming. These are arterial, cavity, hypodermic, surface, and plastination. For ease of explanation, these can be divided into three categories:

1. Embalming through internal changes.

(Injection into the body, withdrawal from the body (blood, organs)).

The opinion of the majority of scholars is that all types of embalming involving internal changes are forbidden in Islamic jurisprudence. This is equivalent to disfiguring and dishonoring the deceased. Methods such as dissecting organs or drawing blood all fall under this category of dishonor. An unconditional prohibition is observed here (which is why countries like Kuwait prohibit embalming in this sense). However, compulsory situations are excluded. (According to the general legal principles of Islam, prohibitions become permissible in compelling circumstances). However, there are those who opine that embalming by injecting certain solutions into the blood vessels, without methods like splitting or dissecting the body, is permissible. The diversity in evaluating the reasons is the cause of the difference in research opinions. In short, in situations where no other methods are available and transportation becomes obligatory, only embalming by injecting chemical substances into the blood vessels without any cutting or dissection can be considered somewhat permissible in this category. (And Allah knows best). This is merely a modern research-based opinion in this field. Islam's general ruling is that nothing should be done to the deceased that would not be permissible to do to a living person. The Prophet ﷺ said:

''ഒരു മയ്യിത്തിന്റെ എല്ല് പൊട്ടിക്കല്‍ അതിനെ ജീവിനുള്ള അവസ്ഥയില്‍ പൊട്ടിക്കുന്നതിന്നു സമാനമാണ്'' (അബൂദാവൂദ്).

2. External Embalming (Application to the Body)

If this type of embalming is performed for reasons permitted by Islamic Sharia, and it does not alter the deceased's condition or dishonor their dignity, then there is no prohibition against it. However, the chemical substances or other materials used must not be those forbidden by Islam. (Like pork fat or intoxicants). However, methods such as immersing the entire body in a solution would fall under the category of dishonoring the deceased. (And Allah knows best).

3. Environmental Embalming (Casket, Room)

This category includes freezers for storing the deceased, and powders and solutions placed in the casket prepared for transporting the deceased to their hometown. Since the general objective is to protect the deceased from decomposition, and it does not harm the dignity of the deceased, scholars see no Islamic prohibitions for this type of embalming. On the contrary, scholarly opinion is that it is essential to preserve the sanctity of the deceased when the body must be stored for necessary reasons. Furthermore, jurists suggest that developing freezer systems is a better way to overcome the confusion surrounding embalming.

(The last two categories are not technically considered 'embalming' in Islamic jurisprudence.)

Expatriates and the Deceased

Once a person dies, it is the responsibility of the living to bury the deceased as quickly as possible. This provides honor to the deceased and prevents them from hardship. Expatriates and relatives in the homeland should make decisions regarding the deceased with this fact in mind. It is first necessary to examine whether the benefit of bringing a Muslim deceased back to their hometown, after days of preservation through embalming and other means from a place where sufficient Islamic community, law, and protection are available, outweighs the Islamic interests concerning the deceased. This is where factors such as seeing the deceased, and providing opportunities for families and others to visit the grave and pray, are evaluated. This means that decisions should be made based on whether these benefits outweigh the difficulties faced by the deceased and those handling the body. Here, it is essential to spiritually enlighten our families and community to think beyond mere temporary emotionality. Moreover, the glad tidings given by the Prophet ﷺ regarding the reward for those who die in a foreign land should bring peace to believers.

The Prophet ﷺ once prayed over a deceased native and said:

''അദ്ദേഹം ജന്മ നാട്ടിലല്ലാത്തിടത്ത് വെച്ചു മരിച്ചിരുന്നെങ്കില്‍!''

When the companions asked, 'Why is that, O Messenger of Allah?', the Prophet ﷺ replied:

''തന്റെ ജന്മനാട്ടിലല്ലാതെ മരിക്കുന്നവര്‍ക്ക് സ്വര്‍ഗത്തില്‍ അത്രയും ദൂരം അളന്ന് നല്‍കി പ്രതിഫലം നല്‍കുന്നതാണ്.'' (നസാഈ).

However, the lesson illuminated by the breadth of Islamic jurisprudence is not to be rigid without considering the family's emotions. (And Allah knows best).

Islamic Ruling on Viewing the Deceased

While viewing the deceased is not prescribed as a sunnah for everyone, it is permissible. The Prophet ﷺ uncovered the face of Uthman ibn Maz'un (may Allah be pleased with him) and kissed him. (Abu Dawud). Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) uncovered the Prophet's ﷺ blessed body, looked at his face, and kissed him. (Bukhari). When something is called sunnah, one can expect a reward from Allah for that action. However, when something is permissible, it refers to actions done out of human interest that are not forbidden by religion.

Summary

  1. Burying the deceased quickly is an Islamic etiquette.
  2. It can be delayed for necessary reasons.
  3. External embalming, as its purpose is to protect the deceased from decomposition and it does not involve difficult interventions on the physical body, is not seen as wrong.
  4. Developing freezer systems is better as it helps to avoid ambiguity.
  5. The living, including relatives, should seek arrangements to bury the deceased quickly. Cultivating the necessary spiritual awareness is the solution.
  6. Allah grants a special reward to those who die in a foreign land.
  7. There is no prohibition in Islam for those who wish to view the deceased or for showing the deceased.

These are some points that became clear through my study and research. Further clarification and corrections may be needed. As the topic is research-oriented, it is natural for multiple opinions to be valid.
