**1. What is the wisdom behind making fasting mandatory?**

Answer: We can understand this by reciting the following Quranic verse. Allah says:

''O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous'' (Quran 2:183).

The purpose of making fasting mandatory is to be mindful of Allah and to worship Him. «Taqwa» - God-consciousness - means abandoning what is forbidden and implementing what is commanded.

Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrates that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Whoever does not give up false speech and acting upon it, Allah has no need of his leaving his food and drink'' (Bukhari).

It is clear from this hadith that it is essential for a fasting person to perform obligatory duties and refrain from prohibitions. If one completely adheres to avoiding backbiting, slander, lying, engaging in forbidden trade, and staying away from obscenities during Ramadan, it becomes possible for them to maintain this in other months as well.

Regrettably, many people engage in acts that do not befit the sanctity of fasting. They fast while neglecting obligatory duties and freely committing prohibitions. Therefore, one must be cautious, as some actions reduce the reward of the fast, while others render the fast itself void.

**2. What is your opinion on the argument that the Muslim community can be unified in fasting and celebrating Eid by basing it on the moon sighting in Makkah?**

Answer: Astronomically, this is impossible. As Sheikh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah stated, sunrise and sunset times vary by location. Therefore, based on both textual evidence and experience, every country has its own rulings.

Evidence from the texts: Allah says: ''...so whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it...'' (Quran 2:185).

If people in some part of the world do not see the moon, but it is seen in Makkah, how can the address in this verse be fulfilled for them? Similarly, the Prophet (peace be upon him) says:

Muhammad bin Ziyad (may Allah be pleased with him) says he heard Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him) say: The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Fast when you see it [the new moon] and break your fast when you see it [the new moon of Shawwal]'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

Based on this evidence, how can people in countries like Pakistan fast if the moon is sighted in Makkah? Because the month of Ramadan has not dawned for them. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said 'when you see it'.

Experience: This is a clear analogy that no one can deny or oppose. We know that the sun rises in the east before it rises in the west. If so, should we fast while it is night for us just because the sun has risen in the east? The answer is no. Similarly, should we break our fast while it is still day for us just because the sun has set in the east? The answer is also no. Therefore, the ruling for the moon is the same as the ruling for the sun. However, the time of the moon is related to the month, and the time of the sun is related to the day. Allah says:

''It has been made permissible for you the night preceding fasting to go to your wives [for sexual relations]. They are clothing for you and you are clothing for them. Allah knows that you used to deceive yourselves, so He accepted your repentance and forgave you. So now, have relations with them and seek that which Allah has decreed for you. And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread [of night]. Then complete the fast until the night. And do not have relations with them as long as you are staying for worship in the mosques. These are the limits [set by] Allah, so do not approach them. Thus does Allah make clear His ordinances to the people that they may become righteous'' (Quran 2:187).

Allah says: ''...so whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it...''

Based on the evidence explained above, every country has its own specific rulings according to its sunrise and sunset. Fasting and Eid are determined based on sighting the moon, as Allah clarified in His Quran and the Prophet (peace be upon him) in his Sunnah.

(The issue discussed here is observing Eid and fasting on the same day globally. However, this contradicts the Quran, the established Prophetic tradition, and the laws of astronomy. It is a known fact that there is a difference in time and days for prayer and fasting according to the difference in sunrise and sunset. Those who wish to know more should refer to the book 'Moon Sighting in Quran and Science' written by Umar Farooq. Trans:).

**3. A person who was fasting in Ramadan traveled to another country. During that time, the new moon of Shawwal was sighted in their home country (from where they started), and Eid was confirmed. However, the moon was not sighted in the country they traveled to. What should be done in this situation?**

![](/assets/ramadan-fasting-rulings-and-prohibitions-20180519-7/19May18-08.jpg)

Answer: If a person travels from one country to another, they should follow the ruling of the country they have reached in their Islamic practices. That is, they should celebrate Eid when the people of the country they reached celebrate it. This is because the Prophet (peace be upon him) said that one should fast when the people fast. Celebrating Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha should also be done with them. Even if it is one or two days later, that is how it should be done. Similarly, if the sunset is an hour or two later in the country they traveled to, they should break their fast according to the time there. In some places, the sunset may be two, three, or more hours later. In that case, they should break their fast when the sun sets there.

(Example: A person who left India for Saudi Arabia at 6 AM during Ramadan should break their fast according to Saudi time, which is two and a half hours later than Indian time. This means they should not break their fast when people in India break theirs).

Because the Prophet (peace be upon him) said 'when you see it', meaning break the fast when you see the moon. The same can happen in reverse. That is, if the new moon of Shawwal is sighted a day or two earlier in the country they traveled to, they should celebrate Eid with them. They should make up for the one or two days of fasting they missed after the first of Shawwal. Because Ramadan will be twenty-nine or thirty days. If one day is missed, make up one; if two days are missed, make up two. Because all lunar months, including Ramadan, will not be less than twenty-nine days. If the new moon of Shawwal is sighted in the country they traveled to before they complete twenty-nine days of Ramadan, they should celebrate Eid with them and make up for the missed days later. However, if one has to fast an extra day, they should do so. Because Eid should be celebrated only when the moon is sighted. Just as an hour or two can be added to a day, one can consider a day added.

**4. Is it permissible for those doing heavy and difficult work to skip fasting?**

Answer: Based on the evidence, my opinion is that it is not permissible to skip fasting for work purposes. If it is not possible to combine work and fasting, one should take leave from work and fast during Ramadan. Because fasting in Ramadan is one of the pillars of Islam. It should not be compromised.

**4. What should a girl do if she fasted during her menstrual period due to ignorance?**

Answer: She must make up for the fast she observed during her menstrual period later. Because the fast of a menstruating woman is unacceptable, even if it was due to ignorance. Similarly, if a girl has her first period and does not inform her family out of shyness, she should not fast. She must make up for all the fasts she missed. When a woman starts menstruating, it means she has reached puberty. Therefore, it is mandatory for her to make up for the missed fasts.

**5. What is the ruling for a person who skips fasting in Ramadan to earn a living for themselves and those under their care?**

Answer: Some scholars have stated that the case of these people is similar to that of a sick person. If a sick person is absolutely unable to fast, they can skip it. They must make up for it after recovering from the illness; if they remain alive. If they die, someone else should make it up for them. If their guardian cannot make it up, they should feed a poor person for each day missed.

However, the opinion of scholars who do not equate them with the sick is as follows: All acts of worship are time-bound. If anyone delays acts of worship from their appointed time without a valid reason, it will not be accepted from them. They should then increase in good deeds and voluntary acts and seek forgiveness from Allah. The evidence for this is:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) says: ''Whoever performs an action which is not in accordance with our matter, it will be rejected'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

Just as acts of worship are not accepted if performed before their time, they are not accepted if performed after their time. However, if there are reasons like ignorance or forgetfulness, they will be accepted.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said regarding forgetfulness: ''Whoever sleeps through the prayer time or forgets it, let him pray when he remembers it; there is no other expiation for it'' (Muslim).

The person who skipped the Ramadan fast to earn a living for themselves and their children thinks, as we explained above, in a way that equates them with the sick. Allah knows best.

**6. What are the reasons for which it is permissible to skip fasting?**

![](/assets/ramadan-fasting-rulings-and-prohibitions-20180519-7/19May18-09.jpg)

Answer: According to the Quran, illness and travel are permissible reasons. Similarly, a pregnant woman can skip fasting if she fears harm to herself or the child in her womb. Likewise, a breastfeeding woman can skip it if she fears harm to herself or her child by fasting. One can skip fasting to save those in danger. For example, saving someone who is drowning in the sea, or someone trapped in a place where a fire has broken out. Similarly, one can skip fasting to engage in Jihad, which Islam teaches. This is permitted by Islam. Because the Prophet (peace be upon him) said to his companions during the conquest of Makkah:

''Tomorrow you will face the enemy, so breaking the fast is better for you to have health and strength. So break your fast'' (Muslim).

If a person skips fasting for a permissible reason, and that reason ends, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. Ex: If someone skips fasting to save someone drowning in water, they do not have to fast for the remaining time after saving them. This is the reliable opinion on this matter. If a person recovers from illness during the day, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. Similarly, if a traveler reaches their home during the day, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. Likewise, if a menstruating woman becomes pure during the day (before Maghrib), she does not have to fast for the remaining time. Because they all skipped fasting for reasons permitted by Islam. Therefore, for them, there is no obligation to fast for the remaining time.

However, if it is realized during the day that the Ramadan moon has been sighted, one must fast for the remaining time. The difference between these two is clear and distinct. Once there is clarity about whether it is a fast or not, fasting that day is mandatory. But before clarity is reached, they are excused due to ignorance.

Therefore, those who realize that this day is part of Ramadan must fast for the remaining time. But those we explained first are those who understood that fasting is mandatory. They skipped fasting for reasons permitted by Islam. The difference between these is clear.

**7. What should a person do if they fall asleep without the intention to fast before the Ramadan moon is sighted, and upon waking up after dawn, they realize that Ramadan has begun? Do they have to make up for that day's fast later?**

Answer: The opinion of the majority of scholars is that a person who falls asleep without the intention to fast before the Ramadan moon is sighted, and realizes upon waking after dawn that Ramadan has begun, should fast for the remaining time of that day and make up for it on another day later. The only one who expressed an opinion against this is Sheikh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him).

His opinion: 'The intention to fast is required from the moment one learns of the moon sighting. Since this person did not know, they are excused. If they had known that the moon had been sighted, they would never have slept without the intention to fast. They are ignorant of that matter. Ignorance is among the excuses. Based on this, their fast is valid.' According to this opinion, there is no need to make it up later.

The opinion of the majority of scholars is to fast that day and make up for it later. In my opinion, for the sake of caution, it is better to make up for that day.

**8. If a person skips fasting for a permissible reason, but that reason ends during the day, do they have to fast for the remaining time?**

Answer: They do not have to fast for the remaining time, because they skipped fasting for a permissible reason. Therefore, when that reason ends, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. For example, someone needs to take a very essential medicine. If they use that medicine, their fast is broken. Since they skipped fasting for a permissible reason, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. Instead, making up for one day is enough. There is no religious benefit in fasting for the remaining time, and it would not be correct. Therefore, since there is no benefit, fasting for the remaining time is not mandatory.

Example: Someone saw a person drowning in water. The person seeing it says, 'If I drink a little water, I will have the ability to save him; if I don't drink water, I won't be able to.' So they drink water and save him. They can eat and drink for the remaining time. Because they skipped fasting for a reason permitted by Islam. For them, the remaining time is not to be honored. Therefore, there is no need to fast for the remaining time. Do we tell a sick person who skips fasting, 'Do not eat unless you are hungry, do not drink unless you are thirsty'? No! Because Islam gives permission to a sick person to skip fasting. Therefore, if anyone skips fasting for a reason permitted by Islam, they do not have to fast for the remaining time even if that reason disappears during the day. However, if anyone skips fasting without a permissible reason, they must fast for the remaining time. Because they do not have permission to skip fasting. They violated the sanctity of that day without religious permission. Therefore, they must fast for the remaining time and make up for it on another day. Allah knows best.

**9. What is the ruling if a doctor advises a woman suffering from paralysis to skip fasting?**

Answer: Allah says: ''The month of Ramadan [is that] in which was revealed the Quran, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship...'' (Quran 2:185).

If they are patients who do not expect recovery, they should feed a poor person for each day. The food we normally eat should be given to the poor. Good quality food should be given. A paralyzed sick woman should give food to a poor person for each day.

**10. When and how should a traveler pray and fast?**

![](/assets/ramadan-fasting-rulings-and-prohibitions-20180519-7/19May18-10.jpg)

Answer: From the time a traveler leaves their home until they return, they should pray four-rak'ah prayers as two rak'ahs. Because Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says:

''Prayer was initially made mandatory as two rak'ahs. It was established for travelers, and completed for those residing at home.'' Another report says: ''And it was increased for those residing at home'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

From Anas bin Malik (may Allah be pleased with him): ''We went out with the Prophet (peace be upon him) from Madinah to Makkah. We were praying two rak'ahs until we returned to Madinah'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

However, if a traveler is praying behind an Imam who is a resident, they must complete the four rak'ahs. Whether they join the Imam from the beginning or in the middle. Because the Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) says:

''When you hear the Iqamah, walk to the prayer. You should walk with calmness and dignity. You should not run. Pray what you get, and complete what you missed.''

'Pray what you get, and complete what you missed' implies that a traveler praying behind someone who prays four rak'ahs must pray fully. Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) was asked: 'Why does a traveler pray four rak'ahs when praying behind a resident, and two rak'ahs otherwise?' He said: 'That is the Prophetic tradition.'

Congregational prayer is not waived for a traveler. Because even during war, Allah said:

''And when you are among them and lead them in prayer, let a group of them stand [in prayer] with you and let them carry their arms. And when they have prostrated, let them be [in position] behind you and have the other group come forward which has not [yet] prayed and let them pray with you...'' (Quran 4:102).

If one hears the Adhan, a traveler should participate in the congregational prayer in the mosques even if they are not in their home country. However, if the mosque is far away, or if they fear losing their companions, they do not have to participate. This is clarified based on the general evidence that those who hear the Adhan and Iqamah must respond and come to the mosques.

Similarly, except for the Sunnah prayers before and after Dhuhr, Maghrib, and Isha, travelers can pray all other Sunnah prayers like Witr, night prayer, Duha prayer, and the two rak'ahs before Subh.

Likewise, Dhuhr and Asr prayers can be combined, and Maghrib and Isha can be combined. They can be performed by advancing or delaying, whichever is easier and more convenient.

However, if one starts staying in one place, it is better not to combine prayers, though it is not a problem if they do. Both have come from the Messenger (peace be upon him).

Travelers can fast in Ramadan. It is not a problem if they skip it. They must make up for the number of days they skipped. If skipping fasting is easier and more convenient for the traveler, that is best. Because Allah loves it when His concessions are utilized. The Lord of the worlds knows everything.

**11. What is the ruling on a traveler fasting while struggling?**

Answer: If a traveler is fasting with difficulty, it is disliked. See the following Prophetic statement:

When the Prophet (peace be upon him) saw someone shading another and people gathering around him, he asked: 'What is that?' They said: 'He is a fasting person.' Then the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'It is not an act of righteousness to fast while traveling' (Bukhari, Muslim).

In cases of extreme difficulty, it is mandatory for travelers to skip fasting. Because when people informed the Prophet (peace be upon him) that it was difficult to fast (while traveling), he told them to skip it. When it was said that some people had fasted, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'They are the disobedient ones, they are the disobedient ones' (Muslim).

If there is no difficulty for travelers, it is better to fast. Because it is established that the Prophet (peace be upon him) fasted while traveling.

Abu Darda (may Allah be pleased with him) said: 'We were on a journey with the Prophet (peace be upon him) during a very hot day in Ramadan. None of us were fasting (on this journey) except the Prophet (peace be upon him) and Abdullah ibn Rawahah' (Bukhari, Muslim).

(The above statement clarifies that since it came in the hadith that the Prophet (peace be upon him) fasted, it is also permissible to do so. However, as the scholars who discussed the matter opined, it is understood from the evidence that a traveler can decide for themselves. After all, everyone knows their own health condition best. **12. In modern times, since there are state-of-the-art travel facilities, fasting is not a difficult matter for a traveler (compared to the past). In this situation, what is the ruling on a traveler fasting?**

Answer: A traveler can fast or skip fasting. Allah says: ''...and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days...'' (Quran 2:185).

Companions used to travel with the Prophet (peace be upon him), and among them were those who fasted and those who skipped it. Those who fasted did not criticize those who skipped, and those who skipped did not criticize those who fasted. The Prophet (peace be upon him) used to fast while traveling.

Abu Darda (may Allah be pleased with him) said: ''We were on a journey with the Prophet (peace be upon him) during a very hot day in Ramadan. None of us were fasting (on this journey) except the Prophet (peace be upon him) and Abdullah ibn Rawahah'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

The rule for a traveler is that they can fast or skip it. However, if there is no difficulty for the traveler, it is better to fast. Because there are three benefits in it:

1. Following the Sunnah of the Prophet (peace be upon him). 2. Convenience: Fasting with people is more convenient than fasting alone. 3. It is possible to fulfill one's obligation quickly.

**13. Is it permissible for a traveler who has fasted to skip fasting to perform Umrah upon reaching Makkah?**

Answer: During the conquest of Makkah, the Prophet (peace be upon him) skipped fasting when he entered Makkah on the twentieth of Ramadan. He prayed two rak'ahs with the people of Makkah and ordered them to complete the prayer. That is what is seen in the hadith:

''...He said to the people of Makkah: 'O people of Makkah, complete your prayer, we are travelers' (Bukhari). It can be seen in Sahih al-Bukhari that the Prophet (peace be upon him) stayed in Makkah skipping the fast for the remaining days. Traveling does not end when one reaches Makkah to perform Umrah. Therefore, there is no need to fast. Some people are seen fasting while traveling. They justify it by saying that the travel is very easy. Then they get very tired and struggle. They ask whether it is enough to perform Umrah after breaking the fast, or whether they should break the fast quickly and perform Umrah.

In this situation, we should tell such people; break the fast first to perform Umrah. Then one can enter Makkah with health and energy. Those who come to Makkah with the intention of performing Umrah should perform that act quickly and complete it. Because when the Prophet (peace be upon him) entered Makkah to perform Umrah, he used to rush to the mosque to complete that act quickly. He used to tie his animal near the mosque. Therefore, those who come to Makkah intending to perform Umrah in Ramadan should skip fasting and complete Umrah during the day with energy, which is better than breaking the fast and performing Umrah at night.

It is established from the Prophet (peace be upon him) as follows: ''The Prophet (peace be upon him) was fasting on the journey during the conquest of Makkah. Then people came to him and said: 'O Prophet, the people are struggling a lot due to fasting. They are waiting to see what you will do.' This happened after Asr. Then the Prophet (peace be upon him) asked for water and broke his fast during the journey. Moreover, he broke his fast in the latter part of the day'' (Muslim).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) did all this to teach people that they can skip fasting while traveling. There is no doubt that fasting while traveling with great difficulty is against the Prophetic Sunnah. The Prophetic statement 'It is not an act of righteousness to fast while traveling' teaches that.

**14. Can breastfeeding women skip fasting? When should they make it up? Do they have to feed the poor?**

Answer: If they fear that fasting will reduce the breast milk for the child, or that some other difficulty will happen to the child, they can skip fasting. But, like the sick, they must make it up later. Regarding this, Allah says:

''...and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship...'' (Quran 2:185).

When the reason ends, they should start making it up. It can be made up in the winter when the weather is cool and the day is short, or in the next year. Those who have reasons that do not go away, or have an illness from which recovery is not expected, are the ones who should feed a poor person for each day instead of fasting.

**15. If a fasting person rests for most of the day due to hunger and thirst, will it affect the acceptance of the fast?**

Answer: It will not affect the acceptance of the fast. On the contrary, the reward increases as the fatigue increases. Because see what the Prophet (peace be upon him) says to Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her):

''Your reward is according to your fatigue'' (Bukhari). Whoever gets tired to obey Allah, the reward increases according to that fatigue. There is no harm in a fasting person trying to alleviate fatigue by cooling the body with water and sitting in a cool place when they get tired.

**16. Will the fast be broken if there is a firm intention to break the fast without eating or drinking?**

Answer: Certainly, the act of worship called fasting includes intention, and refraining from things that break the fast. Through fasting, one intends to draw closer to Allah by refraining from things that render the fast void. Therefore, if one firmly decides to abandon this intention, the fast becomes void. If one does this in Ramadan, they must fast for the remaining time. Because if one skips fasting for a reason not permitted by Islam, they must fast for the remaining time and make it up.

However, if there is doubt without a firm decision, there is a difference of opinion among scholars on this matter. Some scholars say their fast becomes void. Because doubt removes certainty.

A group of scholars clarifies that the fast does not become void. Because fundamentally, the intention remains. The initial intention does not go away unless one firmly intends otherwise. This opinion is the most reliable in my view. Allah knows best.

**17. What is the ruling if a fasting person eats out of forgetfulness? What should the one who sees it do?**

Answer: If someone eats or drinks out of forgetfulness, their fast is valid. But, they must stop as soon as they remember. It is also mandatory to remove what is in the mouth at that moment. See the evidence that the fast is valid:

Narrated from Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): ''Whoever eats or drinks out of forgetfulness, let him complete his fast. Because it is Allah who fed him and gave him drink'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

If anyone does a forbidden thing due to forgetfulness, there is no punishment for it. Allah says:

''...Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred...'' (Quran 2:28).

However, if one sees a fasting person eating out of forgetfulness, it is mandatory to remind them. Because that is forbidding evil.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand. If he cannot, then with his tongue. If he cannot, then with his heart'' (Muslim).

Eating and drinking while fasting is among the forbidden things. It must be stopped. However, there is a concession regarding the acceptance of the fast due to the reason of forgetfulness. But there is no concession for the one who is watching it in terms of stopping it.

**18. What is the ruling on a fasting person applying kohl?**

Answer: A fasting person can apply kohl, there is no problem with it. Similarly, eye and ear drops can also be used. Because that is not eating or drinking. Using drops in the eyes and nose cannot be called eating or drinking. It is only forbidden to eat and drink. This opinion is held by Sheikh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him). However, if medicine is used in the nose and it intentionally reaches the mouth, the fast becomes void. See a hadith:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''If you are not fasting, sniff water into your nose thoroughly'' (Abu Dawud, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah).

--- 

**19. What is the ruling on a fasting person brushing their teeth and using perfume?**

Answer: Brushing teeth at the beginning or end of the day by a fasting person is part of the Prophetic tradition. See the hadith:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Brushing teeth is a means of purifying the mouth and pleasing the Lord'' (Bukhari).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''If it were not for the difficulty on my community, I would have ordered them to use the Miswak before every prayer'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

Similarly, a fasting person can use perfume at the beginning or end of the day. Whether it is in smoke form, liquid form, or any other form, it can be used. However, scented smoke should not be inhaled through the nose. Because scented smoke can enter the nose and reach the stomach. This is why the Prophet (peace be upon him) said to Laqit bin Sabrah: ''If you are not fasting, sniff water into your nose thoroughly'' (Abu Dawud, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah).

**20. What are the things that render the fast void?**

![](/assets/ramadan-fasting-rulings-and-prohibitions-20180519-7/19May18-06.jpg)

Answer: The fast becomes void due to the following things.

1. Sexual intercourse

2. Eating

3. Drinking

4. Ejaculation with desire

5. Using things that replace food and drink

6. Intentional vomiting

7. Cupping (a treatment to remove bad blood)

8. Menstrual blood, postpartum blood coming out.

Evidence that food, drink, and intercourse are forbidden for a fasting person. Allah says: ''...So now, have relations with them and seek that which Allah has decreed for you. And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread [of night]. Then complete the fast until the night'' (Quran 2:187).

Evidence that the fast becomes void if semen (Maniyy) comes out with desire:

It can be seen in a Qudsi hadith: ''He leaves his food, drink, and desire for My sake'' (Ibn Majah).

See one more hadith: The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: 'Your having relations with your wives is charity.' They asked: 'O Messenger of Allah, is there a reward in satisfying one's desire?' He said: 'If they satisfy it in a forbidden way, is there a sin? What do you think? Similarly, if they put it in a permissible way, there is a reward' (Muslim).

Putting it here refers to Maniyy. The reason why it is said that the fast does not break if Madhyy (a type of fluid that comes out of the penis when desire arises) comes out with desire or due to kissing without intercourse, according to the strong opinion, is this.

If one uses things like injections that replace food and drink, the fast becomes void. Even if they are not food and drink, since they are used for that purpose, the fast becomes void if they are used.

Evidence that intentional vomiting renders the fast void:

From Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Whoever vomits, he does not have to make up that fast. But whoever vomits intentionally, let him make it up. Whoever vomits unintentionally, there is no Qada (make-up) for him'' (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi).

Evidence that cupping breaks the fast:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''The one who cups and the one who is cupped have broken their fast'' (Bukhari, narrated as Ta'liq).

Menstrual or postpartum blood coming out: The Prophet (peace be upon him) said to a woman:

''When menstruation occurs, she should not fast or pray'' (Bukhari). Scholars have agreed that the fast of those who have menstrual or postpartum blood is not valid.

The fast becomes void due to the seven things mentioned above only with three conditions.

They are as follows:

1. One must be aware that doing such a thing will render the fast void.

2. One must be in their right mind.

3. It should not be done under compulsion from anyone. It must be of one's own free will. If someone forces another to drink water, the fast does not break.

If any of the previously mentioned things are done without knowledge or intention, the fast does not break. The fast becomes valid. Allah says:

''...Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred...'' (Quran 2:286).

''...There is no blame upon you for that in which you have erred but [only for] what your hearts intended...'' (Quran 33:5).

We can see evidence for this from the Prophetic tradition:

From Adiyy bin Hatim (may Allah be pleased with him): ''He fasted and placed two threads under his pillow. (Those two threads were used to tie the camel). One was black and the other was white. He ate and drank until he could clearly see white from black. Then he fasted. In the morning, he went to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and told him the matter. Then the Prophet (peace be upon him) explained to him: 'The black thread and white thread mentioned in the verse are not the black and white threads we have. Rather, the white thread refers to the whiteness of the day and the black thread refers to the blackness of the night.' But the Prophet (peace be upon him) did not order him to make up the fast'' (Bukhari, Muslim). Since he was ignorant of the ruling, the fast did not become void.

Even if there is ignorance about the time, the fast does not become void. We can see evidence for this: From Asma bint Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with her): She said: ''We broke our fast during the time of the Prophet (peace be upon him) on a cloudy day. The sun set after that'' (Bukhari). The Prophet (peace be upon him) did not tell them to make up that fast. If making it up was mandatory in such a situation, he would have ordered them. If he had ordered them, they would have narrated it for the community. Because Allah says: ''Indeed, it is We who sent down the message and indeed, We will be its guardian'' (Quran 15:9).

Since it was not narrated even though there was a necessary situation, it should be understood that the Prophet (peace be upon him) did not order them so. Since he did not order to make it up, it can be understood that it is not mandatory. Similarly, someone woke up from sleep and ate food and drink. Only after that did they realize that they ate food and drink after dawn. Then they do not have to make up that fast. Because they did it without knowing.

Second condition: One must be in their right mind. If someone eats or drinks out of forgetfulness, the fast is valid. It does not have to be made up. Allah says:

''...Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred...'' (Quran 2:286).

Narrated from Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): ''Whoever eats or drinks out of forgetfulness, let him complete his fast. Because it is Allah who fed him and gave him drink'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

Third condition: It must be done voluntarily. It should not be done under compulsion from anyone. If such things are done without free will, the fast does not become void. Allah says:

''Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his belief - except for one who is forced [to renounce his religion] while his heart is secure in faith. But those who [willingly] open their breasts to disbelief, upon them is wrath from Allah, and for them is a great punishment'' (Quran 16:106).

If this is the ruling regarding doing things of disbelief under compulsion, then we should not doubt in matters of less gravity. See a hadith:

The Prophet (peace be upon him) says: ''Error, forgetfulness, and compulsion have been forgiven for my community'' (Ibn Majah, Tabarani).

Similarly, if dust or something enters the nose of a fasting person and it has a taste and it enters the stomach, the fast does not become void because of that, because it does not happen with their intention. Similarly, if someone is forced to drink water, the fast does not break. Even if there is a wet dream in sleep, the fast does not become void, because it does not happen based on their intention. Similarly, if a husband forces his wife to do things that break the fast, the wife's fast is not broken. Because she did not do it voluntarily, but rather submitted to the husband's compulsion.

One point is raised here; if a man who is required to fast (who has no exemption) has sexual intercourse during the day in Ramadan, five things will come to him through that:

1. He must fast for the remaining time. 2. His fast becomes void. 3. He must make it up. 4. He must perform expiation. 5. He committed a sin.

In this matter, there is no difference between one who knows and one who does not know. That is, if a man required to fast has sexual intercourse during the day in Ramadan, even if he did it without knowing that expiation is mandatory, the rulings we explained above apply to him. Because he did the thing that breaks the fast intentionally. If one intentionally does something that renders the fast void, the ruling for it also applies to him. We can see in the hadith:

In the hadith narrated by Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him), a man came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and said: 'O Prophet, I am ruined.' The Prophet (peace be upon him) asked: 'What ruined you?' He said: 'I had sexual intercourse with my wife while fasting during the day in Ramadan' (Bukhari).

Then the Prophet (peace be upon him) asked him to perform expiation. He did not know that expiation was mandatory if he did so. Here, there is a reason to specifically say 'one who is required to fast'. If a husband and wife have intercourse after returning from a journey, they do not have to perform expiation. Because they are travelers, they are not required to fast. Similarly, if a traveler fasts, they are not required to complete it. If they want, they can complete it, if they don't want, they don't have to complete it. If they don't complete it (since the traveler is not required to fast), they just have to make it up.

**21. Will the fast be broken if asthma patients use an inhaler to make breathing easier?**

Answer: Nothing reaches the stomach by using an inhaler, and since it is a substance without mass, the fast does not become void because of it.

--- 

**22. Will the fast be rendered void due to vomiting?**

Answer: If one vomits intentionally, the fast becomes void. However, the fast does not become void due to natural vomiting. See the hadith:

From Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Whoever vomits, he does not have to make up that fast. But whoever vomits intentionally, let him make it up, whoever vomits unintentionally, there is no Qada for him'' (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi).

--- 

**23. Will the fast be rendered void if a little blood comes from the gums or elsewhere of a fasting person?**

Answer: Nothing happens to the fast due to blood coming from the parts of the teeth. But, it should not be swallowed. Similarly, if there is a cold or something, the fast does not become void, and there is no Qada for them.

--- 

**24. Will the fast be valid if a menstruating woman becomes pure before Subh and bathes after dawn?**

Answer: If she has become pure (the blood has stopped) before Fajr (before Subh), her fast will be valid. It must be ensured that she has become pure before Fajr. Some women think they have become pure, but in reality, they have not. Therefore, some women used to come to Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) with cotton as a sign of purity. Then Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) would say to them: 'Do not rush, you will not be pure until you see the white color.' (Meaning to see the white color on the pad used during menstruation). Women should show patience until they are truly pure. Once pure, make the intention to fast. It is enough to bathe after dawn. Similarly, the matter of prayer should also be taken seriously. One should bathe as soon as possible and pray on time. 

Some women become pure before or after dawn. However, they bathe late after sunrise. They justify it by saying they do so to clean properly and bathe to be cleaner. This is wrong whether it is in Ramadan or otherwise. In reality, one should bathe quickly, become pure, and perform the prayer on time. It is enough for her to perform the mandatory bath to perform the prayer. If she intends more purity and cleanliness, there is no problem in bathing once more later. Similarly, a woman with major impurity can bathe after dawn. Their fast will also be valid. Just like a man who is fasting with major impurity bathing after dawn. There is no problem with his fast, it is valid. See a hadith:

''The Prophet (peace be upon him) used to fast while having major impurity from his wives and bathe after dawn'' (Bukhari, Muslim)

--- 

**25. Will the fast be rendered void if a fasting person has a tooth pulled?**

Answer: The fast does not become void due to blood (a little blood) coming from pulling a tooth or doing something similar, the ruling on cupping does not apply to it.

--- 

**26. Will the fast be rendered void if a little blood is taken for testing?**

Answer: The fast does not become void by taking a little blood for testing. Doctors have to take blood to examine the patient. Because of that, their fast does not become void. It is not like taking blood by cupping. (The opinion that the fast breaks if cupping is done is correct. That is what the Sheikh chose. Scholars say that the fast breaks if blood is given based on this hadith. There is also an opinion that it does not break if cupping is done. See Fath al-Bari. Trans:) Therefore, there must be religious evidence that the fast becomes void. However, there is no evidence that the fast becomes void if a little blood is taken for testing. If a lot of blood is taken to give to someone else, their fast becomes void. Therefore, one who is fasting should not give blood to someone else except in essential situations. In an emergency situation where it is not possible to wait until sunset, a fasting person can give blood, and their fast will break through that. They can use food and drink for the remaining time. One who does so must also make it up.

--- 

**27. Is a fasting person allowed to smell perfume?**

Answer: One who is fasting can smell perfume. There is no problem in burning perfume. However, the smoke of the perfume should not be inhaled into the inside. Because smoke has the possibility of reaching the stomach like water. If it is just smelling, there is no problem at all.

--- 

**28. What is the difference between inhaling smoke and using drops? Why does the fast become void if smoke is inhaled, and not void if drops are used?**

Answer: The one who inhales smoke does so intentionally with the intention of it entering their stomach. However, drops used in the eyes, nose, or elsewhere are not used with the intention of entering the stomach. Rather, they are used to reach the nasal passage. That is the difference between these two.

--- 

**29. Will the fast be compromised if a little water is accidentally swallowed through the mouth while sniffing water into the nose or rinsing the mouth?**

Answer: If a little water is accidentally swallowed through the mouth while a fasting person is sniffing water into the nose or rinsing the mouth, there is no problem. Their fast will be valid. Because they are not doing it intentionally.

--- 

**30. What is the ruling on a fasting person using perfume?**

Answer: There is no problem in using perfume during the day in Ramadan, and there is no prohibition in smelling it, but one should not inhale the smoke, as it reaches the stomach.

--- 

**31. Will the fast be rendered void if something comes out of the nose due to a cold or something?**

Answer: The fast will not become void if phlegm comes out of the nose, even if it is a lot. Because it is not intentional.

--- 

**32. In the prayer time information list for the month of Ramadan in some calendars, it is seen that time is written under the name (Fasting) ten or fifteen minutes before the Fajr Adhan. Is there any record of this in the Prophetic tradition?**

Answer: This is part of Bid'ah (innovation). There is no basis for this in the Prophetic Sunnah. It is against the Sunnah. Because Allah says in the Quran:

''...And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread [of night]'' (Quran 2:187).

See the hadith: The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Bilal will call the Adhan at night. But eat and drink until Ibn Umm Maktum calls the Adhan. Because he (Ibn Umm Maktum) will not call the Adhan until dawn becomes clear'' (Bukhari, Muslim).

People creating a special time like this by saying 'Imsak' is an addition to what Allah has made mandatory. It is exceeding the limits in Allah's religion. Regarding them, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: ''Those who exceed the limits are ruined, those who exceed the limits are ruined'' (Muslim).

--- 

**33. If a person breaks their fast at the airport when the Adhan is called after sunset while on the ground, and then if they see the sun when the plane takes off, do they have to fast for the remaining time?**

Answer: If it is in the form mentioned in the question, they do not have to fast for the remaining time. The time for breaking the fast had arrived while they were at the airport. The sun had set in the place they were in. See a hadith:

The Messenger of Allah (peace be upon him) said: ''If the night comes from here and the day retreats and the sun sets, the fasting person has broken their fast'' (Bukhari).

If they broke their fast when the sun set while at the airport, their day ended. They are not required to fast until the next day.

--- 

**34. Will the fast break if phlegm, things coming from the nose, etc., are swallowed?**

Answer: If phlegm, things coming from the nose, etc., do not reach the mouth, the fast will not become void, there is no difference of opinion among the Madhhabs on this. However, scholars have two opinions on the matter of them reaching the mouth and being swallowed: one group of scholars said that the fast breaks by equating it with food and drink. But the other group says that the fast does not become void by equating it with saliva. Just as the fast does not become void if saliva is swallowed.

If scholars differ in opinion, the reliance is on the Quran and Sunnah. If we are in doubt about whether this matter will spoil the act of worship or not, the basis is that it will not spoil it. Based on this, scholars have clarified that the fast does not become void.

However, even if phlegm or things coming from the nose reach the mouth, whether one is fasting or not, a Muslim should spit it out. But to say that the fast becomes void, evidence is needed to establish it before Allah.

--- 

**35. Will the fast be rendered void by tasting food?**

Answer: The fast will not become void by tasting food. But one should be careful that nothing reaches the stomach. It should be done only when necessary. If something accidentally (not intentionally) reaches the stomach while tasting to see if the food has salt or chili, there is no problem, their fast will be valid.

--- 

**36. Will the fast be rendered void by telling a lie while fasting?**

Answer: Telling a lie is a major sin. A Muslim must refrain from it. The fast will not become void by telling a lie. However, there will be a significant compromise to the spirit and reward of the fast.

--- 

**37. A patient skipped fasting in Ramadan. He died four days later. Does he have to fast on his behalf?**

Answer: If it was a sudden illness and that illness continued until death, there is no need to fast on his behalf. Allah says:

''...and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days...'' (Quran 2:185).

This person should make up the fast after recovering from the illness. But if he dies before the situation for that arises, that obligation is waived from him. Because the time when fasting becomes mandatory for him did not arrive. He is like a person who died in Sha'ban. However, if it is an illness from which recovery is not expected, he should feed a poor person for each day.

--- 

**38. A person has one Ramadan fast to make up. But he could not make it up before the next Ramadan came, what should he do?**

Answer: Allah clearly says: ''...So whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days...'' (Quran 2:185).

A person who skipped fasting for a religious reason must make it up according to the command of Allah, the Exalted and Almighty. It is also mandatory to make it up in the same year. It should not be delayed until after the next Ramadan. That is what the hadith of Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) teaches us accurately:

From Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her). She says: ''I would have Ramadan fasts to make up. I could not make them up until Sha'ban due to the presence of the Prophet (peace be upon him)'' (Bukhari).

'I could not make them up until Sha'ban' in the words of Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) indicates that it should be made up before the next Ramadan comes. However, if it is delayed even after the next Ramadan, he must seek forgiveness from Allah, repent, regret the delay, and make up the fast. The obligation to make it up is not waived because of the delay. If it is after the next Ramadan, he must make it up. Allah is the one who gives success.

(Ta'liq: Hadiths whose chain of narration is not correct according to the conditions of Imam Bukhari are Ta'liq.)
