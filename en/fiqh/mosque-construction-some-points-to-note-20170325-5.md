## Mosques are the Houses of Allah

Mosques are the houses of Allah. The very first task the Prophet (PBUH) undertook upon arriving in Madinah was the construction of the **Masjid an-Nabawi**. Similarly, when Ibrahim (AS) arrived from the village of Ur in Babylonia, Iraq, the first thing he did was build a mosque in the village of Hebron in Palestine.

Mosques are not merely places for Muslims to pray. The mosque served as the central hub for all of the Prophet (PBUH)'s activities. While there are many mosques in our country and new ones are constantly being built, this article aims to highlight some important aspects that are often overlooked.

## The Stage of Building a New Mosque

A new mosque should be built in a locality only when it is felt that a new place of worship is essential to carry out social activities based on Tawheed. Furthermore, if land is acquired, it should not be left idle for years. Those who donated the land did so with the intention that prayers would be held there. Therefore, the initial plan should not be a multi-million rupee project. Spend fifty thousand or a hundred thousand to build a shed and start performing prayers and Jumu'ah. A grand building can be constructed later when funds become available. Mosque construction should not be driven by organizational zeal or ego. Only if the intention is to create a center for the religion of Allah will it earn rewards in the Hereafter.

## The Minaret

There were no minarets on mosques during the time of the Prophet (PBUH), nor during the time of the four Caliphs. Minarets were introduced later during the **Umayyad** period. A single minaret, sufficient to identify the mosque among many buildings, is enough. Minarets should not be built for the sake of grandeur.

## Door on the Qibla Side

To allow those in a hurry to leave after congregational prayers, a door should be provided on the Qibla side. This prevents congestion for those who are still completing their prayers.

## Do Not Waste Wudu Water

Even in the harshest summers, water is supplied to mosques for Wudu. If the Wudu area is constructed at a slight elevation, the water can be diverted to a garden or small farm within the mosque compound. Currently, most mosques ignore this and waste the water.

## If There Are Surplus Funds

If there are additional funds, besides the prayer hall, one can build an Imam's room, a guest room, a reading room, a library, an organizational office, and an auditorium. This allows for study classes or camps to be held without disrupting congregational prayers, keeping the mosque active at all times.

## Toilet Facilities

In the current situation, if someone needs to use the toilet, they often have to search for the Imam to get the key. This must change. Toilets should be designed so that one can use them while wearing shoes without having to remove socks. 

There should also be facilities for those who wipe over their socks (Masah) to perform Wudu without getting their socks wet. Since most people wear pants, European-style toilets and hand showers should be provided. The mosque premises should not be overgrown with weeds or littered with plastic and paper. A garden or lawn can make the environment more inviting.

## The Work of Imams

An Imam's job should not be limited to leading the five daily prayers. If the Imam is provided with some work in a place not far from the mosque, his time will not be wasted, and he will earn a respectable income to live on.

If stackable chairs and lightweight desks are available, the Madrasa can be conducted right above the prayer hall.

## Start with a Shed

Do not wait until you have all the money for the aforementioned plans. You can start by building a shed.

The people of **Gaza**, who immediately set up sheds to hold congregational and Jumu'ah prayers even after their mosques are destroyed by intense Israeli bombing, are a model for us in this regard.

The practice of building large mosques in places where there are not enough people to fill even two or three rows (saffs) should be stopped. Construction should be based on actual need.

## Respectable Salary

It is a lack of justice when individuals who do not hesitate to pay thousands in fees for their children's English-medium schools strongly oppose increasing the salaries of Imams and Madrasa teachers. The main reason qualified and educated people do not enter this field is that committees are often unwilling to provide the respect and respectable salaries that Imams and Madrasa teachers deserve.

## Computerizing Madrasa Education

Although computers are widely available, the field of Madrasa education is still lagging behind in terms of computerization and modernization. With a little awareness and vision, Madrasa education can be made more effective and engaging for children.
