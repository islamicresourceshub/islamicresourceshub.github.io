Organization is Not for Division!

പത്രാധിപർ · 2017-02-18 · Dawah & Spirituality

“The believers are only those who believe in Allah and His Messenger and, when they are with him upon a matter of common interest, do not depart until they have asked his permission. Indeed, those who ask your permission, those are the ones who believe in Allah and His Messenger. So when they ask your permission for something of their affairs, then give permission to whom you will among them and ask forgiveness for them of Allah. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful” (Quran 24:62).

Islamic Obligations in Organized Life

In this verse, Allah teaches the Islamic obligations that believers must uphold in their organized lives and organizational affairs. Commentators have stated that the context of this revelation was the time when believers were lining up against the combined forces of the disbelievers during the Battle of «Ahzab» - the Confederates.

Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) and his followers were digging a trench to defend against the enemy. Amidst the urgent work, some hypocrites slipped away from the scene. To them, their personal affairs were more important than the protection of the believers. The true believers, however, would ask the Prophet (peace be upon him) for permission when they absolutely had to leave, and upon finishing their tasks, they would return to cooperate in the work. This is what happened at the trench.

Etiquette of Organized Life

Regardless of the historical context, we see in this the etiquette of organized life. Allah teaches the relationship between a group and its leader, and the importance of cooperating with discipline in organized activities while obeying that leader. This is not just about the discipline the companions were to follow under the leadership of the Prophet (peace be upon him). Everyone who organizes to propagate the religion based on authentic evidence is obligated to maintain this leader-follower relationship. Although organizations and organized activities exist through individuals, an organization has an identity that transcends the individuals themselves.

An organization that follows the Holy Quran and the Prophetic Tradition without compromise has a sacred existence. We are obligated to protect this sanctity.

Ubadah ibn al-Samit (may Allah be pleased with him) said: “We pledged to the Prophet (peace be upon him) that we would listen and obey in times of difficulty and ease, in times of pleasure and displeasure, and that we would not dispute with those in authority, unless we saw clear disbelief for which we had proof from Allah. We also pledged that we would speak the truth wherever we were, and that we would not fear the blame of any critic in the cause of Allah” (Bukhari, Muslim).

The obligations on both sides of organized life are clear from this hadith.

The Importance of Discipline

The Quranic statement that those who believe in Allah and His Messenger are the ones who ask for permission when they are with the Messenger on a matter of common interest emphasizes the importance of discipline. This discipline is the strength of any organized society. If an organization loses this strength, the reality is that even if the individuals leading it change, this weakness will persist. Along with the followers' obligation to obey, the responsibility of the leaders must also be mentioned.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: “There is no servant whom Allah entrusts with the leadership of a people, and he dies while he is deceiving them, but Allah will forbid Paradise for him” (Bukhari, Muslim).

In short, the ideological commitment and integrity of those in leadership positions, along with the obedience of the followers, are the cornerstones of the Islamic organizational system. If these are shaken, the collective will disintegrate. The current developments taking place in the Mujahid organization are what convince us of this. The catastrophic result of trying to shrink an organization into the isolated and logic-based opinions of individuals is terrifying.
