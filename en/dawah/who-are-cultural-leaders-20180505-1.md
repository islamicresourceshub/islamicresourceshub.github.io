## Who are the Cultural Leaders?

My daughter's question, 'Who are these **cultural leaders**?', paved the way for this note. What criteria must one meet to attain the status of a cultural leader? Who elevates someone to the position of a cultural leader? I doubt anyone can provide a precise and clear answer to these questions.

I recall reading **Sathyamurthy** once wrote that one can understand someone is beginning to be considered a cultural leader when their statements start appearing in newspaper dialogue columns like 'Vaachakamela' (Dialogue Fest), 'Marumozhi' (Reply), and 'Kettathum Kelkkendathum' (Heard and to be Heard). It is said that a cultural leader is someone who regularly gets opportunities to participate in prime-time debates on TV channels, or conversely, that one gets the opportunity to participate in channel discussions *because* they are a cultural leader.

- Literature
- Cinema
- Drama
- Music
- Social affairs
- Painting
- Economic-political analysis

Leaders from various fields such as these come together to form the general group known as cultural leaders.

## One Thing is Certain

A cultural leader must be capable of responding. However, no one should assume that everyone who responds becomes a cultural leader. Sometimes, cultural leaders find themselves under immense tension, unable to decide how to react. Sometimes, they might have to respond more to the question of why they *didn't* react to an incident!

## Highly Respected by the Public

This is because they intervene and react to issues that adversely affect the nation and its people, as well as to significant events. This, in turn, draws the attention of the government to those matters.

## Cultural Leaders are the Common Property of the Nation

- Religion
- Caste
- Class
- Party politics

People wish for them to intervene in the problems of the nation and its people, transcending all these categories. And that is how it should be. However, recently, many of them have been observed adopting a culpable silence on several serious developments. Yet, they react to trivial issues. They take sides based on religion and party politics, becoming eloquent in prime-time debates. There are certainly some who believe that the title of cultural leader will only remain on their heads if they continuously criticize Islam, Islamic laws, and Muslims.

To those who dream of being accorded final rites with official honors after death, with some Kerala Police personnel firing into the sky around them, I say that striving to gain a place in people's hearts by standing up for justice and fairness would be the greatest honor.
