The Relevance of Cleanliness

ഡോ.ജസ്‌റതുന്നിസ · 2018-04-28 · Dawah & Spirituality

One of the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ that highlights the divinity and practicality of Islam, and which Muslims have been familiar with since childhood, is that 'cleanliness is half of faith.' Through the teachings of the Quran and the Prophetic tradition revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ—who was sent as a messenger to a people who were illiterate and lacked civilization over 1400 years ago—we continue to realize that even in this era of 'big bangs' in science and technology, the divinity and practicality of Islam, the religion of nature, remain radiant. The Islamic teaching on cleanliness is a prime example of this.

The Quran is not a book of science or medicine, nor is Prophet Muhammad ﷺ a scientist. However, since the Quran consists of the words of the Creator of humanity and the universe, we can perceive scientific and medical lessons when reading between the lines.

Physical purity from apparent impurities is a prerequisite that a Muslim is strictly required to ensure before performing Salah, the second of the five pillars of Islam, for which one is warned they will be questioned first in the grave.

While all people encourage cleanliness, Islam presents it as a mandatory duty. Personal hygiene is desirable at all times, yet there are certain occasions where it must be strictly ensured. Scholars generally categorize personal hygiene into three types: ritual bathing and Wudu, keeping clothing and surroundings free from impurities «najas», and the hygiene lessons related to specific parts of the body that must be repeated at certain intervals (or otherwise), known as «fitrah».

That cleanliness is essential for Salah, the believer's act of submission before the One God, is an instruction from the Creator:

"O you who have believed, when you rise to prayer, wash your faces and your forearms to the elbows and wipe over your heads and wash your feet to the ankles..." (Quran 5:6). Through this verse, Allah teaches the necessity of performing Wudu before prayer.

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah does not accept the prayer of anyone who has broken their state of purity until they perform Wudu" (Muslim).

A believer who performs Wudu while internalizing its sanctity and precision will always have clean hands, feet, and face. Furthermore, the Prophet ﷺ has stated that on the Day of Judgment, these limbs will appear in a radiant form. If there is anything on the limbs that prevents water from reaching the skin, it must be removed first. Water must reach the limbs properly. One must wash through the beard, spread the fingers to wash between them, and clean the nostrils. Not even a small part that needs to be wetted can be left dry.

Major impurity occurs due to sexual intercourse, ejaculation, menstruation, and postpartum bleeding. Therefore, these are instances where Ghusl (ritual bathing) becomes mandatory. "...And if you are in a state of janabah (major impurity), then purify yourselves..." (Quran 5:6).

"O you who have believed, do not approach prayer while you are intoxicated until you know what you are saying or in a state of janabah, except those passing through [a place of prayer], until you have washed [your whole body]..." (Quran 4:43).

We can see that Wudu and Ghusl are encouraged on many occasions through the Prophetic tradition, even when not mandatory.

It is good for a believer to be in a state of Wudu before a non-mandatory bath, before the Adhan, for Sa'i, when leaving the house, or when going to sleep. Optional bathing is encouraged for the Friday prayer, when a non-Muslim converts to Islam, after washing a deceased person, when entering Ihram for Hajj, when entering Makkah, and for the two Eids. Although bathing and limb hygiene are highly encouraged, we must not forget that Islam insists on moderation in the use of water, as in all other matters.

For those living in Kerala, where there is no shortage of water, bathing is part of the daily routine. We should reflect on how relevant the cleanliness teachings of Islam are, given that it prescribed mandatory bathing (in the 6th century) to a society living in the Arabian desert where rain was rare.

'Najas' refers to specific impurities that we generally consider filthy and are instructed to wash away. Examples include feces, urine, blood, pus, and vomit. Liquid intoxicants, pigs, and dogs are also considered Najas. It is a Muslim's duty to perform purification from all types of Najas using clean water. One's body, clothing, home, and places of worship must always be free from Najas.

'Dental hygiene' is one aspect of personal hygiene to which Islam gives great importance. The health significance of ensuring dental cleanliness is noteworthy here. The occasions for which dental hygiene is described as mandatory or desirable indicate that, along with self-care, it is also about maintaining pleasant relations with others as a social being. Aisha (RA) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "If it were not for the fact that I might make it difficult for my nation, I would have commanded them to brush their teeth before every Wudu for prayer" (Muslim).

The Arabic term 'Miswak' mentioned in the Hadith refers to small twigs of the tree known in Arabic as 'Arak', or "Salvadora persica." This natural, antiseptic, and easy-to-use item is still used in Arab countries as a substitute for a toothbrush. Islam strongly encourages dental hygiene before every prayer and Wudu, before and after meals, when entering the house, when going to bed, when waking up for night prayer, and upon waking from sleep.

When Aisha (RA) was asked what the Prophet ﷺ used to do first upon entering his house, she said that he would first clean his teeth (Muslim).

In a Hadith reported by Hudhayfah (RA), it is seen: "When the Prophet ﷺ woke up for night prayer, he would first clean his teeth" (Muslim).

From this, we understand that a believer preparing for prayer to their Lord in the late hours of the night should ensure their mouth is free of bad odors.

The third category of personal hygiene involves matters of natural disposition. In a Hadith narrated by Abu Hurayrah (RA), the Prophet ﷺ said: "Five things are part of the natural disposition: removing pubic hair, circumcision, trimming the mustache, cleaning the armpits, and clipping the nails" (Bukhari, Muslim).

The vast majority of scholars are of the opinion that circumcision is a mandatory act for men in Islam. It can be seen in a Hadith narrated by Abu Hurayrah (RA) that circumcision is part of the tradition of Prophet Ibrahim (AS). The structural peculiarity of the penis—the loose skin at the tip—justifies the need for circumcision. By being circumcised, the accumulation of urine that might remain after urination is prevented. It also avoids conditions where other dirt might accumulate. While Muslims practice this as a religious duty, medical science practices it as a treatment and preventive measure for diseases like phimosis, balanoposthitis, and chronic urinary tract infections. Studies show that circumcision is also a preventive measure against penile cancer to some extent.

In regions with high HIV prevalence, such as sub-Saharan Africa, the WHO encourages male circumcision to reduce HIV transmission. We may have noticed that organizations like The Royal Dutch Medical Association are advocating for legal protection against mandatory circumcision that ignores the child's interest and lacks the service of qualified medical professionals. We, as believers, must ensure that nothing dangerous happens to the child and that it is performed by a recognized professional with practical training. Like many other superstitions, unnecessary rituals surrounding this should also be opposed.

Since cleanliness is mandatory for rituals, removing hair from private parts helps with body hygiene. It goes without saying that if hair growth in the private parts, which include the anus, urethra, and vagina, is not controlled at regular intervals, it can lead to infection. Believers should ensure that the interval for removing hair from these areas does not exceed forty days. Trimming the mustache and letting the beard grow is the Prophetic tradition; it goes without saying that a mustache growing over the lips and into the mouth is not desirable for cleanliness.

Removing hair from the armpits is also part of the natural disposition. Since this is an area with many sweat glands, hair should not be left unremoved here either.

It is also essential to clip and keep nails clean. Long nails that can harbor dirt are not suitable for a believer.

Islam strictly requires that one cleans the relevant parts meticulously after urination and defecation. If the Prophet ﷺ has warned that one will be punished in the grave for not cleaning oneself properly after urinating, it highlights the importance Islam places on cleanliness. It is also desirable to wear footwear when entering the toilet area. Ignoring all this is not just an act that invites disease, but also a sign of disrespect toward the Sunnah.

Along with personal hygiene, we must also keep our surroundings clean with diligence. The Prophet ﷺ has forbidden bad habits that are harmful to our surroundings and, consequently, the social environment.

Throwing waste in public water bodies and public places is a vile act. Islam strictly forbids urination and defecation in water bodies (especially stagnant water), shaded areas where people rest, and on roadsides. We must get into the habit of managing toilets in mosques and public places cleanly.

Those who dump slaughterhouse waste and other household or commercial waste in public places are committing a cruel social harm. Islam has given a strong warning to such people. No act that is harmful to oneself or one's surroundings should come from a Muslim. It is not enough that dirt and waste are removed from us; it should not be harmful to neighbors and society. Acts that make people's lives miserable and cause the spread of disease reflect the lack of civilization in a society. Islam is the religion that taught that even removing an obstacle from the road is part of faith.

The instruction from Allah to the Prophet as he began his mission was: "And your clothing purify" (Quran 74:4).

Behaving cleanly where people gather is also our obligation. In a Hadith reported by Muslim, it is seen: "Whoever eats onions or garlic should stay away from the mosque; let them stay at home." This prohibition exists because the raw smell might be unbearable for others.

We can also see lessons of cleanliness in the manners of eating taught by Islam. Islam informs us that one should eat only with the right hand and that food items should be kept covered. We must note that animals like rats spread diseases through utensils and food.

Abu Hurayrah (RA) says: The Messenger of Allah said: "When you drink water, do not breathe into the vessel; when you go to the place of defecation, do not touch the penis with the right hand. Do not clean yourself with the right hand" (Bukhari, Muslim).

Islam, which provided guidance for all corners of human life in this world and promised eternal life in Paradise or Hell based on one's actions, has indeed given a high status to cleanliness!

"Indeed, Allah loves those who are constantly repentant and loves those who purify themselves" (Quran 2:222).
