# Da'wah: Obligation and Execution

**Sajjad bin Abdurrazzaq**

Linguistically, the term 'Da'wah' means to invite or call people to something, such as a meal. In its technical sense, however, it refers to inviting and calling people to the religion that Allah is pleased with.

If we are among those fortunate enough to have received 'Hidayah'—the greatest of all blessings granted by our Lord—it becomes an obligation upon us as believers to convey the message of Islam to others so that they, too, may receive the light of this guidance. Allah says:

“يَا أَيُّهَا الرَّسُولُ بَلِّغْ مَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِن رَّبِّكَ ۖ وَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلْ فَمَا بَلَّغْتَ رِسَالَتَهُ ۚ وَاللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاسِ” (5/67).

Islamic propagation is, in reality, a deed promised with a magnificent reward. In a Hadith narrated by Sahl ibn Sa'd (RA), the Prophet ﷺ said: “By Allah! If Allah guides one person through you, it is better for you than possessing red camels” (Bukhari, Muslim).

From Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari (RA): “The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Whoever guides someone to a good deed will have a reward similar to the one who performs it’” (Muslim).

There is no doubt that the question posed by Allah regarding whether there is anything better than the act of inviting others to Him serves as a great motivation for a believer to maintain an active presence in the field of Da'wah. Allah says:

“وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ قَوْلًا مِّمَّن دَعَا إِلَى اللَّهِ وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا وَقَالَ إِنَّنِي مِنَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ” (41/33).

Even though there is immense reward in praying at the Masjid al-Haram, the Masjid an-Nabawi, and the Masjid al-Aqsa, the Prophet ﷺ declared in his Farewell Sermon: “Let those who are present here convey this message to those who are absent.” Why did the companions leave their homes and lands, bidding farewell to these sacred mosques, and travel wherever their horses, camels, and donkeys took them for the sake of Da'wah? History tells us it was because they truly grasped the obligation, responsibility, and the reward of Da'wah.

## The Methodology of Da'wah

Islam, which taught that Da'wah is a mandatory obligation and a deed of great reward, has also taught us the methodology for how it should be performed.

Allah says: “ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ بِالْحِكْمَةِ وَالْمَوْعِظَةِ الْحَسَنَةِ ۖ وَجَادِلْهُم بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ” (16/125).

Here, 'Hikmah' refers to wisdom. What is 'Hikmah'? Sheikh Ibn Baz (RA) said: “It refers to clear evidence that reveals the truth and destroys falsehood.”

Islam encourages a methodology of propagation that is based on evidence and authentic sources.

There is no doubt that Da'wah performed without understanding the pulse of the target audience will be futile. Since the audience may be ignorant, Allah has taught us to adopt the method of 'good counsel'—the way a father advises his son—using the Quran and Sunnah. Beyond being an individual obligation, the execution of Da'wah is also a communal duty. Allah says: “وَلْتَكُن مِّنكُمْ أُمَّةٌ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ ۚ وَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ” (3/104).

Sheikh Ibn Baz (RA) says: “It is essential for everyone to perform Da'wah using the abilities they have been given. Looking at a land as a whole, there should be a 'collective effort' for Da'wah activities there. In some situations, Da'wah becomes an unavoidable individual obligation. For example, if there is no one else in a region to enjoin good and forbid evil, then it becomes a mandatory obligation for you.”

Even while imprisoned, the noble Prophet Yusuf ﷺ seized the opportunity to speak to his brothers who came to ask for dream interpretations, teaching them about the oneness of Allah and the purpose of life. He stands as a model for using every opportunity for Da'wah.

## Da'wah: Objectives

 the primary goal a caller to Islam should aim for is salvation in the Hereafter. Allah says: “مَن كَانَ يُرِيدُ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتَهَا نُوَفِّ إِلَيْهِمْ أَعْمَالَهُمْ فِيهَا وَهُمْ فِيهَا لَا يُبْخَسُونَ * أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ لَيْسَ لَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ إِلَّا النَّارُ ۖ وَحَبِطَ مَا صَنَعُوا فِيهَا وَبَاطِلٌ مَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ” (11/15,16).

The second objective is to lead people toward the light. Allah says: “رَّسُولًا يَتْلُو عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ مُبَيِّنَاتٍ لِّيُخْرِجَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ مِنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ” (65/11).

We must seriously remember that if our goal shifts to seeking worldly support, applause, or media attention, we will lose the blessings reserved for the believers who attain Paradise in the Hereafter.

There is a need for callers to arise who can convey the message of Islam to society with conviction, and who can respond to criticisms and attacks against Islam objectively and with evidence, using both their tongues and their pens.
