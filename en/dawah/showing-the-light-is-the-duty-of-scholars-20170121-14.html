The Duty of Scholars is to Show the Light

ടി.കെ.അശ്‌റഫ്‌ · 2017-01-21 · Dawah & Spirituality

The Approach to Those Who Ask Questions

Among humans, those who think will continue to ask questions. People of intellect will never ignore them. Scholars who possess wisdom, knowledge, and a vision for Islamic propagation should provide clear, well-intentioned answers to those who ask in order to learn. Even when a question is directed toward a collective, those in positions of responsibility must adopt this stance.

Do Not Conceal Knowledge

However, if questions are asked merely to mock or engage in sophistry, such individuals may be ignored. After all, the Quran says to turn away from the ignorant. But for those seeking salvation in the hereafter, it is the duty of scholars to provide clarity on the Islamic matters they inquire about. If a scholar withholds knowledge from someone who asks, it is a grave sin. The Prophet (PBUH) has warned that scholars who conceal knowledge will face severe punishment in the hereafter. If there is a difference of opinion on an Islamic matter, the foundation to resolve it must be the Holy Quran and the way of the Prophet (PBUH). Allah says:

"O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result." (4:59).

There is an important point to reflect upon here. The verse first says, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you in a general sense, but when it comes to resolving disputes, it only says to refer them to Allah and the Messenger. It does not say to refer them to the Ulul Amr (those in authority). This means that when a dispute arises, one must return to the Quran and the Sunnah. We must strive to understand, accept, and practice matters just as the Righteous Predecessors understood, accepted, and practiced them based on the Quran and Sunnah. However, not everyone may be able to adopt this policy. Only those who believe firmly in Allah and are conscious of the hereafter can do so. The phrase if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day makes this clear.

That is the stance that leads to a good outcome and is the best. If we return to decisions reached through our own perspectives as part of compromises made for personal, familial, or organizational interests, we will not have a good outcome. Even if it seems beneficial in the short term, it will ultimately prove harmful.

The Model of the Mujahid Movement

The Mujahid Movement is an ideological propagation collective that emerged in Kerala about a century ago. People—both the common folk and the learned—came before that movement with many questions. They did not answer them by saying, "Our stance is whatever the Quran and Sunnah say on this matter, and however the Righteous Predecessors understood it." Instead, they provided answers based on what the Quran and Sunnah actually said regarding the specific question asked. Mujahid scholars adopted this stance in debates and refutations as well. That is how the change we see in Kerala today came about.

Clear Answers are Essential

If a question arises about whether it is permissible to pray to the dead, the answer must be: No, it is Shirk. This must be stated with conviction. If one merely ends by saying, "Our stance is whatever the Quran and Sunnah say on this matter, and however the Righteous Predecessors understood it," the questioner could interpret it however they wish. It will not be clear to them whether they can pray or not. If asked whether it is permissible to celebrate the Prophet's (PBUH) birthday, the aforementioned answer is insufficient. It must be clearly stated that it is not permissible and that such celebrations are Bid'ah (innovation). Only when precise and clear answers are given to questions regarding matters like collective prayer after Salah or the Qunut in the Subh prayer will people abandon Bid'ah.

Is there punishment in the grave? Is it physical? Many have this doubt. It must be explained with evidence that "Yes, it is real and will be experienced." Only then can one live with greater caution, fearing it.

Otherwise, what is one to understand from the reply: "Our stance is whatever the Quran and Sunnah say on this matter, and however the Righteous Predecessors understood it"?

Was the Prophet's (PBUH) Isra and Mi'raj merely a dream, or was it physical? Should those who have studied the evidence not answer that it was not a dream, but physical? Was it not because he believed it to be so that Abu Bakr (RA) received the title Siddiq?

Does Zamzam water have special properties? Is there merit in it? Yes! Authentic Hadiths have been reported on this matter.

Can one pray to the Jinn? No! That is Shirk.

Is Sihr (magic) real? Yes! But it must not be practiced. It is a major sin. It is Kufr.

Against Exploitation

However, the exploitation that happens in the name of these things must be strongly opposed. Various people—so-called healers, astrologers, Thangals, and others—engage in Sihr, amulets, talismans, and fortune-telling. True believers cannot accept any of this. It is the duty of scholars to clarify the reality of these things. When questions are asked about such matters, those committed to the sources certainly cannot just end the conversation by saying, "Our stance is whatever the Quran and Sunnah say on this matter, and however the Righteous Predecessors understood it" without giving an appropriate answer. They can only create confusion and ambiguity. Through such answers, people cannot distinguish between faith and superstition, or between Sunnah and Bid'ah.

Do Not View Questioners as Enemies

If questioners are viewed as enemies, how can propagation work proceed smoothly? Is it just to tell people who come seeking information that "you are asking this to create trouble and division in society"?

Those who think will not stop asking. Do not view them as agents of division. Study the evidence and provide the answers. Move forward in the field of propagation with good intentions, declaring an uncompromising struggle against all forms of superstition and innovation, using the Holy Quran and the Prophet's words as your weapons, and keeping the salvation of the hereafter in sight. May Allah bless us.
