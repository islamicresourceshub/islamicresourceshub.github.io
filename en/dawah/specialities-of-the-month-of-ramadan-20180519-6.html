The Specialities of the Month of Ramadan

അബ്ദുല്ലത്വീഫ് സുല്ലമി മാറഞ്ചേരി · 2018-05-19 · Dawah & Spirituality

The Virtues of Ramadan

  1. The month in which the Quran was revealed:

    "The month of Ramadan is that in which the Quran was revealed, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion..." (Quran 2:183).

  2. Better than a thousand months:

    "Indeed, We sent it (the Quran) down during the Night of Decree. And what can make you know what is the Night of Decree? The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months..." (Quran 97:1-3).

  3. Prayers are specifically answered:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "There are three whose supplication is not rejected: the fasting person, the oppressed, and the traveler" (Al-Bayhaqi).

  4. The devils are chained in Ramadan:

    The gates of Paradise are opened, and the gates of Hell are closed. (Al-Bukhari).

  5. Fasting is a protection against sins:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "O young people, whoever among you can afford marriage, let him marry... and whoever cannot, let him fast, for it will be a shield for him" (Al-Bukhari, Muslim).

  6. Fasting is a shield:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "Fasting is a shield; it protects one from the Fire" (Ahmad).

  7. Fasting leads to Paradise:

    Abu Umamah (may Allah be pleased with him) asked the Prophet ﷺ: 'Tell me of an action that will lead me to Paradise.' The Prophet ﷺ replied: 'Fast, for there is nothing equal to it' (An-Nasa'i).

  8. Fasting cultivates God-consciousness «Taqwa»:

    Allah says: "O you who have believed, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you that you may become righteous" (Quran 2:183).

  9. Fasting and the Quran will intercede:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "...The fast will say: 'O Lord, I prevented him from food and desires during the day, so let me intercede for him.' The Quran will say: 'O Lord, I prevented him from sleep at night, so let me intercede for him.' Both will be accepted" (Ahmad, At-Tabarani).

  10. A special gate in Paradise for those who fast: > The Prophet ﷺ said: "In Paradise, there is a gate called Ar-Rayyan, through which only those who fast will enter" (Al-Bukhari).

  11. Fasting receives immeasurable reward: > The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah says: 'Every deed of the son of Adam is multiplied, from ten to seven hundred times, except for fasting, for it is for Me, and I will reward it (without measure)'" (Al-Bukhari).

  12. Fasting is for Allah alone: > The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah says: 'The servant leaves his food and drink for My sake. Fasting is for Me alone.'" (Muslim).

  13. Fasting causes previous sins to be forgiven: > The Prophet ﷺ says: "Whoever fasts with faith and seeking reward, his past sins will be forgiven" (Al-Bukhari).

  14. Umrah in Ramadan is equivalent to Hajj: > The Prophet ﷺ said: "An Umrah performed in Ramadan is equivalent to a Hajj" (Al-Bukhari). Another report states: "It is equivalent to performing Hajj with me." (Al-Bukhari).

  15. Freedom from the Fire is granted: > The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah grants people freedom from the Fire at the time of every breaking of the fast. This happens every day of Ramadan" (Ibn Majah).

Acts Related to Fasting

  1. Make the intention «Niyyah» before dawn:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever does not intend to fast before dawn, there is no fast for him" (At-Tirmidhi). Verbalizing the intention is not the Prophetic tradition. "Intention is a resolve, it is a firmness of the heart" (Fath al-Bari).

  2. Eat Suhoor, and delay it:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "Eat Suhoor, for there is blessing in it" (Al-Bukhari, Muslim). Zayd ibn Thabit (may Allah be pleased with him) says: "We ate Suhoor with the Prophet, and the time remaining (until the Fajr call to prayer) was only enough to recite fifty verses of the Quran" (Al-Bukhari, Muslim).

  3. Break the fast as soon as the time arrives:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "People will remain in goodness as long as they hasten to break their fast" (Muslim). "The Prophet ﷺ used to break his fast with fresh dates, if not, then dried dates, and if not, then water" (Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi). When the Prophet ﷺ broke his fast, he would pray: "Dhahaba al-dhama'u wabtallat al-'uruqu, wa thabata al-ajru insha Allah" (The thirst has gone, the veins are moistened, and the reward is confirmed, if Allah wills) (An-Nasa'i, Abu Dawud).

  4. Taraweeh prayer:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever prays in Ramadan with faith and seeking reward, his past sins will be forgiven" (Al-Bukhari). Eleven rak'ahs is what is established in the Prophetic tradition.

  5. Seek the Night of Decree «Laylat al-Qadr» and focus more on the last ten days:

    "When the last ten days arrived, the Prophet ﷺ would tighten his waist-wrapper, enliven the night, and wake his family" (Al-Bukhari, Muslim). It is the Prophetic tradition to frequently recite during the last ten days: "Allahumma innaka 'afuwwun tuhibbul-'afwa fa'fu 'anni" (O Allah, You are the Forgiving, You love forgiveness, so forgive me) (Abu Dawud).

  6. Increase good deeds: - Charity - Studying the Quran - Find more time for I'tikaf (seclusion in the mosque).

  7. Strive for forgiveness:

    Understand the gravity of the Hadith: 'May he be ruined, the one who does not have his sins forgiven in Ramadan.'

  8. Do not make the fast fruitless:

    The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting upon it, Allah has no need of him giving up his food and drink" (Al-Bukhari).

  9. Give Zakat al-Fitr:

    It is the Prophetic tradition to provide the staple food of the region to the deserving, at a rate of slightly more than two kilograms per person.

  10. Eid celebration: > Conduct it in the morning. It is the Prophetic tradition for everyone, including women, to perform the prayer at the Eid-gah (open prayer ground). The Prophet ﷺ would eat a few dates before heading out for the prayer.

Who is Obligated to Fast

  1. Those obligated: Fasting is mandatory for every adult, sane, and healthy Muslim man and woman.
  2. Those not obligated: Children who have not reached puberty are not required to fast, though they should be trained.
  3. Those exempted: The elderly who are not healthy and patients with no hope of recovery are exempted. They must provide food for one needy person for each day of fasting missed as an expiation.
  4. Those who must make up the fast: A patient who expects recovery may skip fasting; they must make it up later. It is best for travelers to skip fasting if it causes great hardship; they can make it up later. Pregnant and breastfeeding mothers do not have to fast if it causes them distress; they can make it up later. If they cannot, they must provide food for one needy person for each day missed.
  5. Those prohibited: It is forbidden for women experiencing menstruation or postpartum bleeding to fast. They must skip the fast and make it up later.

Things That Invalidate the Fast

  1. Sexual intercourse: Engaging in marital relations during the day in Ramadan invalidates the fast. It requires making up the day and a heavy expiation.
  2. Eating or drinking: If done out of forgetfulness, the fast is not broken. There is no need to make it up or provide expiation.
  3. Vomiting: If a fasting person vomits intentionally, their fast is broken.
  4. Injections for nutrition: If one takes an injection to reduce the fatigue of fasting or to maintain health, the fast is invalidated.
  5. Masturbation: Any method used for sexual release during the day in Ramadan breaks the fast. However, if ejaculation occurs during sleep, it does not invalidate the fast.
  6. Menstruation or postpartum bleeding: If menstrual blood or postpartum bleeding begins during the fast, the fast is broken.

Etiquette of Breaking the Fast

It is the Prophetic tradition to break the fast as soon as the time arrives.

  1. Increase supplications: Prayers made before breaking the fast are specifically answered.
  2. Break the fast by saying Bismillah: The order of preference for the Prophet ﷺ was fresh dates, then dried dates, then plain water.

Innovation in the Blessed Month?

Preparing a large feast on the 17th of Ramadan under the name of the 'Anniversary of the Badr warriors', along with special prayers and chanting, is a complete innovation. Making vows in the name of the Badr warriors, praying to them, and seeking their help is, in reality, an act of worship «Ibadah» directed towards them. Directing worship to anyone other than Allah is Shirk (polytheism), which Allah never forgives and which leads to eternal residence in Hell.

Taraweeh Prayer

One of the most important virtuous acts in the holy month of Ramadan is the voluntary night prayer. It is known as 'Qiyam al-Layl' (night prayer), 'Qiyam Ramadan' (when in Ramadan), 'Tahajjud' (when performed after sleeping and waking up), 'Witr' (when concluded with an odd number of rak'ahs), and 'Taraweeh' (due to the rest taken after every two rak'ahs).

Just as fasting during the day is a highly significant virtuous act in the holy month of Ramadan, which contains a night better than a thousand months, so too is the voluntary night prayer. The Prophet ﷺ says: "Whoever prays in Ramadan with faith and seeking reward, his past sins will be forgiven" (Al-Bukhari).

The Prophet ﷺ only led the companions in this prayer as a congregation for a few days. Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) says: "The Prophet ﷺ prayed with the companions in the mosque. He prayed on the second day, and people gathered. On the third or fourth day, people gathered, but the Prophet ﷺ did not come to the mosque. When it was morning, he said: 'I knew what you were doing, but I feared that it would become mandatory for you.' This was during a month of Ramadan" (Al-Bukhari, Muslim). Based on this Hadith, Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) later appointed Imams to perform this prayer in congregation.
