Religious Preachers Must Be Exemplary

പത്രാധിപർ · 2017-02-04 · Dawah & Spirituality

The Obligation of Islamic Propagation

From the time of Adam (peace be upon him) onwards, Allah sent prophets to various regions and eras to propagate His religion. While prophethood concluded with the final Prophet, the mission of Islamic propagation has not ended, nor should it ever cease. Allah and His Messenger have made it clear that fulfilling this duty is an obligation upon the Muslim community. The Quran brings glad tidings to those who carry out this task, describing them as those who speak the best of words:

“And who is better in speech than one who invites to Allah and does righteousness and says, ‘Indeed, I am of the Muslims.’” (41:33).

The Importance of Sincerity of Intention

This is a noble task that carries a massive reward. However, if someone engages in religious propagation to gain praise, status, or other worldly benefits, and does so without seeking the reward from Allah, there is no doubt that they will be among the losers.

The saying of the Prophet, “Indeed, deeds are judged by intentions, and every person will have what they intended,” teaches us exactly this.

The purpose of propagation is for people to find the right path, become inheritors of Paradise, and be saved from the Fire. It is a task rooted entirely in sincere goodwill.

The Sincerity of the Preacher

However, the situation changes if a preacher seeks only their own worldly gain or the benefit of their organization. Such a person may feel compelled to twist religious texts, present things contrary to the truth as if they were authentic, or manufacture agendas based on organizational interests and interpret texts to suit them. In effect, they end up presenting a distorted version of Islam.

If an organization exists to protect the faith, there is no need to twist texts, ignore established facts, or struggle to admit and correct mistakes.

The Exemplary Life of the Preacher

If preachers, whom the public considers great scholars, speak of the terrors of death, the horrors of the grave, and the intensity of the Hellfire to move people to tears and encourage piety, yet live without any fear of God themselves—failing to practice what they preach or value Islamic principles—the people will not only despise them but will also begin to doubt the practicality of Islam itself.

Therefore, a preacher who enjoins good and forbids evil must embody those virtues and shun those vices in their own life to serve as a model for others. We can see this as a prominent characteristic of all the prophets. Allah says:

“He said, ‘O my people, have you considered: if I am upon clear evidence from my Lord and He has provided me with a good provision from Him? And I do not intend to differ from you in that which I have forbidden you; I only intend reform as much as I am able. And my success is not but through Allah. Upon Him I have relied, and to Him I return.’” (11:88).

Propagation is Everyone’s Duty

The Quran and Hadith teach us that enjoining good and forbidding evil is not the duty of only the most learned scholars:

“By time, indeed, mankind is in loss, except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience.” (103:1-3).
