# Drawing Near to the Creator

**Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) narrated from his Lord, the Almighty and Exalted: "Allah says: 'If My servant draws near to Me by a span, I draw near to him by a cubit; and if he draws near to Me by a cubit, I draw near to him by a fathom. And if he comes to Me walking, I go to him at speed.'"**

The vast majority of humanity believes in a Divine Being. While there are differences in concepts and beliefs regarding God, there is no one who does not desire to attain divine proximity. However, there are varying perspectives among different religions regarding the methods to draw near to God.

Some resort to vile practices, such as sacrificing humans for divine pleasure or gathering in graveyards to exhume and consume bones or wear them as garlands. Others live as celibates, Sufis, or ascetics, claiming that home, family, spouse, and children are obstacles to spiritual elevation and that it is better to live without them.

Islam, however, does not require any such vile methods to attain closeness to God. It does not ask us to suppress human emotions and thoughts or to sever all worldly ties in order to draw near to Him.

In the aforementioned narration, where Allah informs the Prophet (peace be upon him) of this truth, the motivation to strive toward Allah is evident. The statement teaches us that the more we draw near to Allah, the more He draws near to us.

How can a true believer draw near to their Creator? The Prophet (peace be upon him) says that Allah states:

"...And the most beloved things with which My servant draws near to Me are what I have made obligatory upon him. My servant continues to draw near to Me with voluntary deeds until I love him. When I love him, I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes, and his foot with which he walks. Were he to ask of Me, I would surely give him; and were he to seek refuge in Me, I would surely grant him refuge" (Bukhari).

There are certain acts of worship that the Creator has made obligatory for His servants, as well as voluntary deeds. Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) demonstrated all of these through his own life. A believer's duty is to believe firmly in the One Creator and the other articles of faith, and to engage in righteous deeds while hoping for the Creator's great reward. That is the path to drawing near to Allah. Along with this, one attains the love of Allah. Those who become recipients of His pleasure will not engage in anything forbidden. The meaning of "I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes, and his foot with which he walks" is that they will not use their eyes, ears, or other limbs to do anything that Allah dislikes.

A believer must hold fast to Islamic values in all spheres of life. One is a father, mother, wife, husband, son, or neighbor; each person occupies many such roles. Along with fulfilling duties toward the Creator, one must also fulfill the duties owed to each of His creations. The true devotee—the one worthy of divine proximity—is the one who stands firm against the rushing torrent of evil.
