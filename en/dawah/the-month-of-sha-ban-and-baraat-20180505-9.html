The Month of Sha'ban and Bara'at

ശമീര്‍ മദീനി · 2018-05-05 · Dawah & Spirituality

The Significance and Sanctity of Certain Times and Places

Certain times and periods possess a status higher than others, just as some places and objects hold greater sanctity. This is a fact acknowledged by the Holy Quran and the Prophetic Sunnah; it is a matter upon which the Imams of Ahlus-Sunnah are in complete agreement. However, assigning such significance or greatness should not be based on the decisions or desires of individuals or groups. Instead, it must be based on what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ have taught us—it must be rooted in authentic evidence. Otherwise, what results is the introduction of many new elements into the religion, which is extremely dangerous.

Allah says: "And your Lord creates what He wills and chooses; not for them was the choice. Exalted is Allah and high above what they associate with Him" (28/68).

There are four months that are sacred, during which fighting is prohibited. Observe how Allah criticizes the actions of the polytheists of Makkah, who altered this sanctity according to their own interests:

"Indeed, the postponing of the sacred month is an increase in disbelief by which those who have disbelieved are led astray. They make it lawful one year and unlawful another year to correspond to the number of what Allah has made unlawful and thus make lawful what Allah has made unlawful. Made pleasing to them is the evil of their deeds; and Allah does not guide the disbelieving people" (9/37).

Therefore, we must acknowledge and respect the sanctity and significance of anything that the Quran and Sunnah have established. Simultaneously, we must not assign any religious significance to anything that is not proven by authentic evidence.

Some people attribute special significance to the month of Sha'ban and perform specific rituals and acts of worship on the 15th of Sha'ban. It is necessary to verify whether these are established based on the Holy Quran and the Prophetic Sunnah. If they are not, the deeds we perform, thinking them to be virtuous and hoping for Paradise, may actually be evils that lead us to the Fire.

We find no specific mention of the month of Sha'ban or the 15th of Sha'ban in the Quran.

The Danger of Bid'ah

Practicing anything in the name of religion that lacks the example of the Prophet ﷺ is a misguided innovation. The Prophet ﷺ taught that all Bid'ah—innovations—are misguidance and that all of them lead to the Fire.

Through innovations, one effectively defies and rejects the word of Allah: "This day I have perfected for you your religion" (Al-Ma'idah: 3). Adding anything to the religion of Islam that was not part of it—after Allah has perfected it and the Prophet ﷺ has provided the model—is a grave offense. It is an act of disobedience to Allah and His Messenger, and an encroachment upon the authority of Allah.

Allah says: "Or have they other deities who have ordained for them a religion to which Allah has not consented? But if not for the decisive word, it would have been judged between them. And indeed, the wrongdoers will have a painful punishment" (42/21).

While following the Sunnah taught by the Prophet ﷺ brings unity, brotherhood, strength, and above all, the love and support of the Lord to the Islamic community, the innovations created by humans—often under the guise of "isn't this good?"—result in disunity, fragmentation, and the wrath of the Lord. Therefore, the devil is more eager to have people perform and propagate innovations than he is to have them commit major sins.

We must be vigilant and cautious to ensure that innovations do not enter our lives.

As Imam Malik said, if someone introduces something new into Islam under the pretext that it is "good," they are essentially declaring that Muhammad ﷺ was treacherous in his mission. This is because Allah has already perfected the religion and is pleased with it. Therefore, nothing that was not practiced by the Prophet ﷺ and his companions should ever be practiced as a religious act in any time or place. (For further details, see the book 'Al-I'tisam' by Imam Shatibi).

Matters Related to Sha'ban in the Hadith

However, there are certain matters related to Sha'ban mentioned in the Hadith:

  1. The wives of the Prophet ﷺ confirmed that he used to increase his voluntary fasting during Sha'ban more than in any other month. (Bukhari).

  2. On the night of the middle of Sha'ban, Allah looks upon His creation and forgives the believers, while granting respite to the disbelievers (so they may accept faith and improve). He leaves those who harbor hatred and enmity until they abandon it. Reports also indicate that Allah forgives the minor sins of everyone except the polytheists and those who harbor hatred. (Silsilat al-Sahihah, Hadith number 1144).

  3. There are also reports in the books of Hadith explaining that Sha'ban is a month in which deeds are raised to Allah, the Lord of the worlds, and that people are heedless of it. The Prophet ﷺ stated that he liked for his deeds to be presented to Allah while he was fasting. (Nasa'i).

Ibn Rajab (may Allah have mercy on him), while discussing the Prophet's ﷺ fasting in Sha'ban, noted something particularly significant: Rajab is a month that falls between two months of special importance—the sacred month of Rajab and the month of Ramadan, the month of fasting in which the Quran was revealed. Many people became preoccupied with voluntary fasting in Rajab, thinking it had more significance than voluntary fasting in Sha'ban. However, the reality is not so. The mention that "people are heedless of it" refers to this. In terms of excellence and virtue, some things that are popular among people may actually be less significant than others. The Prophet's ﷺ intention was likely to correct such misconceptions. Hadith indicate that acts of worship performed when people are heedless of good deeds hold special importance and merit.

Furthermore, as mentioned in the Hadith, the Prophet ﷺ liked to be in a state of fasting when his deeds were presented to Allah.

Ibn Rajab also explains that there are interpretations suggesting this was a form of training to prepare for the difficulties of fasting in Ramadan. (For further details, see Ibn Rajab's 'Lata'if al-Ma'arif').

Innovations in Sha'ban

One can see some people performing various rituals and celebrations in the name of religion during the month of Sha'ban. Staying awake on the night of the 15th of Sha'ban expecting special rewards, performing specific prayers, and dressing up as if it were an Eid are all seen in society. None of these have any basis in the evidence. These are all innovations that were unknown to the righteous predecessors.

Prayers such as the so-called 'Bara'at prayer' (a hundred rak'ahs), prayers performed for the removal of calamities, longevity, or the removal of poverty, reading Surah Yasin with specific supplications on that day, the belief that this is the 'blessed night' (Laylatun Mubarakah) which the Quran described as better than a thousand months, and the preparation and distribution of special food on that day are all innovations lacking the example of the Prophet ﷺ. In other words, these are things not taught by the religion of Islam.

Fasting After the Middle of Sha'ban

Hadith have been established from the Prophet ﷺ prohibiting voluntary fasting specifically after the middle of Sha'ban. Abu Hurairah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: "When the middle of Sha'ban has passed, do not fast until Ramadan."

However, scholars have clarified that this prohibition does not apply to someone who normally fasts on days like the Ayyam al-Beed (the 13th, 14th, and 15th of the Hijri month) or Mondays and Thursdays. Similarly, making up missed fasts or fulfilling vows is not included in the scope of this prohibition.

May Allah bless us to understand the religion based on authentic evidence, to live accordingly, and to be gathered with the Prophet ﷺ in Paradise.
