## Peace and Islam

There is no human being who does not desire peace. Every individual is striving for it. Factors such as monotheism, corresponding acts of worship, moral conduct, tolerance, and coexistence must be considered for the attainment of peace. Since **Islam** provides precise guidance for both man's worldly life and the ultimate life in the Hereafter, its teachings on peace are intrinsically linked to both. Consider the message proclaimed by the **Quran**:

> "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Acquainted" (Quran 49:13).

## The Prophetic Mission and the Message of Brotherhood

**Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him)** fulfilled the mission of the final messenger in the chain of prophethood. With the declaration of the final world teacher:

> "Verily, your Lord is one, and your father is one. All of you are from Adam, and Adam was from dust."

People were liberated from the corruption of tribal pride, racism, color prejudice, and economic exploitation. A state was established where people recognized brotherhood, embraced it, and worshipped only the Creator who provided the opportunity for it. One who submits his life to his Creator in this manner is called a `Muslim` in Arabic. Islam is the complete submission to **Allah**; it is only through this that humans can attain eternal peace.

## The Method of Submission to Allah

How and in what ways submission to Allah should be carried out is determined by the One who created us and those before us. We receive these instructions through the messengers Allah chose from among humanity. The Holy Quran, revealed through the final messenger, Muhammad (peace be upon him), and his unparalleled life, are the only sources for mankind to reach the Creator. It is clear guidance entirely from the Creator.

## Understanding the Quran through the Prophet (PBUH)

Therefore, it is a matter of contemplation how Islam, which claims to lead human hearts to the straight path, views the subject of terrorism. Primarily, to understand the Quran—the words of Allah—we must rely on Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). After all, he is the messenger chosen by Allah to deliver this book to us. Allah has entrusted him with the responsibility of explaining these instructions to the people.

> "And We revealed to you the message that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them and that they might give thought" (Quran 16:44).

## The Distortion of the Quran

After this true message, which is a blessing from Allah, has arrived, there is a possibility that those with crookedness in their hearts may take verses from the Quran that are similar in meaning to create trouble among people. See how the Quran itself clarifies this:

> "...But those in whose hearts is deviation [from truth] will follow that of it which is unspecific, seeking discord..." (Quran 3:7).

To avoid falling into the traps of the troublemakers mentioned by Allah, a believer must follow the paths explained by the Prophet (PBUH).

## Teaching to the Human Community

One of the teachings repeatedly emphasized by the Prophet, who never told a lie in his life, is the fact that we are all descendants of Adam. Therefore, everyone is a brother to one another and must maintain friendly relations. The Quran awakens human hearts:

> "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you" (Quran 49:13).

## The Noble in the Sight of Allah

One who is honored in the sight of Allah is he who strictly follows the Creator's commands and prohibitions and lives with piety and fear of God. Allah considers the life of His servant lived with mindfulness (*taqwa*). It is not the material comforts acquired by man that make people superior or inferior. In his world-renowned final sermon, Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) reminded:

> "...O people, your Lord is one. Your father is one. You are all from Adam, and Adam is from dust. The most noble in the sight of Allah is the most pious. An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab, except through piety..."

## Awareness of the Ultimate Goal

It is in the one who realizes the ultimate goal of human life that the aforementioned mindfulness and piety are manifested. This realization is linked to success in the Hereafter after death. Once convinced of this, a person will constantly self-examine whether all their actions are in accordance with Allah's commands. Along with this, they will share this ideology—which is the cause for safety in both worlds—with their fellow beings and invite them to it. This is also because there is a command from the Lord of the worlds to act in this way. (See Quran 16:125).

## The Cultural Tradition of Islam

Islam has a tradition of teaching the world a culture based on monotheism that is deeply rooted in life. The enemies of Islam have universally attempted to tarnish this religion; there are many slanders manufactured as part of this. Human life must be in accordance with divine revelation; otherwise, those who deny the truth will face a state of suffering in the hellfire on the inevitable Day of Judgment. Polytheists and atheists who dislike this truth have always stood together against the believers who preach it with good intentions. The stance taken by a pious believer is to move forward with their responsibilities without faltering. Moreover, a true believer is aware that under no circumstances should anyone be forced into the Islamic ideology. The reason the Quran gives for this is logical:

> "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion. The right course has become clear from the wrong" (Quran 2:256).

That is, a Muslim does not need to force anyone to enter Islam because its goals and proofs are extremely clear, touching the human intellect.

## The Muslim in a Pluralistic Society

A Muslim living in a pluralistic society should not be one who attacks or mocks those whom polytheists call upon in prayer. The Quran mandates that instead of insulting the deities of ignorant people who worship idols, human-gods, or graves, one should help them understand the evil of what they are doing. Allah, who created human hearts, clarifies the reason for this:

> "And do not insult those they invoke other than Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge" (Quran 6:108).

## Correcting Misconceptions

Islam does not allow even a fraction of terrorism. Yet, a group is labeling Islam as a religion that promotes terrorism and Muslims as terrorists. Every Muslim has the duty to correct the misconceptions fostered by such people. A Muslim who knows and acts upon their religion must not deviate from the positions drawn by the Quran and the Prophetic tradition. These teachings are constantly reminded by Islamic scholars. Above all, contemporary **Salafi** scholars are paying more attention to cultivating in the minds of Muslims the thought that impulsive actions and emotional outbursts, whether intentional or unintentional, might stand as obstacles to attaining Paradise. Such teachings are essential to maintain the moderate path and the excellence introduced by the Quran. Surely, this method of instruction brings only good, for it is the stance of the predecessors (**Salaf**), including the Prophet himself.
