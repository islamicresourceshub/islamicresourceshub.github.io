A Solution for Divisions

ടി.കെ.അശ്‌റഫ്‌ · 2017-02-18 · Dawah & Spirituality

Unity, Ideology, and Movement: 2

Unity among those who advocate for peace is essential to counter the terrorism that threatens global peace itself. As fascist forces use the rod of power to dismantle India's lifeblood—unity in diversity—and attempt to confine the country within a monolithic prison, the times demand a broad unity and consensus among Indian citizens who desire peace. The Muslim community must stand together in all possible areas. A joint movement is essential to isolate those waiting to sow the seeds of communal division by undermining the peaceful landscape of Kerala, which remains a safe haven in India. These were the points we discussed in the previous article. Along with that, we also touched upon the developments that have occurred and are occurring within the Mujahid movement. When those who were divided in the name of ideology unite, it is necessary to explain what decisions were taken regarding the issues of ideological disagreement. Yet, no such decision has been announced!

If Decisions Are Not Announced

If decisions on ideological matters are not articulated, many problems will arise. The public may be led to believe that there were no significant issues among the Mujahids for the past fourteen years. They will reasonably suspect that the problems were merely ego clashes between certain individuals.

This suspicion will naturally turn toward those from both sides who took the initiative for unity. It is natural for the public to ask: what was the purpose of the cases taken to the Supreme Court costing lakhs of rupees, the wealth spent on building new mosques due to divisions, and the energy spent on holding separate conferences during the same period?

Why Must Ideology Be Explained?

If ideological clarity and unity were the reasons for organizational unification, then that must be explained. If they have returned to the decisions of 2001, they could simply print the decisions taken back then, distribute them to the branches, and clear up any doubts, just as was done at that time.

Unification without explaining who was wrong will create a massive ideological crisis among the rank and file. It will again lead to rumors and factionalism. It is not possible to wrap up all ideological issues with the single phrase that they have returned to the Quran and Sunnah. Ideology is not something to be wrapped up and kept away; it is something to be propagated in society. To the question of what is wrong with praying to others besides Allah, the Mujahid movement has consistently provided the clear answer that it is shirk—polytheism. If one simply stops by saying the answer is whatever is stated in the Quran and Sunnah, the listener will not understand the point. Similarly, to the question of whether sihr—sorcery—is effective, it is not enough to just say they returned to the sources. The answer must be based on evidence: it is effective, but practicing sihr is a major sin. Unity becomes honest only when it is said that they have agreed upon such matters.

If it is true that they have agreed upon the sources, then how can there be any division if that truth is spoken?

Is It Washing Dirty Linen?

Some confront those seeking ideological clarity with the question of whether it is necessary to dig up the past and wash dirty linen. Since both groups have decided to forget the past and compromise, digging through the trash is not the right course of action. This same stance should be taken regarding personal allegations and counter-allegations; no one disagrees with that. However, how can explaining the decisions taken on religious matters, which were supported by quoting Ayat—Quranic verses—and Hadith—prophetic traditions—be considered washing dirty linen? How does it become washing dirty linen to state that the Madavoor faction's claim that believing sihr is effective is shirk has been corrected?

What happened to the unity regarding the claim that angels and jinn interact with humans through metaphysical means? What became of the claim that ahad—solitary—hadiths cannot be accepted for matters of faith? Was the claim that the punishment of the grave is only spiritual corrected? Was the claim that the Isra and Mi'raj journey was only a dream corrected? Will the claim that Zamzam water has no healing properties continue? Does the claim that the Hajar al-Aswad is merely a stone still persist? Do they accept the hadith regarding Ajwa dates? What is the decision on the subject of the evil eye? Do they accept what the hadith says about the Masih ad-Dajjal? Does the Sirat bridge exist or not? There are many such issues that have been controversial for the last 14 years. They themselves admitted that there were enough topics to discuss for eleven months. Explaining the decision on these is not washing dirty linen; it is clearing the filth.

Is It Not Enough to Say We Agreed on the Sources?

Some ask if the answer to all these questions is not provided by saying they have returned to the Quran and Sunnah. The answer is that it has not been answered. Both those who accept and those who deny the above topics hold up the Quran and Sunnah as their support. If decisions are not stated, both sides will remain firm in what they believed. There will be no change in the existing situation. That will cause massive problems in the future. It can be said that both factions have, in a sense, betrayed their followers. The TP faction gave their followers the message that the Madavoor faction is correcting everything and returning to the organization unconditionally. However, the Madavoor faction has convinced their followers that the KNM is accepting them without any of their positions being corrected. The Madavoor faction explains that there is an agreement to keep this matter secret for two years for the time being. They give their followers confidence by saying this is a temporary compromise like the Hudaybiyyah treaty before the Fath Makkah—conquest of Makkah—and that the final victory will be theirs.

Were There No Differences of Opinion in the Past?

Some think that there were differences of opinion among Mujahid scholars in the past as well, that these did not cause divisions, and that they can move forward while maintaining different views.

  • There are differences of opinion that are permissible and those that are not.
  • It is natural to have different views on mas'alas—issues—without changing the fundamentals.
  • It is the same with errors occurring in matters where there is room for ijtihad—independent legal reasoning.

However, the past controversial issues are fundamental ones. Accepting the sources, the articles of faith, Tawheed—the oneness of God—and the Sunnah are not disputes over mas'alas. They are fundamental differences. The Madavoor faction propagated that the KNM faction were mushriks—polytheists—because they believed sihr is effective. How is that a mas'ala issue? Which scholar of Ahlus-Sunnah has said before 2002 that believing sihr is effective is shirk?

If the organization had not adopted and propagated the isolated opinions of writers or speakers, and if it had rejected the erroneous beliefs in their books when they came up for discussion, that would be one thing. The difference now is that their views are being propagated through official publications and adopted as the official policy of the organization. Even the 'Unity Formula', which stated that controversial issues would no longer be raised on official platforms and publications, was violated by Shabab, Al-Manar, and Vichinthanam after the unity. Through this, we are seeing the strange sight of shirk and Tawheed going hand in hand. Such a situation never existed in the Mujahid movement in the past.

Therefore, one cannot justify this ideologically bankrupt alliance by citing the past. The stance of saying that on fundamental issues, one can take this view or that view, hiding behind issues where there is room for difference of opinion, is not the stance of the Mujahids; it is that of the Jamaat-e-Islami.

Musa Vanimel's Article and the Core of the Controversy

In an article titled 'An Explanation' written by E. Asgarali Sahib and M. Abdurahman Salafi in the Vichinthanam dated February 3, 2017, it was explicitly written that one should not say it is shirk to believe sihr is effective. Angered by this, the Madavoor faction republished an article by Musa Vanimel regarding sihr from the November 1982 issue of Al-Manar in the February 10, 2017, issue of Shabab. By explaining that this article does not firmly state that sihr is effective, they are trying to refute Vichinthanam.

Where in this article does it say that believing sihr is effective is shirk? It was by arguing that believing sihr is effective is shirk that the Madavoor faction accused the Mujahids of shirk for all these years!

They united without changing this argument. The proof of this is the speech Ali Madani gave at the Madavoor faction's council. He explains that it is our belief that believing sihr is effective is shirk, but that it will not be said out loud now and can be said after two years.

However, the article in the February 3rd Vichinthanam is against this. It says that one should not say it is shirk to believe sihr is effective. It also says that there is no difference of opinion among the predecessors or successors on this.

Does the argument that believing sihr is effective is shirk still exist? Who are the predecessors who argued that? KM Maulavi, Amani Maulavi, Dr. Usman, and others all taught that sihr has a reality. At the same time, they taught with evidence that practicing sihr is a major sin.

It is clear that the claim that problems were solved after eleven months of discussion is not true, as a discussion arose on a disputed topic. One can guess the state of other issues.

The current uproar underscores what we said: that unity has not become a reality and that to become a reality, one must return to the Quran and Sunnah. It is not too late. Study the subject based on evidence. Correct the mistakes. Repent for accusing the Muwahhids—monotheists—of shirk and calling them neo-traditionalists. Do not blindly justify a leadership that changes its stance day by day.

Did Asgarali Sahib not write a note in his own name the very next day contrary to what he wrote in Vichinthanam? Did he not say he was resigning? Did the Madavoor faction followers not say he betrayed us?

It is not a new discovery that a few predecessors had different views on whether sihr has a reality or not. The Mujahid organization never saw the denial of sihr as an official position. The proof of this is Amani Maulavi's Quran translation and KM Maulavi's fatwas. Even in the madrasa textbooks, it is taught that sihr is caused by the work of the devil. The core of the controversy is not whether there were different views on the subject of sihr; it is whether believing it is effective constitutes shirk or not. It is not failing to understand what is said in the hadith, but ignoring it because it cannot be accommodated by one's own intellect and logic, that must be called the denial of hadith.

Is This Like a Family Matter?

There is also a tendency to evade those seeking ideological decisions by giving the explanation that this is our family matter and that our workers down to the grassroots level have been convinced of the matter. If the problems in the Mujahid movement were merely family problems, why was this issue discussed in the public sphere for over a decade and a half? If it had only been said in organizational meetings, it would have been enough to explain it only to the organizational bodies when uniting. It is necessary to inform the public of the decision taken upon unity regarding matters that were explained by quoting Quranic verses in every nook and corner of the land. Mujahids have to work among the public. Only when they provide clarity on the issues asked ideologically can they lead them to the true message.
