Jallikattu and the Islamic Approach to Animals

അബ്ദുൽ മാലിക്‌ സലഫി · 2017-01-28 · Dawah & Spirituality

All Living Beings Deserve Compassion

Every living creature on this earth deserves compassion because they are all creations of Allah. Islam, the religion of the Most Merciful, has granted every creature its due rights. The fact is that no other religion has addressed this subject as comprehensively as Islam.

In the Context of the Jallikattu Controversy

In the wake of the uproar surrounding Jallikattu, the traditional event held during the Pongal festival in Tamil Nadu, this article discusses the Islamic approach toward animals.

This tradition, said to have existed since the 400-100 BC period, has become a subject of controversy through court verdicts and legal summons.

Although numerous animal welfare organizations have protested against this cruelty, there has been little significant result. After a two-year hiatus, Jallikattu took place across Tamil Nadu this January 22nd. It had not been held for the previous two years following a 2014 Supreme Court ban. Although the central government granted permission through a special order last year, the Supreme Court stayed it following a petition filed by the animal welfare organization PETA.

However, the situation changed this year. Following strong protests by students and youth, the Governor granted permission for a special ordinance. The President, the Prime Minister, and the central government extended their full support to the Tamil Nadu government's moves. With the amendment of the 1960 central law preventing cruelty to animals, and the new bill passed by the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly on January 23, 2017, by Chief Minister O. Panneerselvam, it is no longer possible to legally put an end to this tradition.

The world witnessed leaders and followers clinging to the tail of tradition to justify this cruelty. Jallikattu is not just about animal abuse; it is also a suicidal 'sport'—a dangerous game that leads to human deaths and permanent disabilities!

Here, Islam has something to say. As a religion of mercy, Islam's stance and views on any matter are based on compassion and empathy.

Animal Rights in the Quran

All creatures on earth are creations that have as much right to live here as humans do. Observe what the Holy Quran says:

"And there is no creature on [or within] the earth or bird that flies with its wings except [that they are] communities like you" (6:38).

Allah also reminds us that all these living beings glorify their Creator and offer praises to Him:

"Do you not see that Allah is exalted by whomever is within the heavens and the earth and [by] the birds with wings spread [in flight]? Each [of them] has known his [means of] prayer and exalting [Him], and Allah is Knowing of what they do" (24:41).

Thus, every living being on this earth recognizes Allah and lives by praising Him. Islam does not permit us to harm or kill them without just cause.

The Quran also puts forward the view that the resources of the earth belong to them as well:

"And after that He spread the earth. He extracted from it its water and its pasture, and set the mountains as pegs, as enjoyment for you and your grazing livestock" (79:30-33).

Allah also mentions that living beings communicate with each other. The Quran (27:18) informs us that the leader of the ants told its followers to enter their dwellings before the army of Sulaiman (Solomon) destroyed them. Through this, Allah highlights their sense of community and obedience. However, the Quranic view is that humans hold dominion and independent operational authority on earth. Allah says:

"And the earth He laid [out] for the creatures" (55:10).

It is a reality that many living beings interact with human life, directly or indirectly. Perhaps that is why Allah mentions certain animals in the Quran, the book for mankind. This is not to teach the biological aspects of animals, but rather because there are social lessons humans must learn from them. It is to teach these lessons and, above all, to provide the motivation to reflect on them, understand the greatness of the Creator, and live with mindfulness.

If we examine the names of the chapters in the Holy Quran, we can see that many are named after animals. Al-Baqarah (The Cow), Al-An'am (The Cattle), An-Nahl (The Bee), An-Naml (The Ant), Al-Ankabut (The Spider), and Al-Fil (The Elephant) belong to this category.

Why did Allah provide so many chapters named after animals in the Quran, which was revealed as guidance for mankind? This is a thought-provoking subject. Humans have something to learn from all these creatures. Do the strength of the elephant, the planning of the ant, the engineering of the bees, and the docility of the cow not give us certain messages? There are mentions of other small and large creatures in the Quran, such as dogs, donkeys, camels, lions, monkeys, pigs, snakes, woodpeckers, mosquitoes, flies, and mules. If Allah has explicitly mentioned these creatures that interact with human life in His Holy Book, there are certainly things we must learn from them. Allah said:

"And indeed, for you in grazing livestock is a lesson" (23:21).

Animal Rights in the Hadith

Islam instructs us to treat living beings with mercy. The Prophet (peace be upon him) instructed that even when one intends to slaughter an animal, the knife must be sharpened well, and the animal being slaughtered must be given comfort.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"If someone shows mercy even to a sparrow he slaughters, Allah will show him mercy on the Day of Judgment" (Sahih al-Jami, Hadith No: 626).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) also warned: "Whoever denies mercy will be denied mercy on the Day of Judgment" (Muslim).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) gave strict instructions that those who raise animals must be mindful of their food.

Once, seeing an emaciated camel, the Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"Fear Allah regarding these animals. Feed them well and ride them in a good condition" (Abu Dawood).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) did not allow the killing of even a single creature without just cause. He said:

"Whoever kills a sparrow for sport, it will cry out to Allah on the Day of Judgment: 'O my Lord, this person killed me for no purpose; he did not kill me for any benefit'" (Nasa'i).

He strongly forbade the mutilation and injuring of living creatures. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said:

"Do not mutilate animals. Whoever does so, Allah has cursed him" (Bukhari, Muslim).

The Prophet (peace be upon him) taught the etiquettes to be followed during slaughter, such as sharpening the knife, providing comfort, and ensuring other animals do not witness the slaughter.

Islam not only instructs us to show mercy to living beings but also teaches that there is a great reward for it. It clarifies that it can even be a reason for entering Paradise. We see the Prophet (peace be upon him) narrating to his companions the story of a person who earned entry into Paradise for giving water to a dog, and another woman who was destined for Hell for tying up a cat and starving it (Bukhari, Muslim).

The Prophet of Mercy, who taught his companion who gifted him a bird chick to return it to its mother (Muslim), left a great message of love and compassion for the world.

These Islamic views are highly relevant to those who find joy in unnecessarily harming animals under the guise of traditional customs.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) even highlighted the gravity of destroying ants with fire. He taught that there is a reward for showing mercy to any living being with a beating heart.

The scriptures teach that mercy, tenderness, kindness, and empathy should be a part of a believer's life as a component of their Iman (faith).

Followers of Islam, the religion of mercy, can never accept animal abuse carried out in the name of tradition and custom. The bulls released into the crowd do not know that people are 'having fun.' When forced to submit, they attack out of sheer terror. There is a high probability of people losing their lives or getting injured by being gored or trampled. Therefore, this activity, which leads to destruction, is not permissible from an Islamic perspective.
