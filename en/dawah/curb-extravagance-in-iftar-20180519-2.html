Curbing Extravagance in Iftar

പത്രാധിപർ · 2018-05-19 · Dawah & Spirituality

The Significance of Fasting

Fasting in Islam is an act of worship that involves abstaining from food, drink, and the fulfillment of desires from dawn until dusk. Fasting prepares believers to guard themselves against evil. There is no doubt that a person who abandons food and drink and controls their desires solely out of respect for the divine will, will experience an increase in God-consciousness and piety.

Since fasting is a magnificent form of worship that subjects an individual to self-control and frees them from base character traits, if some fasting people still lead unrestrained lives and indulge in indecency, they are undoubtedly those who have not touched the soul of fasting nor absorbed its essence.

Trends That Destroy the Spirit of Fasting

Fasting should be able to bring about positive change in one's life. It should influence our speech, actions, and behavior. However, we are currently witnessing a trend where the night is treated as a time to make up for the food and drink avoided during the day. Beyond merely quenching hunger and thirst, many people overeat to the point of exhaustion, leaving them unable to attend the Isha prayer or the Taraweeh prayer as they fall into a food-induced stupor.

Extravagance and Ostentation at Iftar

Providing food for a fasting person to break their fast is a highly rewarding act. Yet, today, many Iftar gatherings have become occasions for extravagance and showing off. For many, the goal is not to earn the reward of feeding a fasting person, but to gain the reputation of having hosted a grand Iftar. Consequently, ordinary fasting people are often excluded from such gatherings, while wealthy individuals—whether they are fasting or not—are invited instead.

It is a reality that some who lack the funds to host a lavish Iftar even resort to taking out interest-based loans to do so. Consider the contradiction in choosing a forbidden path to earn a reward. The practice of laying out dozens of fried snacks and an abundance of fruits at the time of breaking the fast has not been around for very long. Then, after the Maghrib prayer, a variety of dishes are served, including biryani, kabsa, pathiri, porotta, chicken, and beef. In the end, a significant percentage of this food is thrown into the waste bin.

Change is Essential: Practice Moderation

It is essential that a change occurs. We have reached a state where others think that for Muslims, Ramadan is a festival of gluttony. We are the ones responsible for this. Therefore, it is we who must change, along with our mindset. Islam is a religion that commands moderation in everything, including eating and drinking.

Believers should focus more on enlivening the days and nights of Ramadan with worship, rather than on the variety of dishes served at Iftar.
