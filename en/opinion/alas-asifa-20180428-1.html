Why did Asifa receive so much media attention?

Why did the case of Asifa, the girl who was raped and murdered in Kathua, Jammu, receive such significant media attention? Why did the whole world feel such grief and concern? We saw on social media how some Malayali supporters of the Sangh Parivar ideology, who even suggested that her death was a good thing, tried to communalize the issue by claiming she only gained attention because she was a Muslim.

A clear and precise conspiracy and objective

Asifa was not sexually assaulted and murdered as a result of a sudden outburst of emotion by some individuals upon seeing her. It was not a crime committed in a moment of madness by someone overcome by the beauty of an eight-year-old child. On the contrary, there was a clear and precise conspiracy and objective behind her brutalization. The goal was to drive the Muslim Bakarwal community out of the area where they reside. The accused themselves have stated that their objective was to wipe out Muslims from the region. Spreading terror to reap benefits is a tactic of fascists. Parents cannot bear to see their young children harmed. Fearing for their children's future, they would be prepared to leave their homes. This is precisely what elevates the gravity of this incident. This is why everyone with a living conscience, regardless of caste, religion, or politics, condemned it.

"What would the reaction have been if a Hindu child had become a victim like this inside a mosque?"

How many mosques would have been demolished? How many Muslims would have been killed? How many Muslim women would have been raped? Through her death, Asifa has become a storm

The Arabic word «Asifa» means storm; «Asifa» also means one who is grieving. She departed after enduring immense grief and leaving the world in mourning. Through her death, Asifa has become a storm. Millions of people have responded, protested, and prayed for her. They want the accused to be punished. They are prepared to fight legally against her killers. That is all they can do. Alas, O Asifa... Alas... Alas.
