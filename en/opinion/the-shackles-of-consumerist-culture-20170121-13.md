## Consumer Culture: A Trap

We live in a highly sophisticated era. We have all become part of a specific lifestyle and culture known as **consumer culture**. Consequently, there are many tragedies that this consumer culture has led us into.

We no longer have the habit of cultivating our own food. We have destroyed our farmlands. The current state of affairs is that anything can be obtained for money. So, why bother? This culture has lured us into a massive trap. Extravagance and wastefulness have permeated society. Both the rich and the poor have started living life in excess. One could say that consumer culture has forced them into this.

## The Shackle of Loans

People are ready to spend any amount of money for a luxurious life. The mindset of being content with what one has is gone. There is no desire to spend according to one's income. Regardless of how much one earns, the gaze is always fixed on those in higher strata. One must live like them! The house and vehicle must be as premium as theirs! That requires a lot of money. How to get the money? Anyhow! The easiest way is to take a loan. People take loans even when they don't really need them. Banks are waiting, even hosting 'loan melas' (loan fairs).

Why bother traveling by bus? Come, own a car. Money? No money needed; they don't even ask. An ID card is enough. Ha! What an attractive offer!

- To build a new house
- To renovate the existing one
- To tear out tiles and install marble

... There is a loan for everything. Yes, we are buying everything; things we need and things we don't. Thus, we get caught in the web of consumer culture. Interest... interest on interest... a shackle that cannot be broken. Debt upon debt!

## The Gravity of Dying in Debt

If a deceased person had debts and no one else volunteered to pay them off, the **Prophet (peace be upon him)** would show reluctance to lead the funeral prayer for that body. This helps us understand how sinful it is to die while in debt.

Even the **Prophet (peace be upon him)** cannot pay off a debt. For sins to be forgiven, the debt must be settled. Otherwise, **Allah** might not have given the Prophet (peace be upon him) permission to seek forgiveness for them. If someone stepped forward to take responsibility for the payment, the Prophet (peace be upon him) would lead the prayer.

## Debt is a Tragedy

However, there are those who are forced to borrow out of sheer necessity. Allah knows their situation. Allah may forgive them. Debt is indeed a tragedy. A Companion came to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and asked:

> If I am killed in the path of Allah, will Allah forgive my sins?

The Prophet (peace be upon him) replied:

> If you are killed in the path of Allah while being patient, seeking reward, and advancing without retreating, Allah will forgive your sins. Except for debt. **Jibreel** has told me this.

## The Longest Verse in the Quran

There may be times when one is forced to borrow out of necessity. The **Quran** states that such transactions must be written down and that there must be witnesses. The longest verse in the Quran is related to debt transactions. Borrow only what is necessary. Even if it is difficult, one should have the mindset to avoid debt. Do not borrow for luxury or extravagance.
