It is highly significant that President Pranab Mukherjee stated that it is wrong to look at only one side of historical facts to justify the arguments being raised, and that a historian should possess the impartial mind of a judge rather than the mind of an advocate. The President made this statement while inaugurating the 77th Indian History Congress held in Thiruvananthapuram, in the presence of Kerala Governor P. Sathasivam and Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan.

This statement holds great relevance in the context of contemporary India, where cultural and social shifts are being imposed by distorting and rewriting the history and traditions of Bharat. It is not difficult for those interested in history to understand that there are clandestine attempts to reject and push to the margins of the nation those communities and individuals who have contributed immensely to the all-round progress of this great country.

History is, after all, the recording and study of the past. The English word 'history' comes from the Greek word 'historia', which means the recording of one's inquiries and experiments. History is the record not only of human society but of the changes occurring throughout the universe. If this knowledge is not honest, it will be a grave betrayal of entire generations. Furthermore, it goes without saying that creating new versions of history solely to 'convince' others of one's own hollow arguments is an even greater crime. How terrifying it is that such repulsive activities, which ignore the painstaking efforts and intellectual contributions of renowned historians, are gaining acceptance even in intellectual circles today?!

It is a reality that Sangh Parivar centers have started new research centers and released publications aimed at twisting medieval Indian history. These 'oracles' who make new revelations every day are gifting the younger generation hatred and animosity toward the great figures who contributed golden chapters to history. Research is being conducted that passes history on to future generations in a way that disparages Mahatma Gandhi while praising Godse.

The fall of the Babri Masjid, which was a symbol of secular India, was a tragic result of this process that began decades ago. These trumpeters of new history are making futile attempts to portray Aurangzeb, who led an exemplary life by avoiding extravagance and corruption in governance, as a communalist and a religious fanatic. Recently, a book titled 'Ayodhya Revisited', written by former Gujarat IPS officer Kishore Kunal, was released. The author attempts to argue the bizarre claim that it was Aurangzeb who destroyed the Ram temple and built a mosque there. They are punishing Aurangzeb for his efforts to bring about welfare-oriented administrative reforms while standing against corruption based on his faith.

Historians record that it was from the time of the Mysore Sultans' rule that the region of Kerala gained its place on the world map. The history of Tipu Sultan, who fought valiantly on the battlefield with all his might even when betrayed by everyone, is the history of the dawn of modern Kerala's development. He implemented development projects by playing a historical role in agrarian relations and land tenure systems, upholding righteousness and justice, and creating a new history in sectors such as transport, ports, and vehicles. The fact that the Sultan's revenue officials discovered the land held without documentation and the massive tax evasion by the landlord Manjeri Atan Kurikkal, and took action against him without any regard for caste, religion, or faith, is great evidence that the Sultan ruled without any bias. He also holds the reputation of being a ruler who not only exempted temples from taxes but also provided protection to them. However, those who are communalizing history are trying to distort the Mysore Sultans by branding them with the hallmarks of communalism and extremism.

Whenever extremism and terrorism have tried to conquer the world, it is the Salafi scholars of the world who have taken the strongest stand against them. When some who misused the term 'Salafi' formulated concepts of religious states and even formed suicide squads for the establishment of such states, it was the Salafi scholars who imparted the lessons of the true goals of religion: monotheism, universal humanity, peace, and success in the hereafter. Yet, these groups are trying to give them a communal veneer and gift them with the shields of extremism. Through their new research, they are attempting to cruelly distort history and present a community that preached peace, along with its scripture and Prophet, as a hub of terror.

This is where some of the President's expressions become meaningful. His remarks that individuals' inclinations toward country, religion, and regions often influence them even when they analyze history, and that there should be historical analysis free from such biases, are very important. He reminded us that India, which has been peacefully engaging with diverse perspectives, thoughts, and ideas for centuries, does not have a tradition of intolerance; rather, it has a tradition of rational debate. Stating that the freedom to disagree and engage intellectually must be protected, he also urged historians to strive through accurate historical writing to maintain the diversity of India.

Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan's remark that one should be wary of those trying to hijack the Indian Council of Historical Research, and Governor P. Sathasivam's observation that one should fear the marginalization of historical facts, are very relevant. The call by Leader of the Opposition Ramesh Chennithala that society should respond unitedly against the trend of imposing fabricated perspectives on society and misusing even national symbols for their own interests is necessary for the protection of a secular society.
