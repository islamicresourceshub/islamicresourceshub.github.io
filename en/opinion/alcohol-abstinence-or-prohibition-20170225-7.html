Alcohol Abstinence or Alcohol Prohibition?

സുഫ്‌യാന്‍ അബ്ദുസ്സലാം · 2017-02-25 · Opinion & Analysis

More than the debate over whether we need alcohol prohibition or alcohol abstinence, what is required is an inquiry into how to build an alcohol-free society and the sincere efforts to achieve it. Strong awareness campaigns that influence the public mind against the scourge of alcohol are essential. Governments should not make statements that tend to minimize such disasters. Solutions will not come from changing systems or imposing laws alone. Only when people accept alcohol abstinence as a moral obligation, the government formulates strong laws to regulate it, and active awareness campaigns are conducted, can the goal be reached.

Alcohol Debates and the Supreme Court Verdict

'Alcohol debates' are becoming active again as Kerala has requested the Supreme Court not to consider toddy, beer, and wine as alcohol. Kerala has submitted this strange request to the Supreme Court in the context of a landmark verdict issued by the court on December 15. The court verdict states that all liquor shops on national and state highways must be relocated at least five hundred meters away. It must be said that Kerala has adopted an approach that completely ignores the context and well-intentioned stance of the landmark verdict by the three-judge bench led by Chief Justice T. S. Thakur. The fact that the Supreme Court's verdict was issued pointing out the accidents and social disasters caused by drunk driving highlights the humanitarian aspect of the verdict. The relevant part of the verdict is as follows:

"No liquor shops or their advertisements or billboards will be allowed on national and state highways. All billboards must be removed. Whether they are startling or attractive; they must be completely free from all such things. Sight is the primary motivation."

Most of those addicted to alcohol consume liquor from shops seen on the roadside while driving and then continue to drive. The court observed that this causes a large number of accidents. One and a half lakh road accidents are reported in a year. The court also observed that if the cause of these accidents is examined, alcohol can be found to be the main 'culprit'.

Kerala's Stance and the History of Prohibition

In Kerala, the number of liquor shops to be relocated is 180. The verdict states that they must be relocated before March 31. However, Kerala has so far moved only 25 liquor shops from the roadside. 155 more shops need to be moved. It is in this context that Kerala approached the Supreme Court last week with new demands. The main demands submitted by Kerala are:

  • Exempt five-star hotels in Kerala from this.
  • Exempt liquor shops established by the Beverages Corporation and Civil Supplies Corporation within municipal corporation limits.
  • Allow one more year for relocating others.
  • Direct the central government to compensate for Kerala's financial loss.
  • Exclude toddy, wine, and beer from the definition of alcohol.

The Supreme Court also put forward the observation that state governments have a constitutional obligation to prohibit the sale of liquor. Those who generally criticize prohibition say that prohibition produces the opposite effect. They point to the experiences of states like Gujarat, Nagaland, the arrack ban in Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Haryana, and Mizoram for this. At the same time, Nitish Kumar's introduction of alcohol prohibition in Bihar and the public support it received are noteworthy.

Constitutional Obligation

The Supreme Court's observation that alcohol prohibition is a constitutional obligation makes it clear that governments cannot completely stay away from prohibition. Article 47 of the Directive Principles of the Constitution suggests the implementation of alcohol prohibition. According to the Bombay Prohibition Act of 1949, the then Chief Minister of Bombay, Morarji Desai, introduced alcohol prohibition there. However, protests against the law became widespread, and liquor smuggling increased uncontrollably. In 1977, the Morarji Desai government also attempted prohibition and failed.

Gandhiji's Perspective

It was the British who caused the spread of alcohol in India. Realizing this, Gandhiji wrote in Young India in 1931:

"If I were appointed the dictator of India for even one hour, the first thing I would do would be to close all the liquor shops in the country without any compensation."

Alcohol Abstinence and the Islamic View

It is still unclear what the Kerala government means by the concept of alcohol abstinence. Those who wish to eradicate alcohol do not see the demand for prohibition as a religious concept. Instead, it is because of the realization that it is the mother of all evils that cause great social disasters that strong popular agitations and protests are happening against it. We must be able to communicate with minds numbed by alcohol. In this regard, the observation of the noble Aisha (RA) is very relevant:

"If the first thing to be revealed in the Quran had been 'Do not drink alcohol', they would have said, 'We will not give up alcohol'. If it had been 'Do not commit adultery', they would have said, 'We will not give up adultery'. I was a young girl playing in Makkah when the verse (reminding of the Hereafter) 'The Hour is their appointed time, and the Hour is more grievous and more bitter' was revealed. Surah Al-Baqarah and An-Nisa (which teach Halal and Haram) were revealed only after I joined him (in Madinah)." (Bukhari).

This makes it clear that complete alcohol abstinence is possible only through strong awareness campaigns, following the priorities of propagation, by teaching what needs to be taught first. In the end, there was only a need for the announcement that alcohol had been prohibited. They gave it up completely. There was only a need for the question: 'Fahal antum muntahun?' (Will you not desist?). They said: 'Intahaina, intahaina' (We have desisted, we have desisted).

Instead, it is shameful that the responsible government is using futile efforts and twisted logic to escape from the real goal by trying to patch holes with darkness. By forming collectives of all movements, individuals, and voluntary organizations working against alcohol, awareness against alcohol must be strengthened. Let us work together sincerely and unitedly for an alcohol-free society through correcting mistakes and efficient awareness.
