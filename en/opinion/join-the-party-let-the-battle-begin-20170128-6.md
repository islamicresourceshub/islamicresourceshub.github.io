## Kannur Politics in National Media

Kannur politics has once again made its way into the national media, leaving a scar on the conscience of the Malayali people.

One can only guess the mental state of those who chose to mark such a heinous act at a time when the city was hosting the largest **youth festival** in Asia. Perhaps the funeral procession carrying a corpse through the festival grounds was what earned them the 'A-grade' in this instance.

Every time such an incident occurs, we all try to console ourselves by thinking this will be the last. Yet, defying all expectations, they compete to fly flags stained with blood. The series of murders taking place in Kannur and other parts of Kerala prove that, culturally, modern humans have not progressed much beyond the ancient Stone Age people who passed down hatred and vengeance through generations.

## The Unwritten Law of Parties

Watching the maneuvers of many parties, it feels as though there is an unwritten law that once you join a party, you must prepare to battle against other parties and declared enemies. Those at the helm of many organizations seem to believe that they can grow their party by distributing hatred and animosity, both wholesale and retail.

One cannot blame any single political party for fostering **violent politics**. Almost all parties are at the forefront of declaring their strongholds as **'party villages'** to eliminate any opposing presence. The decree is that not even a kite should fly over the party flag.

Hundreds of human lives are becoming victims of the earth-shattering commands of aging political masters.

There should be no room for dangerous leaders who call for instant retribution or turn police stations into powder kegs. The fact that they need one or two martyrs or sacrifices a year to gain political mileage speaks volumes about the 'agenda poverty' of the existing parties. It is through the case funds squeezed from the poor that their 'Mudakozhi hills' and luxury hideouts for criminals are maintained.

## The Wails of Lamenting Mothers

 the wails of lamenting mothers and the cries of young children are echoing in our ears. For every statue erected on the streets for birds to perch upon, there are twice as many living corpses—people who have lost their mobility, had their limbs severed, and are forced to lead lives akin to the dead.

Leaders must demonstrate maturity and integrity to recognize the far-reaching disasters caused by the violent politics that continue to be a curse upon our land. The consequences of this politics of riot are numerous, ranging from the public's aversion to the policies of existing political parties to the growing trend of apoliticism among the youth and the educated. The strikes and administrative paralysis that follow as side effects of violent politics, along with the resulting economic losses, are immeasurable.

## Women and Voters

For the past several years, women have been the biggest victims of the political and religious theatrics taking place across Kerala. If housewives organize, such malpractices can be eliminated to a great extent. If mothers firmly decide that the children they gave birth to are not 'cannon fodder' to be sacrificed for someone else's cause, then deaf ears will hear and blind eyes will see.

If citizens also realize that **voters** are not merely machines to press a button once every five years, a secure future awaits us.
