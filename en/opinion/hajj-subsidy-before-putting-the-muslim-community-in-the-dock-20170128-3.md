There is an English expression, *'Weeping Boy'*. In the 16th century, this referred to a strange practice where a commoner's child was appointed to receive the punishment intended for a prince whenever the royal heir was lazy or misbehaved in his studies. The arbitrary stances taken by the administration in contemporary events often remind one of these 'weeping boys'—those who must endure the lashes and scolding meant for others.

## The New Hajj Subsidy Controversy

I am referring to the new Hajj subsidy controversy. Every year, as the Hajj season approaches, two banner headlines appear in the media: one welcoming the pilgrims (*'Welcome to the Hajjis'*) and the other demanding the *'Abolition of the Hajj Subsidy'*. The proponents of the latter demand change over time; sometimes it is a Sangh Parivar organization, other times it is a directive from a Union Minister or a Supreme Court judge. Most recently, this was voiced by the respected Minister of Local Self-Government and Minority Welfare of Kerala, who is in charge of the Hajj Committee and the Waqf Board. This is not the first time he has expressed such an opinion; he simply took the statement he made at a Hajj camp last year and repeated it at a press conference in Delhi.

![Hajj Subsidy: Before Putting the Muslim Community in the Dock](/assets/hajj-subsidy-before-putting-the-muslim-community-in-the-dock-20170128-3/28Jan17-03.jpg)

The Minister's opinion is not actually personal. He was merely expressing support for the recommendations of a six-member committee appointed by the Central Government, following the 2012 Supreme Court judgment by a bench comprising **Justice Aftab Alam** and **Justice Ranjana P. Desai**, which directed that the Hajj subsidy be gradually reduced and completely abolished by 2022.

> "If a person does not consent, their money should not be used for someone else's pilgrimage subsidy. Hajj is only for those who have the wealth and health to perform it. It should not be done using someone else's wealth."

While one need not question the sincerity of the Minister's explanation, it is a reality that his motivation remains unclear when he reacts to every controversy that puts the Muslim community in the dock—be it the *Nilavilakku* (traditional lamp), Sabarimala visits, police beards, or the naming of ministerial bungalows—with an attitude of *'Did you hear a voice different from the rest?'*

## The History of the Hajj Subsidy

The Saudi government provides facilities for pilgrims via three routes: sea, air, and road. From India, travel has historically been by sea and air. After 1995, the government discontinued the sea service and focused on streamlining air travel.

The Hajj subsidy began during the colonial era. It was introduced with the *Port Haj Committee Act of 1959*, which continued the concessions granted from the public exchequer to those traveling via the ports of Bombay and Calcutta, a practice that started in 1932. After independence, the government simply continued these British-era concessions through the 1959 Act. The truth is that there was no external pressure or 'Muslim appeasement' behind this.

What is a subsidy? Do all Hajj pilgrims receive it? Many believe so. In reality, only a small percentage of the hundreds of thousands who go for Hajj each year are eligible to go through government channels, and the selection process is quite difficult. It is only these pilgrims who are said to receive a government subsidy, and this is limited strictly to airfare. All other expenses—food, accommodation, luggage—must be borne by the pilgrims themselves. The question that needs examination is whether this subsidy is for the pilgrims or for the airline.

## Air India Appeasement Under the Guise of Hajj Subsidy

If one digs into the details of the Hajj subsidy, what emerges is 'organized plunder' by the government to save **Air India**, which is spiraling into losses. The Government of India has unilaterally granted Air India the right to operate Hajj services. A study of this arrangement—made without global tenders or consideration of rates from other airlines—reveals the exploitation taking place in this sector.

This exploitation begins with the 'throat-slitting' practices carried out under the guise of the 'season'. For example, in February 2017, a passenger traveling 4,063 km from Karipur Airport to Jeddah would normally pay ₹14,126. However, during the Hajj season, this rate is hiked sharply. In 2010, it was ₹47,675, and in 2011, it was ₹54,800. The government provides a subsidy to Air India from this amount. Even if the subsidy is reduced, the ticket price remains significantly higher than non-Hajj season rates. In short, for the government, the Hajj subsidy is merely a risk-free transaction of moving money from the shirt pocket to the pants pocket!

## Daylight Robbery in the Name of Subsidy

We have read above the reality of the subsidy controversy that arises every Hajj season. If one investigates the allegations that 'undeserved benefits were taken', 'tax money of other religious believers is being used to fund others' pilgrimages', and that 'the face of a secular nation has been tarnished', this is the fact we arrive at.

Instead of deliberately ignoring such rhetoric—which is chosen to keep innocent cadres alert in branch classes and night workshops—it would be appropriate to respond constructively. The statement by All India Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen (AIMIM) President **Asaduddin Owaisi** MP, that the subsidy should be abolished and the funds used for the educational progress of Muslim girls, is very significant in this regard.

The propaganda that only Muslim pilgrims receive a subsidy is completely false and malicious. The reality is that the government provides subsidies for travel expenses to all pilgrimage centers outside India.

Communist leader **Comrade P.K. Premnath** has proven, using RTI documents, that the government provides subsidies for travel to the Hindu pilgrimage site of Mansarovar in Tibet and the Christian holy city of Jerusalem. In 2014, 1,042 Indian pilgrims, including 42 from Kerala, performed the pilgrimage to Mansarovar. Furthermore, the Central Government allocates significant funds for events like the Kumbh Mela and the Amarnath Yatra. The late **J. Jayalalithaa** also gained much attention for a parliamentary speech where she countered communalists trying to stir up emotions in Tamil Nadu over Hajj benefits by listing the benefits received by other religious groups.

In short, subsidies are not government charity; they are projects undertaken by the government with multifaceted interests in mind. Whether they understand this or not, representatives should remember that trying to stitch a secular cloak at an inappropriate time will only make them look ridiculous. Those who stand against fascism do not expect from secular-minded leaders what they hear from communalists in the country—even if those statements are about their own community.

## Proposals Regarding the Subsidy

Given that the Central Government has tasked a six-member committee to formulate a practical plan to implement the Supreme Court's directive to gradually reduce and eventually abolish the Hajj subsidy by 2022, the suggestions from the Muslim community—the so-called beneficiaries—are highly relevant.

### A. Make the Alternative System Efficient

If the justification for the new court order—which overturned the ruling by a bench including senior judges like **Markandey Katju** that the subsidy should not be abolished—is sincere, then practical steps must be taken to divert the funds to educational scholarships for female students, as they have proposed. Often, alternative suggestions end up as mere platitudes and are never implemented. The government should not adopt such an approach in this new matter. The court should establish a mechanism to monitor this directly and precisely.

### B. Resume Sea Service

The reason the government cited for its interest in continuing benefits for air-based Hajj pilgrimage was the discontinuation of the sea route, which had been an international service until then. With the widespread availability of luxury ships equipped for long-distance travel, sea travel facilities should be resumed alongside air travel. As the Minister and others have said, 'Hajj is mandatory only for those with wealth and health', but the reality is that the majority of those performing it today are people who struggle to make ends meet. For those who have saved every penny since realizing the importance of Hajj in their religion, despite having no means to make ends meet, this ship service would certainly be a welcome relief.

### C. Empower the Hajj Committee to Call for Global Tenders for Air Services

Currently, the right to decide on Hajj flights is limited to Air India. Either Air India or the airlines they designate provide the travel facilities. This means the government provides a massive subsidy to Air India even for the services of other airlines departing from India. And this is at the rates set by Air India. If Air India's Hajj rates, which are three times higher than normal airfares, are not 'profiteering' as alleged, then they should prove their sincerity by calling for global tenders. Given the religious importance, and with companies like Saudi Airlines competing to provide travel facilities at lower rates during this season, it is certain that better service and decent rates can be found without difficulty.

### D. Make the Accounts Transparent

There is an allegation that there is a huge gap between the amount set aside for the Hajj subsidy and the figures at the time of its transfer. If this is true, then those involved are committing daylight robbery. Some figures recently posted online by an IPS officer from Gujarat are shocking. The figures are as follows:

- Amount a Haji pays to the government for Hajj: ₹1,85,000
- Amount the Hajj Committee returns to the Haji upon reaching Saudi: 2,100 Riyals or ₹38,000
- Amount remaining with the Hajj Committee per Haji: 1,85,000 - 38,000 = ₹1,47,000
- Other expenses incurred by the Hajj Committee (approx.):
  - Accommodation in Makkah: ₹50,000
  - Accommodation in Madinah: ₹20,000
  - Air ticket (non-exploitative normal rate): ₹28,000
  - Other expenses: ₹25,000
- Total cost for one Haji's Hajj: ₹1,23,000
- Amount the Hajj Committee gets from a Haji after deducting all expenses: 1,47,000 - 1,23,000 = ₹25,000
- Hajj subsidy provided by the Indian Government: ₹691 crore (2016 figure)
- Total Hajjis: 136,000
- 691,00,00,000 / 136,000 = ₹50,808
- Adding the subsidy per Haji and the remaining amount after expenses:
  - 50,808 + 25,000 = ₹75,808
- Total amount remaining after deducting expenses for all Hajjis plus the subsidy:
  - 75,808 * 136,000 = 10,30,98,88,000 (One thousand thirty crore)!

Where are the accounts for the remaining ₹339,98,88,000?!

If this is true, we need to consider whether the farce being played in the name of subsidy should continue. If these figures are not entirely objective, the authorities must release accurate data. All other information is transparently available at www.hajcommittee.gov.in, and the details of this could also be made available to the public. For Hajjis who choose to go on pilgrimage after clearing all liabilities on their movable and immovable properties, transparency in this area would be a great relief.

### E. Avoid Five-Star Hajj 'Excursions'

Every year, there is a practice of taking many people for Hajj under VIP consideration as part of a government goodwill delegation. Often, those chosen for this trip are people who do not understand the purity of Hajj or grasp its soul. Moreover, it is a reality that many of them manage to get on this list every single year. The question of how many of these people, who show great interest in 'serving' the Hajjis, actually understand their responsibilities is worth pondering. According to the figures presented in the Rajya Sabha on August 8, 2013, in response to question number 507, 500 seats are reserved annually in this manner:

1. President: 100 seats.
2. Vice President: 75 seats.
3. Prime Minister: 75 seats.
4. Minister of External Affairs: 50 seats.
5. Hajj Committee: 200 seats.

If one examines the list of people who went under the President's Hajj quota in 2016, it can be seen that 4 people each from 5 families, 3 people each from 5 families, and 2 people each from 28 families performed the Hajj under VIP consideration.

With enough staff already present at the Indian Embassy in Saudi Arabia for the welfare of the Hajjis, the relevance of parading so many people with full arrangements needs to be reconsidered.

## Conclusion

When I studied the Hajj subsidy in the wake of the usual controversies that have arisen, the information I gathered was shocking.

> "The drum gets the beating, and the drummer gets the money."

This state of affairs must end. At a time when the Muslim community needs to loudly declare that it is not prepared to be a 'weeping boy' to take the blows for the faults of others, political leaders with foresight should not be among those who dream of finding a place on the secular list by speaking against their own community.

What the country needs is not fake secularists who hide their own beliefs to play the 'good boy' in front of people of other faiths. Rather, it needs a broad-mindedness based on principles—where one maintains religious thoughts in their own life and external symbols, while also providing opportunities for followers of other religions and ideologies to maintain their stances, and working alongside them for their rights. Only through this can the noble concept of 'Unity in Diversity'—where we move forward by getting closer, knowing, learning, and correcting each other—be realized. Praying for such a better tomorrow.
