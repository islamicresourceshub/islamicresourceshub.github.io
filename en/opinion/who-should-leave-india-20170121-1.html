Who Should Leave India?

എസ്‌.എ റിയാദ്‌ · 2017-01-21 · Opinion & Analysis

On the List of Anti-Nationals

For some time now, a voice has been frequently heard in our country: that such-and-such people should leave India and go to Pakistan. Now, BJP Kerala General Secretary A.N. Radhakrishnan has sparked a new controversy. Although Radhakrishnan's statement was aimed at director Kamal, it is not just Kamal; everyone who criticizes the projects of the Sangh Parivar falls into their prepared list of anti-nationals. The gravity of the issue is highlighted by the fact that even M.T. Vasudevan Nair was subjected to threats from the Sangh Parivar for the sole reason of criticizing Modi over the currency ban. Regarding the Jnanpith awardee M.T., a Sangh Parivar supporter wrote that it would be better for the Nalukettu author to live in Pakistan from now on. Attempting to brand everyone who has openly opposed the social evils created by priestly religions in Kerala's social life as traitors is nothing but creating thickets of weeds over the renaissance the country has achieved.

India is a Full Democracy

Every person has the right to express agreement or disagreement with an individual's views and observations. As long as the Indian Constitution grants freedom of speech to its citizens, anyone can hold personal views. Everyone has the freedom to criticize such views. The Prime Minister, other ministers, the judiciary, and the bureaucracy are not beyond criticism. India is a country where a fine was imposed on an official for not providing information to a person who filed an RTI application inquiring about the Prime Minister's educational qualifications. India is a full democracy that does not assign caste or religious considerations, where one has the right to know, speak, and criticize.

The True Interpretation of Nationalism

But how absurd it is to shout that everyone who does not agree with one's views should leave the country, and to portray all dissenters as spokespersons for Pakistan! They are providing a false interpretation that true nationalism is blindly accepting everything the Sangh Parivar says. True nationalism, based on patriotism, is working sincerely together for the development and progress of a nation. Those who have enjoyed the fruits of their own labor while their fellow citizens stood in queues enduring sun and rain, and who have cast patriotism to the wind, have misunderstood that the true interpretation of nationalism is to insult, weaken, and destroy other nationalities. True nationalism should perform the noble mission of connecting people, not building walls between human hearts.

Those Who Do Not Know the Soul of India

When examining the history of the great nation of India over the millennia, it becomes clear to anyone that it is not a region belonging to any one religious group. Pluralism has always existed in India. Even while manifesting diversity and differences, India was guided by the wise thought of being a single people without assigning supremacy to any one religion. The country has never embraced unified civilizations or cultures. Religions, cultures, languages, customs, rituals, food, and attire have been different for Indians since ancient times. Those who propagate that uniformity in all fields is progressive are those who have not touched or known the soul of India.

Communalism Does Not Suit India at All

If pluralism and tolerance are what uphold India's self-respect, then communalism is something that does not suit India at all. Communalism is the refusal to acknowledge the existence of those who do not belong to one's own group, infringing upon their freedom, or wishing for their destruction. Any group has the right to organize itself. But that should not be for the annihilation of other groups. Here, those who shout that those who do not accept their opinions should leave India are promoting a form of communalism that is unfamiliar to India. In reality, those who carry this argument, which India has rejected, are the ones most qualified to leave India.
