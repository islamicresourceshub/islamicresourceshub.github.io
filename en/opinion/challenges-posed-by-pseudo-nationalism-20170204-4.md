## Nationalism and Humanity

An emotional connection to one's land, fellow citizens, and symbols associated with the nation is a natural human quality. Nationalism, or national consciousness, is a unique emotional state where the loyalty and sense of unity that form among a nation's people based on this natural awareness come together. However, any person of sound mind understands that being born in or living in a particular region does not inherently grant one superior human qualities or excellence. What makes a human being human are their humanitarian virtues. Only by upholding the inviolable laws of the Creator and acquiring noble qualities can one achieve love for fellow beings and liberation from evil.

Humanity is a term that synthesizes the harmony, emotional connection, and coexistence that should exist between people based on divine messages. Humanity, when interpreted through such a perspective, knows no geographical boundaries. The service rendered by a wealthy person in New York for a fellow human being, moved by the plight of a starving, dying child in a remote part of Africa, is an example of humanity. Therefore, nationalism, as a component of the fundamental quality of humanity, signifies a consciousness that desires the well-being of one's own land, its people, and its symbols. Someone who understands nationalism in this way will have no difficulty embracing and respecting the nationalism of other nations. However, attempting to impose symbols, signs, and codes considered nationalistic onto others—merely to project the appearance of respecting one's own nation—is, in reality, contrary to the message of nationalism.

## The National Anthem and Controversies

This is where the controversy surrounding the national anthem becomes relevant. Every country holds its national anthem dear. As far as India is concerned, the national anthem was composed by weaving together the various regions and cultures of the country. Only the first stanza of the five-stanza song is known as the national anthem today. At the same time, there were controversies regarding the content of *Jana Gana Mana* even at the time of its composition. During the Calcutta session of the **Indian National Congress** on December 27, 1911, there were criticisms regarding phrases like *Bhagya Vidhata* used by **Rabindranath Tagore**. Critics argued that expressions like *Jana Gana Mana Adhinayaka Jaya He Bharata Bhagya Vidhata* (meaning: Victory to the ruler of the minds of all people, the one who determines the destiny of India) were intended for King George V of Britain. However, the controversy ended once Tagore himself clarified that he was referring to the God of the entire universe.

According to Article 51A of the Constitution, respecting the national anthem and the national flag is a fundamental duty of citizens. It is on this basis that the national anthem is sung in public educational institutions and other places. However, the Constitution does not state anywhere that the national anthem must be sung on specific occasions or ceremonies. People with common sense understand that patriotism and national consciousness are not meant to be imposed; they are duties that a citizen owes to the nation based on their loyalty. This is why the **Supreme Court** itself clarified that there is the freedom to sing or not to sing the national anthem.

## The Bijoe Emmanuel Case and Legal Aspects

On July 26, 1985, the Supreme Court delivered a verdict regarding the expulsion of some students from a school in Kottayam district for not singing the national anthem; they belonged to the Christian denomination known as Jehovah's Witnesses. The students' justification was that they could not emotionally embrace anything other than Jehovah. The fact that the Supreme Court verdict sharply criticized the High Court and lower courts that had upheld the expulsion is something that must be viewed with great seriousness. The Indian pluralistic society must also understand that in this case, known as the *Bijoe Emmanuel Case*, the Supreme Court was interpreting the perspectives of freedom of expression and secularism enshrined in the Indian Constitution. Forcing people to sing the national anthem even when they are in a state of agitation cannot be seen as respect for the anthem. The issue is not whether one should stand up while the national anthem is being played. The Supreme Court's observation that the national anthem must be played before movies in theaters is the exact opposite of the verdict in the aforementioned Bijoe Emmanuel case. The controversies regarding the implementation of Hindi in Tamil Nadu, despite it being the national language, are well known. In the Tamil land, which has a long tradition dating back to the Sangam era, minds were not prepared to accept Hindi, the national language, more than their mother tongue. All this indicates that when India is a hub of various religions, languages, beliefs, and cultures, there is little relevance to compulsions in the name of nationalism.

## The Rise of Fascism

Issues like the Uniform Civil Code, demonetization, and beef have taught us one fact: there is a highly alarming situation where citizens are forced to accept everything without questioning, without even inquiring whether the things decided by the government have legal protection, and where citizens become ready to obey anything, losing their capacity for reaction. This is a sign that fascism has taken hold. A recent incident at the State School Arts Festival in Kannur, noted by the media, is an indication of this. When all eighteen teams participating in the band competition finished their performance and played the national anthem, the audience stood up. This was repeated eighteen times. In previous years, the audience did not give this any importance beyond it being a competition item. No one thought that such a performance, which was part of a competition, required such respect. Now, the audience is ready to stand and show respect without anyone forcing them and without any court directive. It may be the fear of interference by the Sangh Parivar that prompted the audience to do this. The incident where fascists attacked those who did not stand in theaters even before the law came into effect, and the atrocities committed against organizers over controversies at the film festival venue in Thiruvananthapuram, may be why the audience at the arts festival was prompted to stand up eighteen times in one sitting.

One must conclude that the brainwashing of fascism is influencing even the Malayalis, who are considered enlightened and politically literate. They have reached a highly dangerous mental state where they believe it is appropriate to live in submission to the Sangh Parivar, which tries to impose nationalism. 

Even the demonetization was a tool to find out if the public had reached a state of mind where they would accept government decisions without any protest. Along with this, a large section of the population is placed under the shadow of surveillance in the name of money reaching banks, and thus every movement of the citizens is recorded on the government's server. In this way, nationalism is becoming a weapon capable of invading the lives of individuals. If the secular democratic political movements responsible for creating awareness against the tragedies caused by such excessive or fake nationalism do not prepare themselves, the country will face a 'national disaster'.
