Strengthening Secular Political Unity

സുഫ്‌യാൻ അബ്ദുസ്സലാം · 2017-02-18 · Opinion & Analysis

The country is surrendering to fascism. The silence of secular parties often creates fear. Have we drifted far from the goals envisioned by the Constitution? Is there any relevance to secular political unity? Here is an assessment based on contemporary political developments.

Elections and the Threat of Fascism

Elections are underway in five states. Voting has concluded in Goa and Punjab. Elections are ongoing in Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand, and will take place in Manipur in March. As the country burns, dangerously gripped by the tentacles of fascism, these ongoing elections are capable of determining the very future of the nation. Some view these elections as a rehearsal for the upcoming 2019 Lok Sabha elections. For the BJP, the UP election is extremely critical. To secure an absolute majority in the Rajya Sabha after the Lok Sabha, a major victory in UP is essential for them.

Democracy and Civil Rights

Many parties and coalitions have governed at the center. There have always been political criticisms and opposition against the ruling parties and coalitions from time to time. However, since the 2014 Lok Sabha elections, Narendra Modi and Amit Shah have become unquestionable centers of power. The BJP is governing with the firm resolve that whatever the administration decides must be obeyed. In the old feudal system, subjects had no right to respond. But in a democratic system, they are citizens rather than subjects. Citizens have fundamental rights. Fascism is interfering in everything, from the dining table to the deathbed. The administration decides what to write, what to express, what to wear, and what to eat. By declaring everyone who reacts against this as anti-national, they maintain an atmosphere of fear. Fascism skillfully interferes even with the currency held by the people. In short, the country and its people are restless. When such intolerance and subjugation are imposed upon a populace that has lived by experiencing and enjoying tolerance, coexistence, and freedom, a backlash is natural. That is why, even in the Delhi and Bihar elections held after Narendra Modi came to power, the people reacted very strongly against the existing BJP government. We can hope that the vast majority in UP and other states where elections are being held, who desire peace and tranquility, will use this election logically.

India's Diversity and Nationalism

India, with its six major religions, 6400 castes, 1618 languages, and various ethnic groups, is a land of diversity. There is no other country in the world where such diversity exists. Yet, what binds all these diversities together is the sentiment of India. That was the reason for the struggles against British colonial hegemony and the fight for India's independence. Therefore, a strong national sentiment based on secularism was formed in India, transcending caste and religious differences. We can see this in the famous lines by Allama Iqbal that begin with Sare Jahan Se Achha, Hindustan Hamara.

It was in the name of the sentiment of India that people rallied behind the Indian National Congress, led by Gandhiji, transcending caste and religious thoughts. Those who opposed religious ideologies and those who disagreed with the Congress's economic policies also worked as Communist-Left movements while accepting secularism. At the same time, minorities in post-partition India organized themselves for the protection of their own rights. It was on that basis that the Muslim League remained active in India's political sphere, working for the protection of the rights of religious minorities, including Muslims, as declared by the Indian Constitution, while simultaneously strengthening India's secular character.

Pluralism and Religious Perspectives

In a country with various religions, castes, and cultures, there were very well-thought-out perspectives on how to live without religious hatred, without intolerance, and by knowing, informing, giving, and taking from each other. While each section strictly observed its own religious beliefs and rituals, they possessed a social consciousness that they must stand together for the country and its people. No one believed that the various castes and sub-castes within the majority religious groups organizing for their own causes, or minority groups organizing for their own rights, were against the integrity of the country. However, even then, a small minority had formed small groups to establish dominance in the name of religion. While the architects of the nation stood firm on a broad nationalism that accepted diversities, some began efforts to turn the country into a Ram Rajya, holding sticks in their hands.

A small group among the followers of Islam, which taught that there is no compulsion in religion, had also raised the demand for an Islamic state.

It is because the Muslim political leaders and religious scholars in India came out against such ideologies and taught patriotism and pluralism among Muslims that secular perspectives took root among them.

Election History and the Growth of the BJP

After becoming independent in 1947 and a republic in 1950, India held its first election in October 1951. In this election, the Congress won 364 out of the total 489 Lok Sabha constituencies. Independents were in second place. The undivided CPI, which won 16 seats, was the main opposition. The Indian secular mind uprooted the Hindutva organizations like the Bharatiya Jana Sangh, Akhil Bharatiya Ram Rajya Parishad, and Akhil Bharatiya Hindu Mahasabha, which entered the electoral fray advocating for a Hindu nation. Together, the three organizations received only ten seats. It was also the election in which B. Poker Sahib of the Muslim League from Malappuram, then part of the Madras state, reached the Lok Sabha. The Jana Sangh, which was the political form of the RSS, could not influence the Indian mind at that time. In 1977, the Jana Sangh was dissolved. Its members joined the Janata Party, which was formed following the Emergency. However, the Jana Sangh, unable to comply with the demand of Janata Party leaders to sever ties with the RSS, separated from the Janata Party and formed another organization in 1980. That is the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

However, many secular movements forgot that by providing seats in coalitions to organizations with communal fascist characteristics during the race for power, they were cutting the very branch of secularism they were sitting on. The fact that the Janata Party, formed in the name of Gandhian Socialism, was prepared to accommodate the Hindutva communal organization, the Jana Sangh, remains a historical blunder. Even then, the BJP, which split from the Janata Party, received only two seats when they contested alone in their first election in 1984. But by 1989, the fragmented Janata parties had a common platform under the name Janata Dal, led by V.P. Singh, and contested the election. In that momentum, the BJP also got an opportunity to become a link. Thus, the BJP, which had only two MPs, received 85 MPs. This was a massive blunder that occurred due to the shortcuts taken by secular parties for the sake of power.

The Necessity of Secular Unity

Today, as things have reached a state where they can govern the country alone without the help of other movements, the true colors of the Hindutva party are being revealed. This is where the positions taken in the UP election become relevant. Before Modi's entry, there were many events that determined the course of UP politics:

  • The 1989 Mandal reservation agitation
  • The 1992 Babri Masjid demolition incident

It is true that these helped increase the organizational strength of the BJP. Both incidents were caused by the incompetence of secular parties. Along with this, the formation of many parties like the BSP and Samajwadi also caused the division of secular votes. The fact that parties with secular characteristics were also prepared to form alliances with the BJP to get into power accelerated the arrival of fascism.

If the secular political movements in India decide to stand united, it is enough to protect the country from the tentacles of fascism. Movements that love the country's pluralism must have the political will to unify secular votes without letting them divide. One can consider the joining of hands by Rahul Gandhi and Akhilesh Yadav as a sign of that. Even then, parties like the BSP contesting alone and the fact that Muslims will not vote for one single party provide relief to the fascists. This is how the BJP gained an advantage in the 2014 elections in Western UP, a Muslim-majority region.

The lack of leadership capable of keeping secular movements united is the biggest crisis our country is facing. This is where the relevance of a brilliant political leader like E. Ahamed lies. Let us pray that there will be more leaders like him—humble, wise, mature, and prudent—who are capable of maintaining constant contact with secular political movements and leaders in various parts of the country to root out fascism. Along with this, we must be able to eliminate the growing distance between various caste and religious groups, create situations where love and harmony can flourish among them, and foster secular thoughts.
