The Secular Government and the Muslim Minority

സിറാജുല്‍ ഇസ്‌ലാം ബാലുശ്ശേരി · 2017-02-18 · Opinion & Analysis

India is a secular democratic country. The Indian Constitution neither grants special preference to any religion nor maintains a specific distance from any. Anyone is free to adopt any religion, practice its rituals, and propagate it among others. What are the things that Muslims living in such a country should keep in mind? Do the sources of the Quran and the Sunnah—the Prophetic tradition—forbid Muslims from living under a non-Islamic government? Is there any authentic support for such isolated voices? Let us examine this.

The Example of the Prophets

The Holy Quran is a book that encourages the eradication of evils existing in the world and the promotion of goodness to the greatest extent possible. By teaching the history of mankind, the Quran introduces the Prophets who initiated this task.

From Prophet Adam (peace be upon him) to Prophet Nuh (peace be upon him), ten generations followed the same faith and ideology. Regarding them, the Quran testified that mankind was a single community (Quran 2:213). However, during the era of Prophet Nuh (peace be upon him), the vast majority of people in that land were polytheists. There was a prevalent evil practice of worshipping holy men. It was to them that the first Messenger, Nuh (peace be upon him), was sent. At that time, he did not declare, "The majority in this land are polytheists, therefore I am leaving this place." Instead, that great Prophet lived among them, engaging with them while fulfilling his mission of propagation with a sense of responsibility.

Some people in our country propagate the argument that "one should not live among polytheists; one should only stay where there are complete Muslims." This is something that needs to be corrected. All the Messengers sent to the world came to non-Muslim societies where polytheism existed. If it were not permissible to stay in such a society, Allah would not have sent the Prophets there. Allah said:

“O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another...” (Quran 49:13)

Humanity are children of the same mother and father. They are creations of the one and only God. The great Prophets were not sent with a philosophy of hating or opposing anyone without cause.

Sense of Justice and Propagation

Whenever humans deviated from the sense of justice taught by Allah, the task of the Prophets was to call them back to the path of truth, goodness, and unity. Allah said:

“We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice” (Quran 57:25).

The entire human race should become a society based on a sense of justice. It is an injustice to worship the created instead of the Creator, the One God. People need to be saved from that and brought to the noble sense of justice of worshipping only the One God.

Humans, being children of the same mother and father, should not show hatred toward each other without cause. Among the greatest manners that believers must uphold, Allah said:

“O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm for Allah, witnesses in justice, and do not let the hatred of a people prevent you from being just. Be just; that is nearer to righteousness” (Quran 5:8).

Muslims should live in any land in justice and goodness. Establishing justice in the world is the desire of the Quran.

The Migration and Protection of the Prophets

Prophets sent to various corners of the world did not escape to another region with those who accepted the faith, nor did they declare, "I am leaving this land," without any contact with those who did not accept the faith. They did not abandon the land where they were born and raised unless they were expelled or subjected to severe persecution. They created favorable conditions to complete the mission of propagation entrusted to them by Allah. With hearts free of hypocrisy, they told society the great declaration: "We are your trustworthy well-wishers."

Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), who was active in propagation for thirteen years in Makkah, had to endure numerous persecutions and hardships. Yet, he did not leave the land of his birth for his own sake. When leaving his homeland for Madinah, he said, "You are a land dear to me. If your people had not expelled me, I would not have left you." This means that only when the enemies of truth-propagation did not allow him to stay in his birthplace, and after enduring many sacrifices and hardships, reaching a stage where he could no longer stay, did he migrate to Madinah.

It was during his life in Makkah that the Prophet (peace be upon him) asked the Makkans, "The Quraish have prevented me from conveying the words of my Lord to the ears of the people. Therefore, is there anyone among you (non-Muslims) who can help me?" This is an authentic hadith narrated by Jabir bin Abdullah (may Allah be pleased with him) in the Sunan of Ibn Majah. This hadith is evidence that for the great mission of Islam, to convey the voice of Tawheed—the oneness of God—to the ears of the people, one can seek and accept the protection and help of non-Muslims. The eminent scholar and former Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia, Sheikh Abdul Aziz Ibn Baz (may Allah have mercy on him), explained this matter very precisely in his commentary on this hadith and established it with supplementary evidence.

Cooperation of Non-Muslims and Islamic History

Those who lack sufficient knowledge of Sharia and are shallow in their understanding are the ones who criticize carrying out propagation with the cooperation and help of non-Muslims. They propagate that this is contrary to Salafiyyah and the Manhaj—the methodology. The history of accepting the help of disbelievers to convey the voice of Tawheed to the ears of fellow human beings is authentic. Prophet Shu'aib (peace be upon him), who invited people to the path of Allah, was not expelled from his land because he was under the protection of his family and friends who had not accepted the faith. Allah informs us of what that people said:

“They said, ‘O Shu'aib, we do not understand much of what you say, and indeed, we consider you among us as weak. And if not for your family, we would have stoned you [to death]; and you are not to us one respected’” (Quran 11:91).

Sheikh Abdur Rahman Nasir as-Sa'di (may Allah have mercy on him) recorded in his commentary on the above verse that receiving help for propagation through non-Muslims is part of the procedure Allah has ordained. Those who withdraw from this mission with specific interests, discouraging propagators, are those who do not recognize that Allah helps Islamic propagation through kind-hearted people among the disbelievers.

Allah’s Prophet (peace be upon him) carried out his mission under the protection of Abu Talib until his death. After that, the Prophet (peace be upon him) went to Taif seeking help, where the people there abused and drove him away. Later, when he returned, the Prophet (peace be upon him) entered Makkah under the protection of the non-believer Mut'im ibn Adi. After Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), the greatest personality in this community is Abu Bakr Siddiq (may Allah be pleased with him). He also carried out Islamic propagation in Makkah under the protection of a non-Muslim named Ibn al-Dughunna. After Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him), the person with the highest status in Islam is Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him). He lived in Makkah under the protection of a person from among the disbelievers named Asib ibn Wa'il as-Sahmi. The third of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and a well-known companion of the Prophet (peace be upon him) is Uthman ibn Affan (may Allah be pleased with him). After migrating to Abyssinia, he re-entered Makkah under the protection of the non-believer Mughira. It was a custom in the land of Makkah that if a person announced they were under the protection of a certain individual, they could not be harmed thereafter. Even though this law, which provided protection for a person's life and faith, was a law of the land of Makkah, the Prophet (peace be upon him) did not show reluctance in accepting its shelter. Nor did he stop the companions from doing so. He did not push aside that action by calling it a Taghuti—tyrannical—system.

The Breadth of Propagation

There is no greater mission for a Muslim than to convey the noble message of La ilaha illallah—there is no god but Allah—to the ears of the people. Muslims are obligated to find and adopt all non-religious-prohibited ways that help in fulfilling this mission. The great agreement known as Hilf al-Fudul was made at the house of the non-believer Abdullah ibn Jud'an. This goodness-filled agreement, which declared that no native or foreigner in Makkah should be a victim of aggression and no injustice should be done to anyone, was made under the leadership of a non-believer. The Prophet (peace be upon him) spoke in praise of this agreement, which was formed during the pre-Islamic era, even after his prophethood. He declared that it was more valuable to him than a red camel and that if he were called to such arrangements while in Islam, he would respond to it. There is an excellent example for the believers in the Prophet of Allah (peace be upon him). Nothing that interested parties who do not know the religion propagate with narrow-mindedness is religion or righteousness.

The declaration made by Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) after arriving in Madinah from Makkah and assuming its leadership is unanimously recorded by historians. The Prophet (peace be upon him) emphasized that Jews would be considered a religious community in Madinah, that there would be no obstacles to them living according to their religion, and that justice would be available to those who are oppressed regardless of their religion. There are clear lessons in the life of the Prophet (peace be upon him) for all nations of the world and for the rulers of pluralistic states. Justice being served to the oppressed and the oppressors being punished creates a good, harmonious environment. That is what the Prophet (peace be upon him) put into practice.

“Indeed, We have given you, [O Muhammad], a clear conquest” (Quran 48:1). The vast majority of commentators are of the opinion that the "clear conquest" mentioned here refers to the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah. Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him), while explaining this verse, argues that the reason the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah was a victory is that it became possible for a Muslim to communicate with the disbelievers. Through this great peace treaty, an opportunity arose to convey useful knowledge and faith to the ears of all people. The Holy Quran described it as a clear conquest. Because the barrier that existed in the field of propagation was removed, opportunities opened up for Muslims to connect, communicate, and propagate with the disbelievers. Abdur Rahman Nasir as-Sa'di (may Allah have mercy on him) also says in the explanation of this same verse that in places where such opportunities are available, it is commendable to act in a harmonious atmosphere with people who have not accepted the true faith. All this points to the necessity of maintaining a peaceful atmosphere in society.

Allah says:

“Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes - from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly” (Quran 60:8).

Muslims are responsible for inviting people who have strayed from the worship of the One God and are immersed in unrighteousness to the right path. India is not a Muslim nation. But we have the freedom in our country to go from house to house and convey the Islamic message.

In countries like India, where non-Muslims are the majority and Muslims are a minority, if it is the non-Muslims who provide freedom for Islamic propagation, eminent scholars of the Salafi world clarify that Muslims should cooperate in bringing them to power, work with unity, and vote to elect them.

The argument that it is not suitable for a Muslim to cooperate with the administrative systems of countries with democratic secular systems is that of narrow-minded people. Some, who are in the extreme minority, argue that it is not even permissible to cast a vote in elections held in such countries. In our country, where there is freedom to live according to the ideology of the One God and to propagate it, it is their ignorance that makes them portray cooperating with non-believers who help maintain this freedom as actions contrary to Salafiyyah and the Manhaj. The general public should not forget the past history where such schools of thought appeared. The believer community needs to identify whose background motivations are behind the reappearance of the cries of political Shirk—associating partners with God—which were thought to have died out.
