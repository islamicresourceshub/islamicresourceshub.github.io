## The State and Freedom of Speech

If questioning those in power and pointing out their flawed policies is treated as an act of treason, it must be understood that the nation is drifting toward autocracy. In these times, when any ordinary person can respond to injustices via social media, there is no doubt that the number of such 'traitors' will only continue to rise.

It has not been long since the Supreme Court struck down **Section 66A of the IT Act**, which stifled freedom of speech in the cyber world. The Supreme Court ruled that this section, which allowed for the easy arrest of those expressing defamatory opinions on social media and other platforms, was unconstitutional.

However, ignoring this verdict, there is a growing trend of labeling those who speak out as traitors or terrorists and throwing them into prison.

There is no doubt that the loss of the right to express opinions respectfully, point out the mistakes of those in power, and offer healthy criticism in a free democratic country is a step toward dictatorship. At the same time, the stance that 'anything goes' under the guise of freedom of speech should never be adopted.

## Law and Experiences

When Shaheen Dhada, a girl who wrote a post on Facebook about the difficulties faced by the people of Mumbai during the hartal following the death of Shiv Sena leader **Bal Thackeray**, was arrested along with Rinu Srinivasan, a native of Kumarakom in Kottayam who liked the post, a historic court verdict followed a case filed by law student **Shreya Singhal**. Yet, speaking out respectfully remains something to be feared!

Under Section 66A, cases have been registered and many have been imprisoned in various states. In many places, actions are taken on the accusation of insulting political leaders.
- In West Bengal, ruled by the Trinamool Congress, there are those who have been punished for drawing pictures and cartoons of Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee.
- In Uttar Pradesh, a student was jailed for writing against the Chief Minister.

Criticizing in foul language or defaming others is indeed wrong. However, denying the very right to express an opinion under that pretext is unjust. There are some things we must remember here.

## The Double Standard of the Law

One cannot help but feel that there is a stance where some are allowed to say or write anything, while others are forbidden from saying or writing anything at all. Even knowing that certain remarks made by some Members of Parliament and religious figures targeting minority groups are capable of causing far-reaching consequences, no action is taken against them. There is no one to correct them or hold them accountable.

> The Indian Penal Code Section 153-A states that those who commit offenses such as "promoting enmity between different groups on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, etc., and doing acts prejudicial to maintenance of harmony" through writing, speech, or other signs, can be punished with imprisonment of up to three years and a fine.

Yet, the legal system here lacks the courage to touch 'certain' people who spew venom. Meanwhile, those in power find satisfaction in claiming to have saved the nation by arresting and jailing those who merely clicked a 'like' button on Facebook. This is the situation that needs to change.
