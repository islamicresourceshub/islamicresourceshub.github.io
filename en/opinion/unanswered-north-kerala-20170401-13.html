Northern Kerala: The Unanswered Questions

Dr. C.M. Sabir Navas

When the poisonous rain of hatred and communalism seeps into the soil, the tragedy that befalls a land is witnessed in the life of Riyas Maulavi, a native of Kodagu. The Home Department must ensure that those behind the brutal murder of this innocent young man are brought to justice and given exemplary punishment. It is no small feat that the culprits were identified within hours of the incident. However, the charge sheet must be prepared with such precision that the criminals cannot walk free on the streets. There is no doubt that the government's handling of the Kodinhi Faisal murder case has sparked concern within the minority community. One can only hope that the authorities do not dismiss this as a random act of violence by intoxicated individuals.

Karnataka is viewed by Prime Minister Narendra Modi and his chief aide Amit Shah as the next laboratory for fascism following the anti-Muslim pogroms they orchestrated in Gujarat. Among the cadres of the Sangh Parivar, the state is affectionately known as the second Gujarat. The fascist storm being exported from Gujarat to Karnataka threatens to sweep across Manjeshwar and Kasaragod, potentially striking the shores of Kerala. This is the warning we must read into this shocking murder.

If one examines the frequent visits to the north by figures ranging from Pravin Togadia and Ashok Singhal to Shashikala Teacher and Shobha Surendran, the danger of a massive, unacknowledged conspiracy becomes immediately apparent. While the Muslim community in Kasaragod has not always excelled in the fields of education or employment, a small segment has historically maintained a physical robustness that stands in direct opposition to extremist Hindutva communalism. When considering this, one can understand the situation with greater balance. Instead of fostering mutual cooperation and social exchange while maintaining distinct religious traditions, some individuals from both communities often seem to compete in perpetuating hatred and animosity.

If one examines the background of the communal riots and the rising number of murders that have occurred in various parts of Kasaragod district over the years, the reality becomes clear to anyone. The picture is complete when we consider that the police are forced to stand guard with heavy security in front of mosques during temple festivals and in front of temples during the Urs celebrations at Maqams. The problem persists because religious scholars, political leaders, and public figures who should be resolving these issues amicably are instead standing on the sidelines. Kasaragod is not merely a place to organize fundraising rallies when anniversaries arrive. It would be better for everyone to realize that this northern region is meant to be a land of peace, and we must act to ensure that Kerala does not transform into another Gujarat by putting an end to the divisions of communalism.
