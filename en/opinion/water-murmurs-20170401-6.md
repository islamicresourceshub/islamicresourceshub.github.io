# Whispers of Water

**Ibn Ali Edathanattukara**

For most people today, the sight of water tanks overflowing after being pumped from wells is merely a distant memory. The sight of a drying well bed adds immense pressure to the homemaker. When those without drinking water share their plight, the fear of having to share one's own limited supply naturally arises in the heart of the one who still has water.

Water scarcity deeply disturbs the peace of family life. In both private and collective prayers, the plea for water and rain has become a constant presence.

The older generation remembers with nostalgia how the use of the traditional «kindi»—a small vessel with a spout—drastically reduced water consumption. Meanwhile, the new generation celebrates through new media.

Discussions—especially on social media—often revolve around the excessive water wasted while brushing teeth, washing, or bathing with the tap left running, along with statistics and measurements in liters.

Even if one wishes to share the available water with a struggling neighbor, scientific predictions of drought hold them back.

There was a time when birds, the creations of the Almighty, could drink as much as they pleased. Today, individuals and groups celebrate as a grand achievement the act of filling a clay bowl with water and hanging it from a tree branch to provide a drink for the birds!

We now see the sight of young children carrying pots and buckets for miles in search of clean water for cooking and drinking. In such a situation, when we see those blessed with abundance wasting water recklessly to irrigate gardens or wash vehicles, the heat of the sighs rising from the chests of those suffering from water shortages cannot be ignored.

Even water provided through public initiatives like «Jalanidhi» is being used by some for farming and other purposes. There is no doubt that this is a misuse of resources in the current drought conditions.

It is heartening that some people, understanding that water is a blessing and that true happiness lies in sharing rather than hoarding, show the kindness to deliver it to those in need. There is no doubt that the prayers for these kind-hearted souls who help by providing water come from the depths of the heart.

Let us also pray that we may be granted the strength to overcome the trials of water scarcity.

## The Wait

**Shanavas Kulukallur**

Leaning against
the mother's sigh
that this, at least, might turn out right,
is the salt burning on the tip of a coal.
Along with tea stirred with love,
savoring the smile of sweetness,
those who walked out of the threshold of hope
were many in number...!
In the daylight hours when the neck stretched
toward the desire of a dowry necklace,
the news of the 'stoppage' that climbed the steps
was a gift from the party of outsiders.
Waiting for the helping hands of love
that are not caught in the nets of the nerve-wracked,
nor stuck on yellow metal,
without breaking the walls,
candle-like lives
are overflowing outside.
