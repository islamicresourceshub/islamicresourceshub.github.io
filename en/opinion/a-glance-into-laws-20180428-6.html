A Glance into Laws

മുസാഫിര്‍ · 2018-04-28 · Opinion & Analysis

Constitutional Law

India is a union of states. The Constitution guarantees many valuable fundamental rights to its citizens. No law that curtails or removes these rights can exist in India. We have understood the system for legislation and its procedures; these constitute constitutional law. There are many other provisions in the Constitution as well. Whatever is stipulated in the Constitution is collectively referred to as constitutional laws. The law that dictates how legislation should be conducted is found within the Constitution. It is also the Constitution that specifies who should be entrusted with the task of legislation and how. In short, the Constitution is the source of all laws. Constitutional laws are also the criteria for determining the validity of laws. The authority of all laws depends on the Constitution. Therefore, the Constitution can be described as the law of laws or the fundamental law of the nation.

Civil and Criminal Laws

After constitutional laws, other laws can be broadly classified into two: civil laws and criminal laws.

Duties and Rights

In the final analysis, rights and duties are the fundamental components of all laws. They are complementary to each other. The word wife has no meaning without a husband; rights and duties are similar. When a right resides in one person, a complementary duty exists in another. Likewise, a duty vested in one person corresponds to a right in another. For example, a person borrows an amount from another, promising to return it when needed. Here, the person who lent the money has the right to get it back when they ask for it. The person who borrowed it has the duty to return it when the owner demands it. Another example: everyone has the right to use public roads, including pedestrians and drivers. A pedestrian has the duty to walk on the side of the road without obstructing drivers. If there is a footpath, one should walk on it. If it has broken slabs, it is dangerous. Here, the concerned authorities have the duty to maintain the footpath in a way that is not dangerous to those using it. Citizens have the right to insist that it be maintained that way. Drivers have the duty to drive carefully so as not to cause trouble to other vehicles and pedestrians. From the perspective of duties, the law exists to compel those responsible to fulfill their duties. From the perspective of rights, the law helps in protecting rights and obtaining compensation when rights are violated. Rights guaranteed by law are called legal rights. Duties that the law insists must be followed are legal duties. If someone does a favor, there is a duty to say thank you, but that is not a legal duty; it is a moral duty. (To be continued)
