The Democratic Jallikattu

ഡോ. സി.എം സാബിര്‍ നവാസ്‌ · 2017-02-18 · Opinion & Analysis

Tamil politics has once again revealed its true colors through a series of breath-holding political dramas. Following the death of Jayalalithaa, who ruled Tamil Nadu for many long years, the political «Jallikattu»—the traditional bull-taming sport—over who will be her successor is intensifying.

The Political History of the Thalaivi

Although her political career was stained by corruption and personal stubbornness, the 'Thalaivi'—the leader—will be remembered in history as a ruler who stood by the common people. The 'Jaya Magic' that easily won the hearts of the poor by providing rice for two rupees, cement at half price, and idli and dosa at subsidized rates, has come to an end, setting the stage for a dramatic and suspenseful power struggle.

The Tug-of-War for Power

National politics is watching closely to see who will steer the helm of Tamil Nadu: her close confidante Sasikala, who stood by her like a shadow, or the loyalist Panneerselvam, who acted as her representative during times of crisis. It is said that Sasikala Natarajan staked her claim to the Chief Minister's post while keeping all the MLAs 'hostage' at a resort in Mahabalipuram.

O. P. Panneerselvam, known as OPS, who served as interim Chief Minister twice, entered the fray to retract his resignation—a move that sent ripples through national politics—just as he seemed to be silently accepting his departure from office.

The Politics of Corruption and Nepotism

Whoever rules, Tamil Nadu is destined to witness a series of unscrupulous theatrical performances. The Mannargudi Mafia, led by Sasikala's husband Natarajan—who pulled the strings from behind the scenes during Jayalalithaa's rule—is aiming for multi-crore corruption deals.

When one hears that five crore rupees are being distributed to each of the MLAs standing by her side, the scale of the impending corruption and nepotism becomes clear. Goons are now standing guard over democracy to ensure that the MLAs do not defect.

New Equations in Tamil Politics

While Tamil parties, which prioritize Tamil unity and the Dravidian movement, have been willing to go to any lengths for regionalism, it is noteworthy that they have remained far removed from communalism. Secularists are watching with anxiety to see if the BJP will gain a foothold in Tamil soil through the back door opened by Panneerselvam to retain power. It would be for the best if the flight of OPS—the phoenix bird that rose from the ashes of destruction and despair—over the lotus ponds does not lead to the political map of Tamil Nadu being rewritten in blood.

Let us keep our eyes and ears open toward Tamil Nadu. A climax and anti-climax that surpass old MGR movies are the standard style of Dravidian politics. In a playing field where predictions hold no relevance, anything can happen at any moment. We may have to watch, without blinking, as those trying to become Chief Minister become the primary accused, and those who were merely leaning against the pillars end up taking the reins of power. In a land where elected representatives have no qualms about treating whoever they see that day as their father and mother, it is civic consciousness and democratic values that lose their worth.
