The statements made by **Professor M.G.S. Narayanan** during a lecture series on nationalism have become a subject of intense debate. The essence of what he stated, as reported by the media, is as follows:

> It is a misconception that there was nationalism in India before the British arrived. Nationalism emerged only when people began to oppose them. Before the arrival of the British, there was no country called India; there was only a cultural concept of India. If history is to be written based on evidence, there is no truth in the claim that India had a nationalism in the past which the British destroyed.

![When National Tradition is Questioned](/assets/when-national-tradition-is-questioned-20170121-2/21Jan17-02.jpg)

> Two factors are essential for nationalism: first, an enemy, and second, a perception of a golden past that evokes a sense of pride. Thus, national consciousness grew by viewing the British as enemies and the Gupta period as India's golden age. When the British wrote history, they portrayed it as a series of foreign invasions. To counter this, Indian historians created a national history. Nationalism in India took shape in the late 19th century when the opposition to the British began. It is foolish to believe that there was a spiritual culture and spiritual nationalism in ancient India. In that complex social system, there was no room for nationalism. One cannot equate the Mauryan or Gupta empires with the modern nation-state. Those who propagate this new nationalism claim that the **Valmiki Ramayana** is the history of Lord Rama. Our ancestors had no such notion; Valmiki was called the 'Adi Kavi' (First Poet), not the 'Adi Itihasakar' (First Historian). For the past thirty years, there has been an attempt to prove that Sri Rama is a historical figure. It has reached a point where the goal of patriotism is seen as synonymous with accepting **Hindutva**. The word 'Hindu' has no connection to religion; there were various caste groups here at that time...

## The Final Verdict in the Shah Bano Begum Case

When the Supreme Court suggested the implementation of a Uniform Civil Code while delivering the final verdict in the **Shah Bano Begum case** on April 23, 1985, this suggestion was naturally seized upon by **Hindutva** proponents. They began propagating that Islamic personal laws lack gender equality and that Indian Muslims are alienated from nationalism.

## The Philosophy of Islamophobia

Thus, the ordinary mind within the hardline nationalist began to move and think under the influence of fear towards Islam. Those who link the intensity of Indian nationalism with Hindutva ideology began to rely on the philosophy of Islamophobia.

> The Sangh Parivar has long propagated the idea that "if you are a nationalist, you cannot help but be a Hindu or refrain from being violent towards Islam and Muslims."

## Levels Where Nationalism Interacts with True Nationalism

This propaganda has, at certain stages, influenced and misled Indian society. At the same time, pseudo-nationalism, which carries the seeds of fear, has retreated at times when it could not ideologically engage with the true stream of secular nationalism.

There are certain levels where the nationalism envisioned by Hindutva proponents interacts with true nationalism:

1. Firstly, Hindutva nationalism theorizes hatred towards the Islamic religion and Muslim sociality.
2. Secondly, Hindutva nationalism relies on weak emotionalism.
3. Thirdly, instead of empowering society, it facilitates its fragmentation.

On the other hand, true nationalism does not take a theoretical stance of affinity or disagreement with any religion. Moreover, it encourages thinking about society with a strong sense of reality, completely setting aside weak emotionalism. Simultaneously, it unifies society beyond all forms of divisive thinking.

## False History as a Crucial Tool

In every era, false history has been used as a crucial tool to spread fear about Islam. The lies masked as history, unleashed by Hindutva political movements in the post-partition phase, were primarily aimed at accusing Islam and Muslims. Such accusations included many stories linking past Muslim rulers, Sultans, and Muslim administrators of various princely states to negative narratives. Some propagated that the only thing all Muslim rulers who governed different parts of India over the centuries did was to kill Hindus, destroy temples, and annihilate the traditional remnants of this land. The specific type of hatred that emerged from the social fear of upper-caste Hindus later developed into artificial history-making. The religious hatred that grew under the guise of nationalism in the 19th century became a convenient backdrop for the distribution of artificial stories and rumors. Prior to that, the Hindutva-based propaganda that emerged in various parts of North India during the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries had already prepared the theoretical framework and background for anti-Muslim and anti-Islamic hatred.

## Division Under the Guise of Nationalism

It is under the guise of nationalism that the idea of partition began to be discussed. Although we have seen hardline nationalists transform into staunch opponents of partition in Indian history, it is a fact that the origin of the partition ideology lay in the minds of hardline nationalists or cultural nationalists.

## The Anti-Islam Propaganda of the 1920s

Partition ideology, which Hindutva politics found to be its trump card, utilized the energy of Islamophobia. Some of the things propagated in the 1920s were as follows:

> The culture of India, which is tens of thousands of years old, and its remnants were destroyed by Muslims who arrived in India 1000 years ago. Muslims gained power here using swords and threats, and kept Indians, including the upper castes, suppressed. Islam is a highly dangerous and terrifying philosophy. It is a religion that fosters extreme aggression and violence. A peaceful life is impossible for anyone here without deporting Muslims from India.

## M.G.S.'s Rebuttal

M.G.S. Narayanan has refuted the arguments of those who claim that Indian nationalism is Hindu nationalism and that it has a tradition spanning tens of thousands of years.

History shows that the social divisions and the *Chaturvarnya*-caste system that have existed for millennia have only created deep divisions among the people, and it has never been possible to bring Hindus under the umbrella of a single nationalism. It is this disunity that caused constant foreign invasions. Could this 'national thought with a tradition of tens of thousands of years' defend against such invasions? No; in fact, the reality is that there were people within the country who provided support and assistance to the invaders. It was another Hindu king, Jayachand, who invited Muhammad Ghori to fight against the Rajput king Prithviraj! When Shivaji fought for the 'liberation of Hindus,' the Rajput kings of Maharashtra stood on the side of the Mughal emperors! Such historical truths put Hindu nationalism in the dock, making M.G.S.'s observations relevant.

 the problems created by the proponents of communalism in India are part of a sinister attempt to keep the wounds of India alive with their stinging pain. It can be seen that some evil forces are desperately trying to undermine the peace and tranquility of the country by keeping the Muslim community on the edge of suspicion. Muslims are being cruelly attacked with accusations.

## Nationalism Based on Hindu Communalism

It is, in fact, an **RSS**-led government based on Hindu communalism that has now come to power in India. When the **NDA** government came to power at the center in 2000, the Sannyasi Sansad held in Lucknow demanded that the Constitution be amended in accordance with the instructions of the **Manusmriti** to abolish reservations. Attempts to amend the Constitution accordingly and reshape it according to the concept of a Hindu Rashtra...
