## Muslims Living in Secular Democratic India

Should one remain aloof from the electoral process? Is it not an obligation for Muslims to play their part in the process of nation-building? This is a factual inquiry.

![](/assets/elections-the-stand-must-be-well-considered-20170325-10/25Mar17-04.jpg)

## Dar al-Mu'ahadah - The Land of Covenants

India is a nation where Muslims and followers of other religions live together peacefully with mutual understanding. According to Islamic jurisprudence, this is a «Dar al-Mu'ahadah», or a land where Muslims and non-Muslims live under a covenant. A Muslim is not permitted to incite riots here or act against the country by violating agreements. A Muslim is one who fulfills covenants. In a hadith narrated by Imam Muslim, we find the following:

> Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "Nothing prevented me from participating in the Battle of Badr except for this one incident. I and Abu Husayl set out for Medina. On the way, the polytheist Quraysh intercepted us and asked, 'Are you heading to Muhammad?' We replied, 'We do not intend to go to him; we are only going to Medina.' They made us swear by Allah that we would only go to Medina and would not fight alongside him. We went to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and told him the matter. He said: 'Proceed, both of you. We fulfill our covenant with them and seek Allah's help against them'" – (Sahih Muslim: 4740).

## Violation of Covenant is Treachery

This incident teaches us that the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) instructed that even a covenant unilaterally imposed by enemies upon those who had to migrate, leaving their homes and lands behind, must be honored.

Therefore, while living in a country, engaging in secret movements against it or inciting riots is an act of treachery. When Abu Rafi (may Allah be pleased with him), who had come as an envoy from the enemy side, said to the Prophet (peace be upon him), "I have accepted Islam, and I will not return to Mecca," the Prophet (peace be upon him) replied, "I do not betray covenants. If you have Islam in your heart, then return."

![](/assets/elections-the-stand-must-be-well-considered-20170325-10/25Mar17-05.jpg)

Furthermore, scholars have clearly written in light of hadiths that even in enemy nations, one who has entered with their permission and trust must not engage in activities or aggression against them. Imam Ibn Qudamah states in his book **Al-Mughni**:

> "One who has entered the land of the enemy with their guarantee of safety and trust must not betray them or misappropriate their wealth. Betraying them is forbidden, because they granted him permission to enter safely only on the belief that he would not betray or attack them" – (Al-Mughni: Volume 10, Page 507).

This is the etiquette Islam shows even toward enemies.

## The Punishment for Violating Covenants

If this is the case with enemies, how much more so for those living in mutual understanding with Muslims! The agreement that exists in this country is that those living here should not attack one another. Anyone not prepared to uphold that covenant is free to renounce their citizenship and leave the country. However, if one violates the covenant and takes the life of a non-Muslim, they will not even smell the fragrance of Paradise.

> Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As narrated that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "Whoever kills a person who has a covenant with Muslims will not smell the fragrance of Paradise, even though its fragrance can be smelled from a distance of forty years" (Sahih al-Bukhari: 3166).

## Methods of Response

Islam does not permit responding to disagreements with the government by destroying public property or causing hardship to the public. Instead, a Muslim can respond to protect their rights in a dignified manner and within the limits permitted by the law of the land. Such responses, agreements, and disagreements can be expressed publicly or privately. While the instruction "be patient and ask Allah for what is yours" applies to nations where there is a Muslim government, in countries like India, a believer can respond publicly and legally.

## Relations with Non-Muslims

Since the most important aspect of Islam is the worship of the One God, Islam teaches Muslims to maintain distinctiveness in faith, rituals, and even dress. Yet, it permits and encourages maintaining human relations and dealings with others, teaching us to be just and kind to them. Allah says:

> "Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes - from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly" (Quran 60:8).

It is well-known that when a young Jewish boy fell ill, the Prophet (peace be upon him) visited him and prayed for his recovery; through the Prophet's kind approach, the boy found Islam. When the Prophet (peace be upon him) passed away, his armor was held as collateral by a Jew. Even when the Prophet (peace be upon him) migrated from his hometown of Mecca to Medina to escape the persecution of polytheists, he chose a non-Muslim as his guide. Remember, this was at a time when a bounty of 100 camels was announced for anyone who killed Muhammad (peace be upon him). However, a believer cannot agree with the beliefs, rituals, or specific religious symbols of non-Muslims, as these are part of their faith. If I invite someone who does not eat meat to my house and prepare a meat biryani, it makes no sense to ask why they are not eating it after I have prepared my favorite food for them. Their beliefs or customs may not permit it. Similarly, a Muslim cannot perform acts that compromise their faith. Therefore, they might not light a lamp or partake in certain offerings, because their faith does not permit anything associated with polytheism.

Islam does not forbid cooperating with non-Muslims in worldly matters, doing good to them, or treating them with tolerance and compassion, provided it does not compromise one's own faith and principles. On the contrary, this is what Islam mandates.

## Duty Toward Neighbors

Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) did not forbid trade with non-Muslims, nor did he teach that non-Muslims should not be in the neighborhood. Instead, he taught that you should not eat your fill while your neighbor is hungry, regardless of who they are. He said:

> "He is not a believer who eats his fill while he knows his neighbor is hungry" (Cited in Al-Albani's Sahih).

Islam should not be understood by looking at anti-Islamic forces; rather, it should be understood from the sources of Islam itself.

## Voting and Running for Office in Democratic Systems: The Islamic Stance

In nations where a democratic system exists, the Salafi stance is that one should participate to preserve the goodness, honesty, and justice of the land, and to protect the religious and worldly rights of Muslims. Mawdudi taught that even in the aforementioned form, this is shirk (polytheism) and kufr (disbelief). However, whether in Kerala or abroad, Salafi scholars have taught that one should participate and strive for good as much as possible.

**Sheikh Ibn Uthaymeen (may Allah have mercy on him)** says:

> "My view is that participating in elections is mandatory. We must choose people whom we perceive to have goodness. Because if the good people stay away, then who will take their place? Naturally, it will be the people of evil. Or it will be people who have no particular purpose, neither good nor bad, who just follow whoever raises their voice the loudest. Therefore, we must choose the person we perceive to have goodness" (https://youtube/u7hgfceIjb4).

**Sheikh Abdul Aziz Al ash-Sheikh**, the Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia, says:

> "Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah are proponents of goodness, think in a positive way, and have good intentions. If they sit idle in their homes and let others play with matters as they please, what will be the state of affairs? One does not participate in an election with the assumption that they can achieve everything. They participate with the intention of striving for as much good as possible. May Allah have mercy on anyone who helps Islam even with a single word. A single honest Muslim is enough to stand courageously against thousands of thieves. The issue is whether there is an honest intention. If one participates in an election with the intention of doing good, Allah knows that they have not participated without the intention of steering things in a good direction. If they rectify matters and do good as much as they can, they will have Allah's blessing... We should not turn our faces away and say, 'That is enough, we don't want anything, we are doing nothing because they are there.' We should participate, do the good we can, and make every effort to ensure an election without corruption takes place... Good individuals with good thoughts and intentions should have a place there. This is to prevent bad people from occupying those positions. If good people retreat and open the door for others, they will not be able to control matters. They will become irrelevant to everyone. They will have no voice later" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tisT1_E1fEU).

**K.M. Maulavi (may Allah have mercy on him)**, while responding to Jamaat-e-Islami, notes the same:

> "If all capable and just Muslims remain aloof, the country will generally suffer from injustice, and Muslims themselves will have to experience more harm due to oppressive laws" (Al-Manar, 1963, Volume: 04, Jamaat-e-Islami and Ibadah).
