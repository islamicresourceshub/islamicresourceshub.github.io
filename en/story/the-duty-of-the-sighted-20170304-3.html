The Duty of the Sighted

ഉസ്മാന്‍ പാലക്കാഴി · 2017-03-04 · Stories & Literature

An Unexpected Arrival

Jasim and his younger brother Fahim, who usually walk home slowly after school, arrived running that day. Placing his bag on the table, Jasim called out loudly:

"Umma... Assalamu Alaikum."

"Wa Alaikumussalam... Why are you both panting today? What happened? Did a dog or something chase you?" asked their mother.

"It is nothing like that, Umma," said Fahim.

"Then what is the matter? It seems like something happened," their mother asked curiously.

"Umma, I did something today that Allah loves very much," said Jasim.

"Me too," added Fahim.

"Oh... is that so? I was worried. Anyway, what is this great thing that my children did?"

Helping a Blind Traveler

Jasim explained:

"While we were walking, we saw a blind man on the road. He was trying to cross it. But he had to wait for the vehicles to pass before he could cross! He was standing there struggling because vehicles were rushing back and forth. So, I took his hand and helped him to the other side..."

Before Jasim could finish, Fahim interrupted:

"Umma, I also helped him. I gave that poor man the two rupees I had with me."

"Fahim is right, Umma. He gave the two rupees he had to the man. I also gave the two rupees you had given me to buy sweets," Jasim added, as if not wanting to be outdone by his younger brother.

Mother's Advice

Their mother smiled happily and said: "Very good, my children. What you did is excellent. But it would have been even better if you had kept one thing in mind."

"What is that, Umma...?" they both asked in unison.

"It is more appropriate to keep it hidden when you help someone. Have you not heard how Caliph Umar (RA) used to disguise himself and roam at night to understand the condition of his subjects, helping those in need without them knowing who he was? Do what you can to help others while seeking only the reward from Allah. Do not publicize the help you have given. That is more virtuous. Regardless, I am happy with what you did. May Allah reward you appropriately for your good deed..."

"Ameen," Jasim and Fahim said together.

Intention and Reward

"We have learned that Nabi (SA) said that deeds are accepted based on intention and that doing good deeds to show off to people is punishable. Is it wrong that we told you, Umma, about helping the blind man? Will Allah not reward us?" Jasim asked with a hint of sadness.

"My children, didn't you do the favor while desiring the reward from Allah? Therefore, you will not be denied the reward. It is not wrong to share the joy of doing a good deed with your mother. Just make sure you don't go around telling everyone you meet from now on," their mother said, comforting them.

"Umma... I am starving..." Fahim ran to the kitchen, followed by Jasim.
