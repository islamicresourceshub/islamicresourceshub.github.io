Rules Are Meant to Be Followed

ഉസ്‌മാൻ പാലക്കാഴ‍ി · 2017-01-28 · Stories & Literature

It was raining heavily, pouring down in sheets. Suhail was at a dental clinic in town, having accompanied his mother to see the doctor. There were many patients waiting with tokens, and it would be hours before their turn came. The clinic was on the second floor of a building, and Suhail stood looking down, lost in the rhythm of the rain.

Men, women, and children were all rushing to reach their homes or other destinations through the downpour. Some had umbrellas, while others ran without any cover. Even those with umbrellas were getting soaked by the intensity of the rain. Those getting off the bus without umbrellas scrambled to the shelter of nearby shops. Suhail found the scene quite interesting.

After a while, the rain eased slightly. That was when Suhail spotted a boy running. He was moving carefully, looking left and right to avoid bumping into people. Soon, Suhail recognized him—it was his friend, Raees!

When he reached the traffic signal, the boy stopped. He had no umbrella, and his clothes were drenched. He must have been shivering with cold. Poor thing!

“Mom, I’ll be right back. My friend is standing down there in the rain,” Suhail said, grabbing an umbrella and running downstairs.

He rushed to his friend, greeted him with Salam, and asked:

“Raees, why are you standing here in this rain? If you want to cross to the other side, why don’t you just go? There are very few vehicles on the road because of the rain.”

Raees returned the Salam, pointed to the red signal light, and said:

“It is this light that is holding me back, even in the rain, my friend. When this red light is on, one must not cross the road. There is a green light below it; only when that lights up should one cross.”

Suhail asked in surprise:

“Is it necessary to stand here like this, ignoring the rain? If you don’t see any vehicles coming, why not just run across?”

“No, that’s not right! Whether it is raining or sunny, one should not do that. Traffic signals are rules for safe travel. A Muslim should be one who follows the rules. Everyone benefits from that. If we ignore them and cross the road, a vehicle might come speeding by at that very moment. It could even cost us our lives. We must remember that our carelessness can cause trouble for us, our families, the driver of the vehicle, and the owner.”

By then, the rain had stopped. The green light for pedestrians turned on. Raees said goodbye and prepared to cross the road.

“My friend, you have taught me a valuable lesson. I will never forget this,” Suhail said, before walking back to where his mother was waiting for him.
