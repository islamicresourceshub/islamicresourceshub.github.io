The Currency That Was Not Accepted

കുഞ്ഞിമുഹമ്മദ്‌ മദനി പറപ്പൂർ · 2017-01-14 · Stories & Literature

We Learned the Value of Money

When the wealth earned through a lifetime of hard work was declared invalid currency, people were left in total bewilderment. There were long queues in front of banks. When we had to settle for meager amounts after standing in line for hours, ignoring the heat, cold, illness, and exhaustion, we truly learned the value of money. The panic from that experience has not yet fully faded. This is our lived reality.

The State of Human Life

This is exactly the state of human life, especially for those who profess faith. We are working hard, both individually and collectively, in the name of faith, principles, and adherence to religious texts. If asked why we engage in all these religious activities, everyone’s answer is that they are earning something for the Hereafter. Therefore, these actions we save for the afterlife must be amal—deeds—that are actually beneficial there. In Arabic, the coins used to make life in this world comfortable are called umla. We panicked when those became invalid notes. Yet, there was some relief in knowing there were systems in place to deposit them into banks and convert them into valid, usable currency. But what will we do if the deeds we perform to take from this world to the next are not accepted there? Therefore, we must have a firm resolve that the deeds we perform are those that Allah will accept.

A Time of Emotional Intensity

We live in an era of a certain emotional intensity. In this state of emotion, people forget justice, fairness, right, and wrong. Take, for example, the celebration of the Prophet’s birthday. If someone advises that it is an innovation and that the money, effort, and environmental pollution spent on this practice will not be of any use in the Hereafter because it contradicts established religious proofs, some brothers respond with stubbornness, adopting even more dangerous practices just to say, "Look at this." Many prefer to approach matters emotionally rather than with wisdom. We must remember that arrogance, hatred, and immaturity destroy all goodness.

Let the Thought of Goodness Guide Us

We should be guided by the thought of whether every word, action, and association of ours is a good deed that will be useful for tomorrow. If we forget our own salvation and the pleasure of Allah in our eagerness to defeat others or in our rush to achieve unjust gains, we must fear that the deeds and efforts of this life will turn into invalid currency in the Hereafter.

Honesty and Sincerity

The Prophet (peace be upon him) used to pray, "O Allah, grant me the fortune to do more good for as long as You extend my life."

That should be our thought and our pursuit. Remember that even if one manages to hold on for a while through cunning and trickery, only honesty and sincerity will be of any use for the day that is to come.
