Obey Your Parents

Usman Palakkazhi

"My son, Faisal... please change out of your uniform, have something to eat, and go to the shop for me. I need to buy a few things," his mother said as soon as Faisal returned from school.

He felt a surge of anger. He had promised his friends he would join them for a game of cricket as soon as he finished eating. His team had lost to Salim’s team yesterday, and he was determined to beat them today. Just as he was planning his victory, his mother asked him to run an errand. Doesn't this mother of mine have anything else to do?

"I can't go to any shop or anywhere else. I am going to play!" Faisal shouted back angrily.

"The game can wait. You must go to the shop now," his mother insisted.

"It is not convenient for me right now. If I have time after the game, I might go," he said firmly.

"At least eat something first," his loving mother said, trying to appease him, fearing that if she argued further, her son would skip his meal.

Faisal gulped down his food and ran out to play. Ignoring his mother’s call of "My son," he shouted to his friends and dashed ahead.

About half an hour into the game, it happened. While running after the ball, Faisal tripped and fell. He rolled on the ground, unable to get up. His friends rushed over to help him, but he could not put weight on his legs. He cried out in agony. His leg bone had broken somewhere. The game stopped, and everyone gathered around him.

"Take me to the hospital quickly... my leg is broken... oh, Mother!" he wailed in intense pain.

Just then, Najeeb Maulavi was passing by. Hearing the cries, he approached the children. Realizing the severity of Faisal’s injury, he called for an auto-rickshaw and took him straight to the hospital. After admitting him, he informed Faisal’s home. His father was working in the Gulf, and his elder brother would not be back from college until evening. Upon hearing the news, his mother rushed to the hospital.

"Oh, my dear son, what happened to you?" she cried as she reached his side.

"There is nothing to worry about. We suspect a small fracture in the leg bone. The doctor has ordered an X-ray," Najeeb Maulavi reassured her.

His mother sat beside Faisal, stroking his hair while tears streamed down her face.

"My son... you ran off without listening to your mother, didn't you?" she sobbed.

That same night, Faisal’s leg was put in a cast. He was discharged the next day, but he was bedridden and unable to walk. He would have to stay like this for a month and a half. The thought brought tears to his eyes.

Najeeb Maulavi came to visit Faisal. After sitting by his side and praying for his recovery, he asked:

"I heard your mother ask you at the hospital if you had run off without listening to her. Is that true?"

"It is true. When she asked me to go to the shop, I told her it wasn't convenient and ran off."

"Even after you showed such disobedience, your mother did not get angry or resent you. When she heard you were hurt, she rushed to the hospital, crying out for her dear son. Do you realize now that it was wrong to disobey a mother who loves you so dearly? Haven't you learned in Madrasa that if you upset your parents or disobey them, you will face the displeasure of Allah?"

Faisal squeezed his eyes shut. Tears were flowing from them.

Even though he had been disobedient and angry, his mother held no grudge. She loved him so much, yet he was not returning that love. These thoughts filled him with sorrow.

Najeeb Maulavi said:

"You must understand the gravity of your duty toward your parents. Have you not heard the verses regarding this in Surah Al-Isra? Allah says:

وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوا إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا ۚ إِمَّا يَبْلُغَنَّ عِندَكَ الْكِبَرَ أَحَدُهُمَا أَوْ كِلَاهُمَا فَلَا تَقُل لَّهُمَا أُفٍّ وَلَا تَنْهَرْهُمَا وَقُل لَّهُمَا قَوْلًا كَرِيمًا وَاخْفِضْ لَهُمَا جَنَاحَ الذُّلِّ مِنَ الرَّحْمَةِ وَقُل رَّبِّ ارْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرًا

"I have studied this verse in Madrasa, but I never remember it," Faisal said, wiping his tears.

"There are also Hadiths that specifically mention the duty toward one's mother. Once, a man came to the Prophet and asked: 'O Messenger of Allah, who is most deserving of my good companionship?' He replied: 'Your mother.' 'Then who?' 'Your mother.' When asked a third time, he said: 'Your mother.' When asked a fourth time, he said: 'Your father.' From this, you can understand the depth of the duty toward a mother."

"I have realized my mistakes. From now on, I will never disobey my mother. I will not speak loudly to her. I will not get angry," Faisal said with a firm resolve.

"A very good decision. May the Almighty bless you. I am leaving now. Assalamu Alaikum," Najeeb Maulavi said as he departed.
