When the Burden of Sin Increases...

A Morning at the Madrasa

"Me first, me first!" Saleem shouted as he sprinted onto the Madrasa veranda. Although Safiyya, Sadiq, and Salman were running right behind him, Saleem was the first to make it inside.

"There are still five minutes until the bell rings," Sadiq said, checking the new watch his father had brought him the day before.

"Oh, I am sure we will get many more warnings about the time today," Safiyya said with a laugh.

These four neighbors were all students in the fourth grade at the Madrasa. They were all excellent students who loved to play, and they always traveled to and from the Madrasa together.

Sameena, Naushad, and Abdu all rushed into the classroom. The first bell rang. The first period was Holy Quran recitation. Yunus Mash arrived just as the bell finished ringing.

"Assalamu Alaikum."

"Wa Alaikumussalam wa Rahmatullah," everyone replied in unison.

The second bell rang. All the students stood up, recited the prayer, and sat down. Yunus Mash took out the Holy Quran, and the students opened theirs.

"Sir, you said we would study Surah Al-Fath today."

"Yes, today we are going to study Surah Al-Fath. What is the meaning of the word Fath?" the teacher asked.

"Victory," Salman shouted out.

Yunus Mash began to recite Surah Al-Fath according to the rules of Tajweed. The students loved his melodious recitation. Listening to him, the students began to recite along with him.

By the end of the class, every student had learned to recite it well.

Broken Promises and Lessons

'Rring...!'

It was the signal for the second period. All the students closed their Qurans. The teacher said his salutations and stepped out.

"Did you write the notes for the moral studies lesson?" Sameena asked Safiyya.

"Yes, I finished writing the notes yesterday."

"What is wrong with you?" Safiyya asked Nadira, who was sitting with a sullen face.

"I had borrowed her book to copy the notes from the previous lessons. I promised to return the book to her house yesterday. I completely forgot," Sameena said.

"Haven't you learned that you should not break a promise once you have made it? Because of that, she couldn't write her notes either," Safa said.

The Burden of Sin and Life

In the meantime, Yusuf Mash entered the classroom with a greeting. He was holding a glass of water!

"Why do you have that water?" Saleem was eager to know the reason.

"Everyone, settle down..."

Everyone sat in silence, filled with curiosity.

"How many milligrams of water do you think are in this glass?" the teacher asked.

  • 50
  • 100
  • 75

All the students began shouting out the amounts they guessed.

"Alright, alright... to know exactly, one would have to measure it. But I have a different question."

The students' curiosity grew.

"If I hold this glass of water out like this for a few minutes, what will happen to my arm?"

"Nothing will happen," all the students said in unison.

"Okay. What if I hold it out like this for an hour?"

"Your arm will ache, it will start to cramp..." Salman said.

"Yes, my arm will ache. But what if I hold it like this for an entire day?"

"Your arm will go numb, sir. Then you would have to go to the hospital to get it fixed," Sadiq shouted out. Hearing that, all the students laughed.

"Yes, Sadiq is right; you would have to take me to the hospital. Would it be enough to just reduce the amount of water in the glass to prevent that?"

"That wouldn't do anything. You could just put the glass down and avoid the problem altogether," Safiyya said with a hint of humor.

"Yes, that is correct. Safiyya is right. Children, the small problems in our lives are just like this. We walk around thinking about them excessively. In the end, it creates great difficulties."

The students were listening intently.

"There is another matter that causes even greater loss. Do you know what that is?" the teacher asked.

Saleem jumped up and said: "Our small sins!"

"Yes, well done! Can you explain that?"

"If we go around without asking Allah to forgive the sins we have committed and keep repeating those sins, the difficulties caused by them will increase day by day."

"Yes, if the weight of the glass in the outstretched arm increases and the time spent holding it grows, it will be very difficult for the arm to return to its original state. Even if sins are minor, if they accumulate, we become great sinners. We become people who do not hesitate to commit any wrong. However, for the one who sincerely presents the sorrows and difficulties of their heart—and the sins they have committed knowingly or unknowingly—before the Most Merciful Allah, expresses regret, asks for forgiveness, and stays as far away from those sins as possible, they will receive a profound sense of peace. Their burden of sin will not increase."

"Sir, when you explained the meaning of the 4th verse of Surah Al-Fath that we studied earlier, you told us that 'It is Allah who sent down tranquility into the hearts of the believers'," Saleem said.

"Yes, very good! It is Allah who provides peace. Those who have attained victory will be in ultimate tranquility and peace."

"Sir, isn't it about them that Allah spoke of the 'tranquil soul' in Surah Al-Fajr?" Sameena asked.

"Ya ayyatu-han-nafsu-l-mutma'inna..."

Upon hearing that, the teacher's heart was filled with joy.
