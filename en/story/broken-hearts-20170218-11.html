Hearts with Holes

അറബി കഥ - പുനരാഖ്യാനം: തൻസീൽ · 2017-02-18 · Stories & Literature

Once upon a time, in a village, there lived a very mischievous boy. He caused his parents unbearable distress. He became a nuisance to his neighbors and peers as well. Despite his parents constantly advising him, it had no effect.

He was not only unwilling to obey anyone who said something against his wishes, but he would also speak harshly to them. If anyone younger than him approached, he would threaten and hurt them.

The Loneliness of the Mischievous Boy

Everyone started calling him the mischievous boy. He would sometimes feel that his school friends and neighbors were distancing themselves from him. At those times, he would feel like becoming a better person. But no matter how hard he tried, he could not control his anger. This worried his parents more than anyone else. Although many people advised him in many ways at different times, he did not change. Consequently, he became more isolated. His friends hesitated to befriend him or play with him.

One day, he was sitting alone by the river near his house, staring at the ripples. Just then, his father came and sat beside him, gently stroking his hair and starting to tell him jokes. He talked about the mischief the boy had done when he was a little child. Hearing his father's affectionate interaction and learning about the hardships his parents had endured for his sake, the boy thought, "No one treats me like this anymore." He guessed it must be because of his bad behavior.

"I must change. From now on, I will not treat anyone badly; no matter how angry I get," he decided. He and his father walked back home from the riverbank. When he reached inside, a cat ran from the kitchen and hissed at him. Angry at the cat for hissing, he threw a cricket ball that was lying on the floor at it. Seeing this, his mother said to him:

“No matter how much we tell you, why are you like this, my son? Why are you throwing things at it?”

When he heard that, his anger flared up. But his father held his hand. With that, his anger subsided.

A Way to Control Anger

The next day, when his father returned from work, he brought a plastic bag full of nails. There was also a small hammer in the bag. His father told him:

“From today, whenever you get angry, go behind the house and take a nail from this bag and hammer it into the fence. Hammer it into the fence with all your might until your anger subsides. Do this every time you feel angry.”

From that day on, he started doing exactly that. - At first, he hammered many nails into the fence with great force every single day. - Later, it became occasional; the number of nails and the force with which he hammered them decreased. - As days passed, the intervals between his outbursts grew longer, and the number of nails he hammered continued to drop. - Eventually, he reached a state where even if he got angry, he would calm down quickly.

One evening, his father took him to the fence behind the house. The fence was full of nails! In the beginning, the nails had sunk deep into the fence. Those were the marks of intense anger. Later on, the nails had not sunk in as deeply. The number of nails had also decreased significantly.

His father told him to pull out the nails one by one over the coming days. From that day on, he started pulling out the nails just as his father had asked. In the final days, he was able to pull out the nails he had hammered very quickly. However, he could not even budge the nails he had hammered in the early days.

Learning the Lesson

The next day, he informed his father that he had pulled out the nails. His father took him to the fence. He saw that the nails from the first few days could not be removed, and where the later ones had been pulled out, there were holes left behind. His father said to him with great love and affection:

“My son, consider this fence as the hearts of those you were angry with before. Consider all the nails hammered into it as the pain caused to their hearts by your words. Even if the nails hammered into those hearts are pulled out, the marks will only fade with time. Yet, some nails will remain in those hearts in a way that they can never be removed.”

At that moment, his eyes filled with tears from the weight of his sorrow.

The Splendor of Allah

Abu Faida

February 18, 2017 | 23 Jumada al-Awwal 1438

Look at the parched, dry earth, my children, Not a drop of water, not a single blade of grass, Only the hardened soil remains. Not a drop of water, not a single blade of grass, Only the hardened soil remains.

When the doors of the sky open and rain falls upon that earth, Life stirs, and thousands of plant species sprout forth, Life stirs, and thousands of plant species sprout forth.

It wears a cloak of green, with flowers and fruits, A cooling rain for the eyes, What a wonder, how beautiful, Everything is the splendor of Allah, Everything is the splendor of Allah.

Think about it, my friends, if we were to leave this world, Would the One Allah not be able to create us again just like this? Would the One Allah not be able to create us again just like this?
