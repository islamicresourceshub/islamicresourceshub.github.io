Diet in Ramadan

ഡോ. മുഹമ്മദ് കുട്ടി കണ്ണിയന്‍ · 2018-05-19 · Health & Medicine

This article outlines some essential points that both patients and healthy individuals should keep in mind regarding dietary habits during the fasting period.

When breaking the fast

Dishes made from rice flour or wheat flour—such as noolappam, pathiri, appam, puttu, and chapati—are rich in carbohydrates and digest quickly, providing an immediate source of energy.

Avoid deep-fried snacks and spicy dishes as much as possible. These can lead to acid reflux, gas, vomiting, stomach pain, belching, and abdominal bloating. It is also best to avoid parotta and khubz made from refined flour.

Water is essential

Water is vital for all bodily functions and significantly influences blood pressure and circulation. Therefore, make sure to drink plenty of water. Since the beginning of the fast may fall during warmer weather, the risk of dehydration during the day is higher; pay special attention to drinking enough water at night to prevent urinary tract irritation.

Include fruits and vegetables rich in minerals and water content in your diet, such as watermelon, papaya, pineapple, cucumber, sapodilla, plantains, small bananas, dates, and mangoes. Diabetic patients should avoid fruits with high sugar content that cause rapid spikes in blood glucose levels.

Gas and constipation

Gas issues often worsen when spicy, acidic, or oily foods are consumed on an empty stomach. Constipation is also common due to a lack of fiber-rich foods and vegetables in the diet.

Eat in small, frequent portions

Eating a heavy meal immediately upon breaking the fast can cause bloating, gas, and stomach pain. Furthermore, it can leave you feeling lethargic, diminishing the spiritual vitality of your night prayers.

Break your fast with dates, followed by water or juice. You may also have some fruit. After the Maghrib prayer, eat a light portion of rice-based food. If you wish, you may have a little more fruit before heading out for the Isha prayer. After completing Isha and Taraweeh, having a light porridge with vegetable curry is a healthy choice.

Aim for a balanced diet

Pairing rice-based dishes with meat, fish, or chickpeas ensures you receive the necessary protein. Including vegetable salads and stir-fries provides fiber, which helps prevent constipation.

Suhoor

Do not limit your Suhoor to rice dishes alone. Try to include protein. Consuming milk and fruits is excellent, especially for children, as it provides protein, hydration, and carbohydrates. Aval milk is a highly recommended option. Drink plenty of water. Avoid spicy and acidic foods as much as possible, and strive for a balanced meal that includes fruits.

  • Drink plenty of water.
  • Instead of eating a large amount at once, eat smaller portions at intervals.
  • Instead of deep-frying, opt for steaming or grilling.
  • Use black pepper, ginger, and garlic instead of red chili powder to aid digestion and reduce gas.
  • Never skip Suhoor.
  • Avoid eating out at restaurants during Ramadan.
  • If preparing biryani or ghee rice, add plenty of vegetables.
  • Always accompany your meals with a generous portion of vegetable salad.
