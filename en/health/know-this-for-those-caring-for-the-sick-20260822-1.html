Understand Them: Caregivers of the Sick

Dr. Fusan Noorazanna

When someone in a home is bedridden for a long period, the primary responsibility for their care often falls to one family member. However, that individual may not have a clear understanding of what they are enduring, or even how to care for themselves amidst their duties.

Moreover, there is the question: what would be the state of a family if the person caring for someone in the home were to fall ill themselves? Therefore, they need to be aware.

They must be aware of the changes occurring in their own body and mind, in addition to providing self-care. This too is a very important matter.

However, the significant responsibilities of a family member and the health of the caregiver themselves are also a cause for concern. During the patient's treatment period, special attention must be paid to ensuring their care, as well as maintaining the caregiver's own physical and mental health.

They too should be allowed adequate rest and permitted to step away from the patient's care occasionally. Support for this must be ensured from others.

For Others Too

The patient who doesn't even feel like eating on time. The patient who waits for hours in hospital corridors. The patient who lives dependent on others for their own needs.

But they could be a wife caring for her ailing husband. A husband standing strong for his wife battling cancer. Children or in-laws supporting elderly parents who are bedridden, having lost the ability to do things for themselves due to old age. Parents of children with conditions requiring special care, such as autism or cerebral palsy.

Whatever the illness, whatever the relationship, it is love that carries them forward in this worldly life.

But this worldly life is not always easy. Sleep can be lost. The mind can be filled with constant worry. One may have to neglect their own health and needs.

Perhaps one may even have to quit their job.

The person affected by the illness may always feel isolated.

There can always be a feeling of isolation. Helplessness can also be experienced.

Often, the patient they care for may be in a state where they cannot even recognize their love and sacrifice, or express gratitude for it. Yet, the joy they receive when they see some relief or progress in the patient is indescribable.

If one lamp gives light to another, it must also have a wick. Similarly, the person caring for another also needs rest occasionally. If someone inquires about them, or says a sincere word like, 'It's because of your immense care that we see this positive change. But amidst all this, please also take care of your own health, okay...', it would be a great comfort to them. Because those who care for others also wish to know that someone cares for them.

Therefore, when visiting the sick, not only the patient,

but let us also pay attention to the caregiver. To help a caregiver, one does not need great financial means.

If one can help with the patient's care for an hour, or provide rest, or prepare and deliver a meal, or accompany them to the hospital or workplace, or if none of these, then patiently listen to their worries, that too is a great help. Sometimes, small acts of care become the greatest strength.

Islam teaches us to help one another and show compassion.

The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: «اللَّهُ فِي عَوْنِ الْعَبْدِ مَا كَانَ الْعَبْدُ فِي عَوْنِ أَخِيهِ» (صحيح مسلم).

Let us learn to see not only the patient, but also the caregivers who, in their helplessness, render service of love. Let us strive to lighten their burden and be a support for them.
