Nutrients for Thyroid Health

ഡോ. മുഹമ്മദ് കുട്ടി കണ്ണിയന്‍ · 2018-05-05 · Health & Medicine

Just like any other bodily function, diet plays a significant role in both supporting and hindering the performance of the thyroid gland. The following are the key nutrients that are helpful for thyroid health.

Iodine

In humans, the thyroid gland is the only organ that absorbs iodine. The thyroid gland utilizes two-thirds of the total iodine in our body. Since both the deficiency and excessive intake of iodine can affect the gland's function, the levels of this element must be carefully regulated.

The primary sources of iodine are seafood, leafy greens, vegetables, and grains. Although seawater is a major source, the salt produced by evaporating it is not a very reliable source. Iodized salt ensures the correct amount of iodine in the diet.

Selenium

Most of the selenium in the body is found in the thyroid gland. This element is essential for the production and regulation of the T3 hormone. Furthermore, it is necessary to maintain the levels of T3 hormone in other organs and the blood. Selenium is a key component of enzymes required for thyroid gland function, just as it is a component of major antioxidant enzymes. As an antioxidant, it has the ability to prevent cancer and enhance immune function, cognitive ability, and reproductive health. Selenium is found in seafood such as crab and tuna, as well as in mushrooms and liver.

Iron, Zinc, and Copper

These are three other essential trace elements required for thyroid health. It has been observed that a deficiency in zinc lowers TSH levels, iron deficiency reduces the functional capacity of the thyroid gland, and copper affects thyroid hormone production.

Foods such as liver, leafy greens, mushrooms, nuts, beans, and seafood should be included in the diet in appropriate amounts.

Vitamins

While all vitamins are necessary for thyroid health, Vitamin D, Vitamin B12, and antioxidant vitamins like beta-carotene, C, and E are particularly important.

Vitamin D: A deficiency in Vitamin D contributes to the acceleration of bone loss that occurs in hyperthyroidism. In addition to fatty fish, milk, eggs, and mushrooms, it can also be obtained through sunlight. Antioxidant vitamins such as C, E, and beta-carotene are also seen to play a major role in thyroid health. Leafy greens, colorful vegetables, fruits, and vegetable oils are excellent sources of these.

Omega-3 Fatty Acids

Among fats, these are the most essential for thyroid health. They are abundantly found in fish, fish oil, flaxseeds, and coconut oil.
