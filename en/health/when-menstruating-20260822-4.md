# When Menstruation Begins

**Dr. Rafeeda K.**

> وَقَدْ خَلَقَكُمْ أَطْوَارًا
> He has created you in stages (Quran 71:14).

Human life is a beautiful journey through various stages. Every transition—from infancy to childhood, from childhood to adolescence, and eventually to adulthood—is part of the divine order established by Allah. The first menstruation, known as menarche, is one of the primary markers of a girl's entry into adolescence. It is not the beginning of fear in her life; rather, it is an entry into a new phase of knowledge, self-confidence, and a sense of responsibility. Menstruation is neither a disease nor something to be ashamed or afraid of. It is a natural biological process that Allah has placed within the female body.

When menstruation begins for the first time, some girls may experience fear and confusion. Many questions arise in her mind: What has happened? What should I do now? Do I need to see a doctor? How should I maintain hygiene? What should I be mindful of regarding my diet? Therefore, it is essential to provide the child with scientific knowledge and practical wisdom about what to do beforehand.

With the onset of menstruation, children must also be taught the religious matters associated with it. They need to learn the correct Islamic rulings regarding the performance of prayer, fasting, and the ritual bath known as ghusl.

## What is Menstruation?

When a girl is born, all her reproductive organs, such as the uterus and ovaries, are already present in her body. When menstruation begins, it does not mean new organs have formed; rather, the existing reproductive organs are becoming functional under the control of hormones.

Typically, the menstrual cycle is 28 days. However, it does not have to be exactly 28 days every time. A cycle ranging from 21 to 35 days is considered perfectly normal and healthy.

Each month, an egg matures in the ovary and is released, usually about 14 days before the next period. This is called ovulation. Simultaneously, a soft lining forms inside the uterus to prepare for a potential pregnancy. When pregnancy does not occur, this lining is shed along with blood and leaves the body. This is what we call menstruation or periods.

## Personal Hygiene: What Parents Must Teach

Teaching a girl proper personal hygiene habits during her first period is a primary responsibility of parents. Matters should be explained in an age-appropriate manner without causing fear or embarrassment.

**Teach how to use a sanitary pad:**

1) Tell the child how to use a sanitary pad and that it should be changed frequently depending on the flow. To avoid infections and discomfort, encourage the habit of changing the pad every four to six hours, even if the flow is light.
2) Hands should be washed with soap both before and after changing the pad.
3) Dispose of used pads safely and in an environmentally friendly manner; do not flush them down the toilet.
4) Keep the private areas clean and bathe daily.
5) Wear clean, comfortable undergarments and change them when necessary.
6) There is no need to use strong perfumes or harsh cleaning products on private areas.

In addition to pads, the menstrual cup has become an increasingly accepted alternative. Those who use a cup must strictly follow instructions regarding its correct size, usage, cleaning, and sterilization. It is advisable for girls experiencing their first period to choose the method that is most suitable, safe, and comfortable for them.

## Diet and Nutrition

Since blood loss occurs during menstruation, a nutrient-rich diet is very important. Foods containing iron, folic acid, calcium, magnesium, and protein help reduce fatigue and provide energy to the body.

It is beneficial to include spinach, drumstick leaves, pulses, pomegranate, raisins, dates, eggs, fish, milk, yogurt, and various fruits in the diet.

Foods rich in Vitamin C, such as oranges, lemons, and gooseberries, help increase iron absorption. Drink plenty of water to keep the body hydrated. Warm water, coconut water, and lemon juice are also beneficial. Warm water with ginger or other healthy beverages can help reduce abdominal pain and fatigue.

It is best to minimize fried foods, junk food, sweets, and carbonated drinks. Tea and coffee, which are high in caffeine, may increase discomfort and abdominal pain in some, though this does not affect everyone the same way.
